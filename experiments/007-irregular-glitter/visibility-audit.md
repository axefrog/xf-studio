# Browser Glitter visibility audit — 24 September 2026

The user-chosen 500,000 setting was a count of **global generated candidate IDs**, not a count of particles in the painted shape or of visible glints. The study scripts in this directory measure the deterministic field and a bounded contrast proxy from the two locally held user reference photos. The originals stay ignored and undistributed.

The measured eye shape is the initial XF Studio `Petal wash` layer with the opt-in candidate. Its fixed shape and seed make these figures reproducible; another edited shape has different counts. `measure-studio-visibility.ts` uses the same two fixed eye UV regions and the production 16-sample covered-only bake. A centre is counted inside the shape when the procedural mask covers its UV position. The live worker's faster nearest-texel approximation reports 5,447/5,441 centres at 1K/2K for the same 500k case; the exact centre query below gives 5,549. Neither number describes screen pixels.

Both settings paint 11,496 mask pixels at 1K or 45,954 at 2K. At 500k/.00025, the median nominal flake diameter is 0.39 atlas pixels at 1K and 0.77 at 2K; the 95th percentile is 0.81/1.61. At 350k/.00045 the respective medians are 0.70/1.40 and 95th percentiles 1.45/2.90. These ratios explain why higher global count plus smaller flakes **reduced** resolvable area and strong glints. Catalogue construction took roughly 152 ms for 350k and 194 ms for 500k in one local Bun run; this excludes tile bake and browser rendering.

At a fixed close-eye camera in the isolated `?verify=1` browser, I compared 500k/.00025 at the same base colour against a zero-flake render. In two eye rectangles, 1K/2K produced 746/1,079 screen pixels with luminance difference ≥ 20/255, but only 69/245 pixels at ≥ 30. Eight-connected components at the ≥ 30 threshold numbered 36/111. These are high-contrast *image components*, not identifiable physical flakes; JPEG capture, interpolation, eyelashes, and the chosen framing affect the figures. Control forehead/cheek rectangles showed zero differences at the same thresholds. The full-face 2K image still reads as pale speckles on pink instead of the supplied fine clustered glints over coloured sheen.

For a rough photographic scale comparison, `measure-photo-speckles.py` subtracts a 7px median background from manual eyelid crops and counts small highpass components. Reference 6's 400×135 crop has 758 components of ≤ 30px area at a +30/255 threshold, median area 3px; reference 7's 420×165 crop has 327, median area 5px. These counts include bright skin/eyelash/texture signals and cannot be equated to cosmetic particles or to the screen difference test. Their key value is qualitative: the photos show many independently bright fine features, while the current Studio texture collapses its far more numerous generated IDs into weak averaged coverage.

The bounded path forward is a **new opt-in versioned browser model** drawing deterministic, UV-anchored polygon glints under the actual preview lights, with a continuous base sheen and nonuniform fine/occasional large facets. The independently authored `glitter-glint-study.ts` already explores a 960-cell field, 3×3 local search, size populations and minification fade; its whole-face appearance is directionally closer to reference 6. It is a shader experiment, not recipe-7 semantics or game material proof. Before integration, it needs a frame-time/whole-head budget, stable seed and control mapping, close/normal/distant and moving-light inspection, worker-independent material disposal, and a new serialized model identifier. The raster candidate must remain available for existing recipe-7 files. Game compiler Glitter rejection remains in force.
