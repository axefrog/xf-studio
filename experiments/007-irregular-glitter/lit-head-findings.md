# Fine glitter on the lit studio head — 23 September 2026

The 160k and 350k region-limited fields replace the conspicuous regular dots with much smaller irregular fragments. The 350k study keeps 15,760 fragments near the two painted eye regions; the rest of the nominal global field is not generated. The fixed Backdrop mask is entirely inside those regions at 1K and 2K. These outputs are not valid full-atlas materials.

The [fine-region measurements](../../projects/xf-studio/authoring/tools/glitter-fine-metrics.py) show that the 350k field covers 10.76%/10.79% of the valid painted area at 1K/2K. The median fragment diameter is 0.69 pixels at 1K and 1.38 pixels at 2K. Most painted pixels contain a mixture of fragment and base pigment, so averaging normals over all samples discards much of their angular variation. Sixteen coverage samples improve edge integration but do not solve reflection filtering.

We compared `fine350k` and `fine350k-covered` on the same local head, Backdrop, blink 0.7, close-eye camera, light 25°, room intensity 0.2, separate pigment/flake reflection mode and flake roughness 0.05. The latter averages only covered sample normals and uses a neutral normal for empty pixels. Their 1K/2K surface and colour files are byte-identical; only the normal maps differ. At 2K both show more isolated bright highlights than the stock material, and the covered variant shifts their locations/intensity. At 1K flakes lose much fine detail. Both still look too much like pale flecks on purple paint compared with Nathan's supplied photographs. This is a qualitative browser observation, not a numerical photographic match or game parity claim. [Inputs, hashes and runtime checks](lit-head-evidence.json).

The isolated Two-BRDF shader evaluates pigment and flakes separately through the installed Three.js physical lighting chunks, then mixes the lit radiance by flake coverage. This avoids treating the whole makeup sheet as one partially metallic surface, but a single filtered normal per texel cannot preserve the distribution of subpixel facets. A 90-frame browser sample at 2K measured 16.7 ms median and 16.8 ms p95 both with the two-reflection comparison enabled and with the original material path; that sample establishes no visible frame-cadence regression on this machine, not a GPU budget guarantee. The tab reported no console errors and 20 textures after switches.

**Decision:** retain the editor's current glitter while investigating a view/light-dependent glint model with coherent filtering at distance. Preserve fixed spatial flake IDs; never use frame-random sparkle. Test an angular distribution or stochastic microfacet representation before freezing a recipe/export contract. The browser study is necessary visual evidence, but REDengine material feasibility remains a separate task.
