# Rendering every head character-creator option

**Maturity: Draft.** Consolidated from the vanilla 2.31 resources (female first, male where cheap), the generic resolver's output for four synthetic creator states and the reference save, one decompiled skin shader, the installed complexion mods of one MO2 profile, and an audit of the Studio preview on 25 September 2026, updated when steps P0 (brows, lashes and hair) and P1 (the head's skin) landed the same day and when rank 2 (the face decals) and the piercings landed on 26 September; all of them render from the resolver's output (§6). No claim here has runtime evidence. Evidence grades follow the [knowledge rules](README.md): **[source]**, **[resource]**, **[wiki]**, **[runtime]**, **[hypothesis]**; **[community]** marks values taken from a third-party tool. Provenance, hashes and the resolver runs are in the [head CC render evidence](../research/character-customization/head-cc-render-evidence.md).

This page answers, for each head option: which resources it resolves to, how the creator's choice selects them, which material template draws them and with which key parameters, whether the Studio preview draws it today, and what a save stores. It ends with a ranked render plan tied to the [feature-module platform](../research/authoring/feature-module-platform.md#5-rendering-ui-02-ui-11). The option catalogue itself (counts, links, groups, switchers) is in the [CC file chain](cc-file-chain.md#4-every-cc-detail-and-what-drives-it); shader internals are in [materials and shaders](materials-and-shaders.md) and [hair shading](hair-shading.md).

## 1. Every head option at a glance

"Selector" is how the chosen index reaches resources: **S** switcher (activates other options), **A** appearance option (definition name = `.app` appearance), **M** morph option, **mA** mesh appearance, **mask** chunk mask. Paths abbreviate `base\characters\head\player_base_heads\` as `…\`. "Preview" is the state of `projects/xf-studio/authoring/src` on 26 September 2026. "Save" names the entry the save's `CharacetrCustomization_Appearances` node carries: `(option, app hash, definition)` in the listed groups, or `(region, target)` for morphs ([save format](cc-file-chain.md#7-how-a-save-stores-cc-choices)).

## 2. Skin: type, tone and the complexion texture set

**Type and tone are separate axes** [resource]. The head mesh holds 60 local materials, one per (tone, type). Each overrides only `Albedo` (the type's `d0N`) and `Normal`; everything else, including the tone, is inherited through the four-level tone chain. `default_female_head_<tone>.mi` holds the shared maps, `default_head_<tone>.mi` the microdetail scales 20/8 and the tint mask, and `<tone>.mi` the tint and detail values. So "skin type" swaps one albedo texture, and "skin tone" changes three numbers:

| Tone | `TintColor` | `TintScale` | | Tone | `TintColor` | `TintScale` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `01_ca_pale` | 171,155,150 | 0 | | `03_ca_senna_01_honey` | 181,141,29 | 0.67 |
| `01_ca_pale_00_warm_ivory` | 255,245,181 | −0.15 | | `03_ca_senna_02_band` | 17,62,11 | 0.57 |
| `02_ca_limestone` | 131,149,83 | 0.38 | | `04_ca_almond` | 189,180,173 | 1.0 |
| `02_ca_limestone_00_beige` | 172,166,31 | 0.58 | | `04_ca_almond_00_umber` | 160,113,70 | 0.716 |
| `03_ca_senna` | 202,177,153 | 0.70 | | `05_bl_espresso` | 117,98,96 | 0.80 |
| `03_ca_senna_00_amber` | 199,116,112 | 0.52 | | `06_bl_dark` | 63,51,39 | 0.845 |

**What the shader does with them** [source: skin G-buffer program `12806642364631437234`, decompiled; see the [evidence](../research/character-customization/head-cc-render-evidence.md#observations-recorded-here-in-detail)]:

| Step | Arithmetic |
|---|---|
| Tint weight | `w = abs(TintScale) · TintColorMask.R` |
| Tint | `TintScale ≥ 0`: multiply, `saturate(TintColor · albedo)`; `TintScale < 0`: **overlay** blend with `TintColor` (so warm ivory brightens rather than darkens) |
| Base colour | `lerp(albedo + cavity, tint, w)`; cavity darkening comes from `Roughness.B` × `CavityIntensity` |
| Blood flow | `Bloodflow`-masked blend toward `BloodColor`, driven by two per-vertex values cubed (an animation-driven flush) [hypothesis for the driver] |
| Secondary albedo | Composited over the result by `SecondaryAlbedoInfluence · SecondaryAlbedo.A`, tinted by `w · SecondaryAlbedoTintColorInfluence` (freckle and tattoo frameworks use this) |
| Microdetail | `MicroDetail` is a 2:1 atlas of two RG normal tiles; `TintColorMask.B` (5 steps) raises both UV frequencies, `TintColorMask.G` (6 steps) blends between the tiles, `Roughness.B · MicroDetailInfluence` weights the result. The wiki's microdetail image shows the same two-tile atlas [wiki: `hair-and-skin-material-properties.md`, `skin_shader_microdetail_scale.png`] |
| Output | GBuffer0 = `sqrt(colour)`; roughness and the RG normal as in [materials §4.1](materials-and-shaders.md#41-basematerialsskinmt-and-skin_blendable-skin_morph) |

`TintColorMask` is therefore three masks in one: R = where the tone applies, G and B = microdetail region selectors. The tint masks are **gamma textures**: the base game's `h0_000_base__tintcolormask.xbm` and the texture framework's replacement both have `isGamma` set, so the engine's sampler sRGB-decodes them (and the G/B selector steps) before the arithmetic above [resource]. `TintColor`'s encoding into the shader (byte/255 or sRGB-decoded) is still [materials open question 11](materials-and-shaders.md#7-open-questions); the tone table above is in bytes. The two readings differ strongly for strong tones: at senna's 0.70 a mid-grey albedo keeps about 85/78/72 % per channel under byte/255, against 71/60/52 % under sRGB decoding (test ask 8).

**Where the tone does not reach** [resource]. `TintScale` 0 (pale) leaves the albedo untouched, so type and tone only look different through the albedo. With a complexion replacer that ships one fair albedo per type (the reference profile's), every tone therefore starts from fair skin; senna, for example, darkens it only by its multiply.

**Global render options also shape skin** [community]. The engine exposes runtime `GameOptions` for characters: `Editor/Characters/Skin` (`SkinAmbientIntensity_Factor` 0.4, `SkinAmbientMix_Factor` 1.0, `SubsurfaceSpecularTintWeight` 0.3 with tint 0.21/0.26/0.29), `Editor/Characters/RimEnhancement` (`GlobalCharacterFresnel` 3.0, `LightBlockerInfluence` 0.7), `Editor/Characters/Eyes` (`DiffuseBoost` 0.1), plus feature toggles for character SSS, rim enhancement and contact shadows. Values are the "Vanilla" preset of Arkhe's Character Rendering Editor; the names exist in the 2.31 executable. CET scripts can change them at runtime, and the reference profile runs such a script, so a capture must record its state. How each option enters the lighting programs is not decoded [hypothesis].

## 3. The head decal family

Everything drawn *on* the skin, brows included, is a **morph-skinned decal mesh over the head** with a `post_gbuffer` decal template [resource]. They share the head's topology region, carry all 105 face targets, and draw in the decal stage after the skin ([materials §2.4](materials-and-shaders.md#24-what-a-post_gbuffer-decal-does-to-the-pixel-under-it)).

| **Shared meshes, many looks**: the eye-makeup, lip and freckle meshes each serve every style through mesh appearances; newer cyberware designs are extra appearances on the freckle and tattoo meshes | [resource]; the head cheat sheet's warning that 2.2 cyberware appearances are missing from the meshes does not hold for the 2.31 player meshes |

**Vanilla lip finishes are mostly colour** [resource]. For style 05, the regular and glossy finishes differ only in colours and alpha, and neither writes roughness (template default surface alpha 0), so the lips keep the skin's own roughness. Only matte writes roughness (white at 0.4 coverage). The reference save's glossy style 08 also writes none. The Studio's own Glossy finish therefore already does more than vanilla glossy lipstick; this is a point of comparison, not a defect.

**How the preview draws the face decals (rank 2)** [source for the arithmetic; approximation for the forward pass]. Selection, record and renderer:

- **Selection** is structural: every head choice in a third-person face group (`TPP`, `face`, `beards`) whose option no other detail claims (brows, lashes, hair, eyes and skin keep their creator slots), drawn by the decal-family chunks of its components; the skin type's own decal parts (the personal-link port) join it. Piercings (layered), teeth (skin) and the face rig are not decals and stay out; a choice with no appearance in the installation draws nothing. CCXL makeup options qualify the same way whatever their slot: the reference save's three legacy XF selectors, whose material derives from a copied `mesh_decal` template at front priority, resolve and draw.
- **Record** (`xfs/render-detail-4`): the `face` slot, components in draw order, and per chunk the template's own name and priority beside its scalars, colours and raw textures. The served GLB holds only the drawn chunks, with sparse facial deltas (a scar mesh exports all eleven scars with dense deltas for 105 targets, 45.6 MB, for one 273-vertex chunk); see §6.
- **Material**: one family (`face-decal-material.ts`) with the decompiled `mesh_decal` and double-diffuse arithmetic: transformed UV, contrast-adjusted squared coverage with the secondary mask, three target alphas clamped at 1 (so a blush `DiffuseAlpha` above 1 saturates), roughness and metalness as R × scale + bias, the RG normal with mode 1's fade of flat texels. The gradient-recolour member uses the blendable variant's colour and linear coverage without its Fresnel term [hypothesis for the plain template]. `Color` parameters are sRGB-decoded like the brows' (materials open question 11).
- **Forward approximation**: the decal is drawn over the skin as one blended pass lit by the skin's own light (profile lobes and subsurface wrap; metal above 0.1 skips the wrap as the engine skips SSS). The skin's colour, effective roughness and metalness (its Roughness map's G) under each decal vertex (read on the drawn head) let the pass solve the "over" colour that reproduces the square-root-space blend exactly per channel, raise its alpha to the surface or normal write where those exceed the colour, and keep the skin's roughness and metalness where the decal writes none. The solve is in linear light, and both lighting presets blend in the display's scene-linear target (`src/linear-display.ts`), so it holds under both; a GPU without a renderable half-float buffer draws the Studio stage straight to the canvas, where it is approximate. Limits: the skin under a decal is known per vertex, overlapping decals each blend against the skin rather than the decal below, and a surface-only or normal-only write softens the fine skin texture by its coverage.
- **Order**: opaque skin, then the face decals by priority and then by the creator resource's option order (merged CCO, so CCXL options follow vanilla; the fallback for the unknown order), then the Studio's authored plate (the look being authored is always on top), the eye's wetness shell, brows and lashes. The decal meshes carry their own 0.4 mm lift, measured at 0.40–0.41 mm from the drawn head's vertices (the personal link 0.52 mm), so no depth offset is added.
- **Limits by code**: a decal template the family does not draw yet (the emissive, parallax and other 2.31 family members) is recorded, hidden and reported as `decal-template`; the presentation words it.

**How the preview draws the Studio's authored plate** [source for the arithmetic; approximation for the forward pass]. The export merges a preset's exportable layers into one `mesh_decal` texture: square-root colour premultiplied by coverage, in layer order, with √coverage in the alpha so the template's squared coverage is the merged coverage again (`preset-compiler.ts`). The game then blends that one decal over the skin in the G-buffer (colour in square-root space, roughness and metalness linearly at that coverage, a faceted preset's normal in mode 1), and the deferred light shades the blended pixel once, as skin ([materials §2.3–2.4](materials-and-shaders.md#23-the-deferred-lighting-model)). The preview draws exactly that decal and lights it once (`src/plate-blend.ts`):

- **Which layers.** The export's own plan (`planPresetExport`) over the drawn layers picks the layers the one decal carries and its route (flat, faceted, or Fresnel for a one-pigment Colour-shifting preset).
- **Composite** (`src/plate-composite.ts`). Small UV-space passes per change draw those layers, in order, as premultiplied "over" into an accumulation target over the plate's UV rectangle at the masks' texel density (half-float where the GPU can render it): √colour, roughness, metalness, the Shimmer facet normal, and coverage. The per-layer preview facet maps carry the mode-1 fade already, so the pass undoes it (to within their 8-bit steps) and the plate fades once. A resolve pass then writes the merged decal as the export stores it: the merged normal per texel (rounded to the export's bytes, flat where nothing covers) and its squared length, and the GPU builds that target's mips once (box means, which are the export's: coverage-weighted colour, roughness and metalness, plain means of the normal and its moment). Last, one draw per level writes the export's faceted roughness chain (`facetedMipChain`): level 0 unwidened, lower levels widened by the facet variance their averaging lost, (r⁴ + variance)^¼. Nothing runs per frame while idle; the skin is read once per head or skin change, when a layer first needs it.
- **One lit plate.** One plate mesh reads the composite and the skin under each plate vertex (colour, roughness and metalness: the face decals' underlay, read on the drawn head) and forms the blended G-buffer surface G: colour in square-root space, roughness and metalness at the coverage, the facet normal at √coverage × saturate(50 − 50z), and the decal's roughness sampled from the export's roughness chain, as the game samples the exported map (so the widening follows the mip level and never appears at level 0, where the game has none). Colour-shifting adds its Fresnel tint to the decal colour before the square root. The plate lights G with the skin's own light when a resolved skin is drawn (the profile's two lobes and the subsurface stand-in, which the blended metalness above 0.1 switches off, as the engine's SSS combine skips such pixels [source]); without a resolved skin it uses the standard light, as the face decals do. It lights the skin under the vertex, S, the same way and writes Y at alpha A with A·Y + (1 − A)·L(S) = L(G), so what shows is the blended surface's light plus the skin's own texel detail at 1 − A. A is the colour solve's alpha (the face decals' `forwardDecal`), raised only where Y would go negative.
- **Why once, not per layer.** Light is not linear in roughness or metalness. Layers lit separately and mixed afterwards show two highlights where the G-buffer holds one roughness in between, and they cannot place the SSS switch on the blended metalness. The earlier preview drew one plate per layer with the standard light.
- **Measured on a real GPU** (`tests/webgl-display.test.ts`, headless Chrome, ANGLE D3D11): Board 5's black Matte steps leave 0.562, 0.249 and 0.064 of the skin, against (1 − a)² = 0.561, 0.248 and 0.063; plum at 60 % under pale gold at 50 % lands within 0.2 % of the export's merged decal over the same skin. Under the skin's light with a key light and the room environment, the plate matches the blended surface drawn as one opaque skin-lit quad within 0.2 % for stacked Matte and Satin, Glossy, Metallic at 12 % and 30 % coverage (blended metalness 0.08 and 0.20, either side of the SSS switch) and black Matte at 50 %. For a surface as rough as the skin, the plate and the face decals' pass give the same pixel; for Glossy at 36 % coverage the face decals' per-pass light is 3–5 % darker than the lit blend, and the plate is within 0.1 % of it. The composite's faceted chain (`tests/webgl-plate-composite.test.ts`, Shimmer at 30–100 % over Glossy at 50 % on a 16-texel grid) equals `facetedMipChain` of the compiler's bytes for the same texels at levels 0 and 2 within 0.6 of a byte step for roughness and coverage and 1.1 for the normal (8-bit targets: within 2); at the texel with Shimmer 50 % over Glossy 50 % the export's roughness is 0.353 and the composite's 0.354, where the earlier composite, which kept each layer's facet moment, widened it to 0.444 at level 0.
- **In the app** (experiment 016's Board 1, default V on the reference profile, `tools/plate-light-look.ts`, private captures): under the skin light, Metallic turns from a dim tinted stripe (median luminance 0.37–0.41 of the bare skin at the same pixels) into a bright pale sheet as bright as the skin (0.89–1.12) with 2.7–4.7 times its highlight; Glossy's highlight rises from 0.5–2.3 to 1.1–4.9 times the skin's (most under the creator rig); Satin's is about the skin's (1.0–1.2); Matte stays at or below the skin's. What this predicts for the in-game Board 1 reading is under [materials §6](materials-and-shaders.md#6-makeup-finish-implications).
- **Limits.** The skin is known per plate vertex (its texel colour detail passes through at 1 − A, exact for black), and under the covered part the skin's own normal detail gives way to the plate's geometric normal. The V's face decals below the plate are not part of what it blends over. Colour and metalness are filtered premultiplied, so between texel centres at coverage edges they weigh by coverage where the game filters the stored colour. Preview-only models (Glitter, the earlier Glossy clear coat and thin-film study) and Colour-shifting layers the export leaves out of a mixed preset keep their own plates with the ordinary linear blend and standard light; one that sits between exported layers draws above all of them. Without a renderable half-float buffer the composite is 8-bit (its roughness chain within 2 byte steps of the export's) and the studio stage's blend is approximate, as for the face decals, and without the skin under the plate every layer falls back to its own plate and a linear blend.
- **Open.** The face decals still light each pass with a surface interpolated between the skin's and the decal's; they could take the same residual light, which matters only where a decal writes a roughness or metalness different from the skin (vanilla eye makeup's 0.50 and matte lipstick's 0.4, for example).

## 4. Eyes, lashes, brows, hair and beard

- **Eyes**: one mesh, three chunks: lashes (`hair.mt`, used only by the lash option), eye (`eye_gradient.mt`, `eye.mt` or `multilayered.mt`), wetness shell (`eye_shadow.mt`) [resource]. The eye's G-buffer program projects the iris from a refracted view ray onto a plane inside the eye, writes a cornea normal and a second iris normal, and the Eye class lights them with its own Lambert-plus-GGX model; the shell darkens the eye towards the lids and adds the tear line in a forward pass. All of it, with the mod-eye chain and the preview spec, is on [eye rendering](eye-rendering.md).
- **Lashes, brows, hair, beard cards**: `hair.mt` plus `.hp` profiles and `mesh_decal_double_diffuse` / `mesh_decal_gradientmap_recolor` for brows and caps. The colour model is decoded in [hair shading](hair-shading.md) and drives the preview for **any** resolved choice since P0: the planner selects components by the creator slot (`eyebrows_color`, `eyelash_color`, `hair_color`, shared by vanilla and CCXL options [resource]) and the third-person consumer groups (`TPP`, `hairs`), and each chunk is drawn by the adapter for its template. Each hair mesh repeats its parts per level of detail (render-chunk `lodMask`); the preview draws the highest level only [resource].
- **Shadow-only parts**: each hairstyle `.app` adds a `*_shadow_npc` mesh, and CCXL hairs add their own shadow component. Vanilla hair shadow meshes have `isShadowMesh = 1`, but so does the visible beard-stubble mesh, and one CCXL shadow mesh has the flag clear with visible-looking materials. Which components the preview must skip is open. The preview draws no chunk whose template it has no adapter for, which leaves out the vanilla `*_shadow_npc` mesh (`metal_base.remt`), and draws layered (`multilayered.mt`) chunks only for piercings and eyes, which leaves out the CCXL `hair_shadow` mesh (`glass.mt`, `multilayered.mt`) [resource; that these draw nothing visible in game is a hypothesis].

## 5. How skin and complexion mods take effect

A complexion mod changes what the head looks like through two file mechanisms and, sometimes, a runtime one. The installed example in the reference profile uses all three [resource] ([scan](../research/character-customization/head-cc-render-evidence.md#complexion-mod-scan-mo2-profile-2025-again)):

| Mechanism | What it changes | What the generic resolver must follow |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-path replacement** (legacy archive override) | A mod archive ships the vanilla depot path: the five female head albedos `…\h0_000_pwa_c__basehead_d01…d05.xbm`, the head normal `h0_001_pwa_c__basehead_n01.xbm`, and **global** resources used by every `skin.mt` user: `microdetail_n.xbm` and the template-default skin profile `engine\materials\defaults\default.sp` | R1 archive precedence for **every** resource the material chain reaches, including textures and profiles, not just `.app`/`.mesh`; texture export must read the winning archive. Tone `.mi` files were untouched, so tones keep vanilla tints over replaced albedos |
| **ArchiveXL mesh patch** | A texture framework's `.xl` patches the head mesh with a donor mesh whose appearances replace vanilla ones; its local materials become the material source, add a `SecondaryAlbedo` overlay, an emissive mask and a tint mask, and point `Roughness` and `DetailNormal` at **new** paths that only the donor references | R3/R10: `resource.patch` of meshes, patch-mesh materials as the source, and resolving the new paths through the same mount set. Already implemented and shown on the reference save ([validation](../research/character-customization/resolver-validation.md)) |
| **Runtime render options** | A CET script sets the character `GameOptions` of §2 | Not in any file. Record as capture context; offer the vanilla values as the preview default |

Consequences: no per-mod code is needed, but the preview must (1) take the head material from the resolver's **effective** chain, (2) decode textures without assuming vanilla resolution (the replacement albedos are 4096² against 1024² base, per the [saved-skin trace](../research/eye-artistry/saved-skin-resource-chain.md)), and (3) treat global replacements (`default.sp`, microdetail) as affecting every `skin.mt` part that does not set its own value (the teeth set their own skin profile, so only the microdetail reaches them). Male heads are untouched by this particular package, which is female-only. Since P1 the preview does all three: on the reference profile the default V, the reference save (type 5, warm ivory) and a new-game save (type 3, senna) all resolve the replaced albedo, normal and microdetail, the replaced `default.sp` (`lobeMix` 0.6, `blurSize` 2.5, falloff 255/155/119 against the base game's 1, 1.4 and 255/178/165), and the framework's donor material with its secondary albedo, tint mask and new roughness and detail-normal paths. The texture framework's emissive mask carries `EmissiveEV` 2 but is black in this install, so nothing would glow [resource]; the preview has no emissive path yet and says so only when a mask would light up.

A visible change confirms mod-over-base for the albedo; a change after disabling only the texture framework confirms the ArchiveXL mesh patch.
3. **Decal order.** Choose eye makeup style 5 black, blush 10 and facial tattoo 2 together; capture where they overlap near the cheekbone.
4. **Lip finish roughness.** Lipstick style 5 regular, glossy and matte in turn under a moving light: expect regular and glossy to share the skin's highlight and matte to dull it.
5. **Render options.** Toggle the Character Rendering Editor between "Vanilla" and the installed preset once, same frame, to size how much runtime tuning changes the reference portrait.
6. **Switcher exclusivity and Off.** *Done 25 September; result under open question 6.* New game, female V: skin type 3, brows 3, hairstyle 5, scars Off, everything else default. Make a manual save straight after the creator and hand it over. Expected from the save's appearance list: `skin_type_03`, `eyebrows_color3` and `hair_color5` only, with no `skin_type_01`, `eyebrows_color1`, `hair_color1` and no `scars` entry. One face with no z-fighting at the hairline and brows is the visual cross-check.
7. **Replaced hair profiles.** With the reference profile's hair-colour mod enabled, the resolver binds its `brown_liquorice.hp` and `blonde_platinum.hp` over the base game's (R1). The preview therefore shows the default V's hair as pale yellow at the roots fading to saturated brown at the tips, and "blonde platinum" as a warm tan-blonde with dark roots and tips. In the creator, photograph hairstyle 1 in brown liquorice and hairstyle 5 in blonde platinum, then disable only that mod and repeat. A change confirms mod-over-base for `.hp` profiles in game. NPC hairdos in brown liquorice already show the yellow-to-brown look in game, anecdotally ([yellow-hair investigation](../research/character-customization/yellow-hair-profile-override.md)).
8. **Tone strength (tint encoding).** With the reference profile, creator skin page, skin type 1: photograph tone 1 (pale, no tint) and tone 5 (senna, multiply 0.70) under the same light, then type 3 in senna. Also record the same pair in the preview. If the game's senna darkens a mid-tone skin by about a fifth, `TintColor` is byte/255 (the preview's choice); if by about two fifths, it is sRGB-decoded. This settles [materials open question 11](materials-and-shaders.md#7-open-questions) for skin and every other `Color` parameter.
9. **Iris gradient coordinate.** Creator eyes page, one frame each: gradient red, gradient light blue and gradient brown, then blood gradient red. Bright red-orange and mid sky-blue irises mean the iris mask is read raw; near-black red and dark slate blue mean it is sRGB-decoded ([eye rendering §2.3](eye-rendering.md#23-the-gradient-coordinate-raw-or-decoded)). This also bears on how every gamma-flagged mask is sampled, the skin tint mask included.
10. **Iris axis.** Same page, the V looking straight at the camera: one close-up of both eyes with gradient light blue (a well-defined limbus). The preview will render the same frame with the iris axis turned 5° outward and 5° inward; the matching one fixes the sign of the per-eye axis.
11. **Wetness shell.** Photo mode, close-up of one eye, a single light moved slowly from above the head to below the chin: the tear-line highlight should appear along the lower lid margin only near the mirror position, and the inner and outer corners should stay darker than the middle of the sclera throughout.
12. **Cornea against iris.** Same framing, rotate the light around the head horizontally: the catch light should stay sharp and small across the iris and sclera, while the iris darkens smoothly (matte) as the light moves to the side.
13. **Face decals against the preview.** Load the reference save; in photo mode, one frontal close-up of the lips and one of the left cheek under a single fixed light, then the same with the light moved 45° up. Compare with the preview's reference-save frames: the lipstick's colour and strength (it keeps the skin's gloss), the blush's extent on the cheek and nose tip, and whether the legacy XF layers sit over the vanilla makeup. This settles open question 8 for decals and anchors test ask 3.
14. **Layered parts against the preview.** Creator face page, one close-up each under the same light: piercing style 9 in black, style 1 in silver and gold (vanilla metals), and eye colour 24 (the heart design). Compare with the preview's frames: black plastic should keep a narrow highlight, silver and gold read as brushed metal of their tint, and the heart sit the right way up. A gold that shows grey, or plastic without its colour, answers the colour-mask question (materials open question 10) against the preview's "tint everywhere" choice.
