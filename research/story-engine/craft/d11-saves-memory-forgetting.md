## In brief

- **The evidence is thin, and the dossier says so first.** The only psychology of save-scumming I found is a commentary and a conference abstract, and neither reports a finding. No study I found compares stable content against re-rolled content in a game, tests how a world's forgetting changes trust, or measures what helps a player resume a story after weeks. Most principles below are `[hypothesis]` or `[analysis]`, and §12 is a testing agenda built to replace them with evidence. The memory science underneath is old and solid, but it comes from word lists and faces, not from fiction.
- **The reload promise needs an exact scope, and today it has a hole.** The engine can promise that its own decisions are a function of the saved state, V's act and a key that names a cause, never the session (RLD-02). That is cheap, because the kernel already uses hashed draws with no stateful generator, and it matches what shipped games do when they fix a seed (XCOM, Slay the Spire). Three things leak. The kernel's session `epoch` and dossier 7's schedule "epoch" share a name and must never share a role. Hidden session state that shapes content, namely the sifter's pool (dossier 6 R-28) and the pacing state (PR-11), is not saved, so a reload can change *whether and when* a sown offer arrives even though a made offer is stable. And the game's own combat, loot and crowd draws re-roll on load and are not ours to freeze. The honest promise is: content given state is stable, timing may vary, and the player is told which is which.
- **Forgetting should be lazy, shaped like blurring, and a lens over a record that never shrinks.** An NPC's belief gets an accessibility that is a pure function of its fields and the query time, `ln(2n) − ½·ln(T) + importance + cue`, which needs only `ln`, `sqrt` and arithmetic, so it fits the kernel's allowed operations exactly. Nothing ticks it, a jump over thirty game days and a thirty-day tick agree, and the draw that decides a recall is keyed by the encounter, so the same question gets the same answer after a reload. Forgetting *widens* a belief (an exact day becomes "a few weeks back", a name goes first, the gist stays) before it drops it, which the kernel's `region` already represents. The record of what happened is never forgotten; people forget it. The player's memory settings are therefore lenses over an append-only record, and changing one destroys nothing.
- **There are two clocks of absence, and mixing them is how games end up punishing players.** The world's forgetting runs on game time. The player's forgetting runs on wall time, and the world does not move while the game is closed, so a returning player's problem is always their own memory, never the world's. That makes real-time punishment impossible by construction if no wake-up reads the wall clock (S2-I-12 below). The return view is then assembled from the record: a resume line, who is waiting, what changed, and a dismissible story so far, scaled by the age of the save loaded, built only from what V was shown, naming acts and never feelings.
- **The city remembers through three channels, and one of them is missing from the spec.** People forget on a curve, media items go stale in days, and traces in places last as long as their carrier. Reputation is a gist, so it can last a whole playthrough without anyone recalling a single day. S2 §2.5's `cosmetic` class says "only the canon event; no pin", which leaves nothing to draw the mark again after a load. The class needs a carrier with a durability and a rule for re-realising it (S11-S2-04).
- **Two precedents show how not to treat absence and reload.** A town-keeping game that punished real-time absence with weeds and pests later patched in help for lapsed players. A tactics game that fixed its random seed to stop reload-on-miss left its lead designer saying he probably should have offered an option. The first is the rule (never punish or reward absence, ABS-09); the second is a reason to keep a fresh-draw option possible and decide it from evidence (RLD-10, T11-09).

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

Quality bar 4 says V's choices change outcomes that the world remembers. A player who can reload at will sees two versions of any moment, and a player who returns after weeks has lost part of their own memory of what the world is meant to remember. This dossier asks what the engine owes a player who reloads, how a record that branches with every save should behave, what a character, a rumour and a city should remember and for how long, what forgetting should feel like, and what a returning player should see. It also says which of these are player settings.

### 2.2 What the failures have in common

1. **Stability, or its absence, is applied where the player did not expect it.** XCOM's fixed seed frustrated players who wanted variety, and a free rewind left no weight. Neither is wrong in principle. What is wrong is not saying what is stable, and having no way to choose.
2. **Hidden state breaks a promise silently.** Slay the Spire's streams were separate in intent and correlated in fact. In our case the equivalents are the session epoch, the sifter's pool and the pacing state: each is a store that content can read and a reload does not restore (§2.3 of this dossier, RLD-02, S11-P5-03).
3. **Real-time clocks make absence a debt.** The town-keeping game turned a break into a chore and had to undo it. The rule the dossiers already hold (BND-26) is also a structural one: no wake-up reads the wall clock.
4. **Forgetting is read as indifference when it hits what the player cares about.** The lens's strongest claim (one forgotten significant act teaches "the world forgets everything") rests on a reported controversy and forum material, not a study. The cases in this dossier cannot test it, which is why §12 puts it first among the human tests.
5. **The returning player's loss is their own,** and the sources that talk about it are essays, forum posts and a laboratory result on interruptions measured in seconds.

### 2.3 Where the advice itself fails

| Advice | Where it fails |
|---|---|
| "Derive offers from the saved state, so a reload shows the same offer" (SAV-02; PR-12, PR-13) | It covers an offer already made. An offer not yet made depends on session state the save does not hold: the sifter's pool (R-28), the pacing state (PR-11) and the settling period. The same save, loaded twice, can send the next push at different times, and a different push if the world moved in between |
| "Power-law forgetting with an importance boost reads as more human" (L12 F18 p1) | The power law is well supported as a description of lab retention, but the lab tasks are word lists, faces and nonsense syllables, and the chosen form is contested: a large review of 210 data sets could not separate four candidate functions. No study I found compares a power law, a hard time-out and perfect recall in a game |
| "Reloading is a free, normal activity" (BPS; Shadow Gambit) | Researchers in the BPS piece cite work that reads it as wish fulfilment that weakens the weight of choice, and the Shadow Gambit designer says players feel cheap. The engine can stay neutral, and still the weight has to come from elsewhere than the cost of undoing |
| "Rate each memory's importance when it forms" (generative agents; Ryan's salience) | The rating is the designer's, and the lens notes importance scores that disagree with the player's own sense of significance. Salience from the NPC's concerns must be corrected by what V showed mattered (MEM-05) |
| "A recap helps a returning player" (practice; Witcher 3 and Dragon Quest XI examples, from secondary sources) | No experiment I found measures it for games. It could also leak unseen content, or read as hand-holding, or be skipped. §12 tests it |
| "Misremembering is charming" (L12 F18 p2) | Untested. A misremembering that reads as a bug costs trust, and an error about what V did is exactly the one the engine must never make (AGY-39) |

## 4. Principles

Grades are P1 §4's. `[hypothesis]` is the commonest grade here. "Produce" and "Check" say how a procedure could make or test the principle, and name the layer and the specification section. Numbers marked **[J]** are judgement calls to be replaced by §12.

### A. Reloading and save-scumming (SAV-01, SAV-02)

| ID | Principle | Grade | Source and instance | Scale · layers | Operationalise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RLD-01 | **Treat a reload as ordinary play. No rule reads a load or a reload as an input to what V is offered or told.** | [hypothesis]; [analysis] | BPS (opened): little research, two motives, competing views, and harsh judgement of save-scumming may rest on assumptions about why players do it. | RLD-02 | **Outcome is a function of the saved state, V's act and a key that names a cause. A key may name a seed, a role, a choice and an attempt. ### 5.2 The recall function

For a belief `b`, a believer, a query time `t` and an encounter `e` (the contact event's stable `Id`):

```
T      = max(1, (t − b.formed) / (game day · m))          // m: memory scale (§5.6) and the believer's trait (MEM-14)
a      = ln(2·max(1, b.uses)) − ½·ln(T) + I(b.salience) + G(b) + S(slot) + C(cue)
u      = clamp(rand("recall", believer, b.id, e), 2⁻⁵³, 1 − 2⁻⁵³)
z      = a + s·ln(u / (1 − u))                              // logistic noise, from ln only
form   = exact if z > τ₀ ; region if z > τ₁ ; gist if z > τ₂ ; gone otherwise      (τ₀ > τ₁ > τ₂)
```

- **Allowed arithmetic only.** The closed form needs `ln`, `+ − × ÷` and a comparison. If use times were stored, the exact sum `Σ t⁻½` would also need `sqrt`, since `t⁻½` is `1/sqrt(t)`. No `exp`, no `pow` ([S0 §1.3](../spec/kernel.md#13-arithmetic)). The logistic noise `s·ln(u/(1−u))` makes `P(z > τ)` equal to the logistic function of `(a − τ)/s`, so the table below can be computed with `exp` offline while the engine uses only `ln`.
- **Nested outcomes.** One draw `u` serves all three levels. If the belief is recalled exactly, it is recalled at region and at gist. So a half-memory is never "better" than an exact one.
- **Terms.** `uses` counts corroborations, recalls and retellings (MEM-09), with a gain cap per game day. `I` is salience: classes 0 to 3 map to 0, 0.75, 1.5, 3 **[J]**. `G` is a gist bonus for memorable beliefs, +3 on the gist level only **[J]**. `S(slot)` is the slot-class offset of §5.3. `C` is the cue: +0.5 for the encounter place, +0.5 for a person in the encounter, +1 if the subject is in an active frame of the believer, capped at 1.5 **[J]**.
- **The base form is the ACT-R base-level equation** with decay 0.5 and evenly spaced use, as quoted in Trafton and colleagues: `ln(2n/√T)`. If use is clustered early, the form overstates; the alternative is to store the use times, which the belief record does not hold. This is a compromise for the ledger (S11-S0-03).
- **Lazy.** `a` is a pure function of the belief and `t` (a `Derive` that reads the query time, [S0 §2.1](../spec/kernel.md#21-dimensions-and-values), v4), so no process ticks it. The only scheduled work is the coarse sweep that drops beliefs below the floor and writes the widening replacements (S11-S0-04, S11-S0-05).
- **Fixed by the encounter.** The same `e` gives the same `u`. Asking twice in one contact, or reloading, changes nothing. A recall adds one to `uses`, so the next contact is likelier to succeed (FGT-05).

**Illustrative curve** (`s = 0.5`, `τ₀ = −0.63`, tuned so a once-seen minor favour is a coin toss at 14 game days; no cue; the exact level only; **[J]**). Probability that the detail is recalled exactly:

| Belief | 1 day | 7 days | 14 days | 30 days | 90 days | 365 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minor, seen once (`uses` 1, salience 0) | 0.93 | 0.67 | 0.50 | 0.32 | 0.14 | 0.04 |
| Minor, retold three times (`uses` 4) | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.94 | 0.88 | 0.71 | 0.38 |
| Significant, seen once (salience 2, `I` = 1.5) | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0.95 | 0.90 | 0.76 | 0.44 |
| Significant, retold three times | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.93 |
| Grudge or debt (salience 3, `I` = 3) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.94 |

Read it as the detail level. A significant act that V returned to is memorable, so its **gist** is recalled with a probability near one throughout, whatever the exact-level column says. The point of the table is the relative shape: retelling and salience push the curve out by orders of magnitude, a trivial thing is gone within a season, and nothing here ticks.

### 5.3 What forgetting does to each kind of slot

An event as an NPC holds it is a set of belief slots. They fade at different rates, which is what makes gist outlast detail (MEM-06). The offsets are added to `a` **[J]**.

| Slot class | Example | Offset `S` | What blur does | Error forms allowed (MEM-11) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V's act and its outcome | "V paid the debt" | +2.5 | Stays at gist; never in the error classes | None. Never altered |
| Who it concerned (V and the NPC's own ties) | "it was Tomas's debt" | +1.5 | Region to a set of near candidates only for people outside the NPC's ties | Conflation of two similar *strangers* only |
| Place | "at the docks stall" | +0.5 | District, then city | Swap to a nearby place |
| Time | "the 14th" | 0 | Interval widens: day, days, a fortnight, a season | Shift within the interval |
| Amounts and wording | "forty eddies", "what V said" | −1 | Order of magnitude, then gone | Round or swap a nearby amount |
| Names of minor others | "the courier, Ana" | −1.5 | Name first, then role ("some courier") | Swap for a similar name |
| Reasons and valence of others' acts | "why the collector came" | +0.5 | Assimilation to present attitude allowed | Assimilate |

Names are first to go because name retrieval is a frequent site of blocking (Schacter, read), and Ryan's model makes the chance of mutation depend on the facet type. A region never excludes the true value until the belief is dropped, so a half-memory is honest. Because a region is the kernel's own form for an `int` interval or an `enum` set, widening is a replacement by `BeliefSet` and needs one new registry function, `Widen` (S11-S0-05).

### 5.4 The three channels of a city's memory

Only the form (two or more channels with different lifetimes, the longer one being recorded) comes from the study of collective attention. The lifetimes are for a game that covers days to months of game time **[J]**.

| Channel | Carrier | Typical lifetime of detail | What keeps it | What ends it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| People | Beliefs held by residents, passed on by telling | Minor: about two weeks to a season. Significant: gist for a playthrough | Retelling, cues, salience, being memorable | Turnover, no retelling, supersession |
| Media and records | Feed items, news, logs, a bounty board | An item stays fresh for 3 to 7 game days, then is archived, not erased | A follow-up, an anniversary, an archive someone searches | Replacement by newer items |
| Places | Traces: a mark, a rebuilt stall, a posted notice, a shrine | By carrier: paint 60 to 180 days, a notice 7 to 30, a rebuilt structure or a scar until changed | The carrier's durability | Cleaning, tearing down, rebuilding, being streamed out and not redrawn |

An event's civic memory is the longest carrier it has. Reputation is the gist aggregated over the first channel and fed by the others (CTY-02).

## 6. Walk-throughs

### 6.1 A reload at a decision

V has taken a gig and is offered a choice by the client, a pay-in-advance or a pay-on-delivery. V quicksaves, picks advance, and the collector later turns up angry.
1. V loads the quicksave. The engine classifies the pin as *behind* (the ledger has later entries), replays the branch's ops from the nearest snapshot, silently (I-13), and starts a new epoch. Pacing restarts in settling for 90 play-seconds.
2. The pending offer was displayed and is unanswered, so it is re-presented as the same `Id` where the channel keeps it, not as new.
3. V picks delivery this time. The next commit forks the branch. The collector does not turn up: the cause was the advance, and the cause is not in this branch.
4. In the first branch the collector's arrival followed from the advance, through a draw keyed by the seed and the choice, not by the load. Loading the quicksave a third time and picking advance again gives the same collector, the same line and the same price.
5. Nothing the engine said or did mentioned the reloads. A branch-diff report shows that the two branches differ in the client's tie, the collector's presence and two facts downstream, all inside the cone of the choice (M11-03).

What this does not do: freeze the fight in the warehouse. Who stands where and how the shooting goes are the game's.

### 6.2 Two clocks

V's vendor, Ana, saw V cover a debt (salience 2, retold twice) and, on another day, V buy a drink (salience 0).
- **Case A. Three real weeks away, no game time.** V loads a save that is 21 days old. Settling runs, the resume line names the last thread, and V is offered "where things stand": the debt, the courier job, and that Ana is waiting on a part V promised. Ana remembers everything exactly as before, because her clock did not move. Nothing is lost by the break.
- **Case B. Thirty game days in play, no break.** V sleeps and works across a month. When V returns to the stall, Ana's reunion line names the debt (gist and detail both recalled; the draw is keyed by this encounter). She has no memory of the drink, and nothing is said. She half-remembers when V first came by ("sometime before the rains") because the time slot has widened to an interval. V mentions the docks and she places it.
- **Case C. Both.** The rules compose without interacting: the world's blur follows game time, the recap follows the save's age, and the returning player sees a story so far that is built from the record and not from what Ana still recalls.

## 8. Tensions

| Tension | One side | Other side | Resolution here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stability or variety on reload** (SAV-01, SAV-02) | Fixed outcomes stop fishing and make the world feel real (XCOM, Slay the Spire) | Players may want variety, and a fixed result can look scripted (XCOM's designer; the lens) | Stable by default; variety through differing states and acts (RLD-03); a salt left in the keys for a later option (RLD-10); decide from T11-09 |
| **Neutral on reloads or settle after a load** (SES-02, DIR-59) | No rule reads a load | The pacing state restarts, so a load must change delivery | Loads gate delivery only, never content (RLD-01); write the exception into SES-02's audit |
| **Stable offers or a pool that is not saved** (PR-13, R-28, PR-11) | A reload shows the same offer | The sifter's pool and the pacing state are session state | Made offers are pinned. Unmade ones are stable in content given state and may differ in timing. Measure the divergence before deciding to persist more (S11-P5-03, open question 2) |
| **Forgetting for realism or total recall** (SAV-03; the map) | Decay reads human and keeps the record small | One apparent forgetting teaches "the world forgets" | Forget detail, not gist; protect what V showed matters; mark half-memories; guard the first one (MEM-05, FGT-01, FGT-02) |
| **Realism of the curve or the feel of it** | The power law fits laboratory retention | Hard time-outs ship in a popular game, and lab tasks are not fiction | Use the power law because it is lazy and keeps gist; tune the numbers by feel; test against a time-out (T11-10) |
| **Showing what the world remembers or keeping a model of V out of sight** (SET-05, ETH-20) | A view builds trust and counters the "forgets everything" reading | A view of beliefs risks exposing the director's model of V | Show only what a person would say; never the director's beliefs |
| **A recap that helps or one that spoils** (ABS-04, ABS-08) | It restores orientation | It can leak unseen state, hand-hold, or be skipped | Exposed-only, acts not feelings, offered not forced, tested |
| **Author-set persistence or the player's dial** (NAR-26, SET-01) | Authors know which facts must last | Players know how much they want to carry | Authors set kind and a `remember` flag; the player sets scale; promises and debts are never scaled away |
| **Wall-clock input or determinism** (S2-I-1) | The return view needs the gap | The world must not read it | The gap is a recorded session input that only the return view reads (ABS-06, S2-I-12) |
| **Memory depth or record size** (SAV-03, P7) | A long memory is richer | The record grows with every belief | Coalesce into pattern beliefs; sweep below the most generous floor only; measure (M11-12) |

## 9. Lateral links

- **Counter-based random streams (Slay the Spire's suggested fix) are the kernel's hashed draws.** The kernel keys every draw by name and index, with no generator state (S0 §1.4), so it already has the property the Slay the Spire 2 write-up says would avoid replaying call counts on load.
- **Lazy forgetting is a cache policy.** Trafton and colleagues compare activation to deciding which item to evict from a cache, where recency predicts need. The kernel's mark and sweep ([§7.3](../spec/kernel.md#73-retention-and-forgetting)) is the same family: keep what is likely to be needed.
- **The ledger tree is a version-control history.** A fork at divergence, a parent pointer and an immutable entry are a branch and a commit. The branch-diff tool is `diff` between two commits.
- **A lens over an append-only record is event sourcing.** The record is the log and the memory settings are a read model.
- **Widening a belief is the same operation as a coarser level of detail.** A region that grows with time is the dimensional model's "less resolved" locality, applied to a belief. The foundation is the source of that idea; the memory literature is a cross-check on how fast it should happen (comparison only).
- **Outer Wilds' ship log and the exposure ledger** are both a persistent record of what the player has been shown.
- **A returning player is an interrupted task resumed.** Trafton and colleagues' warning lag, prospective goals and cues are the resume note, the "where you were" line and the cue at the place.
- **A town that punishes absence and a studio that fixes it** is the dossier's one shipped lesson about the player's clock.
- **Rumour distortion and the serial reproduction of a story** are the telling operators of MEM-10 (the map's SYS-18 and PER-03).

## 10. Open questions

1. **Does the player prefer stable or fresh draws, and does it change how real the world feels?** No study. T11-09.
2. **How much does the unsaved sifter pool and pacing state change what a reload shows?** Replay a save N times with the same acts (T11-01, with the full engine from M3) and measure the divergence in which seed is sown and when. If large, either persist more or accept and say so. Linked to R-28's revisit trigger.
3. **Where should the wall-gap thresholds be?** The buckets are guesses shaped by laboratory curves of days. T11-12 and T11-20.
4. **May a player declare a lost history?** Dragon Age Keep lets players set world state. For us the declaration would be a pin from the player's own account, flagged `declared` and weaker than an observed pin. Does it break D-09 or TIM-06? Needs a decision.
5. **Can authored content use a diegetic time loop without reading loads?** A loop device would have to be an in-fiction act that the engine itself triggers, not a detection of the player's reload. Probably a content-pack design, not an engine feature.
6. **Does the view of what the world remembers help or expose too much?** T11-13.
7. **How many game days does a real session cover?** The curve's scale depends on it, and I have no figure. The test sessions and the quest corpus (P2) can give it.
8. **Do NPCs rehearse privately?** Rumination would raise `uses` for grievances. It would also make grudges immortal. Keep `uses` for tellings and recalls until a story needs it.
9. **Does socially shared retrieval-induced forgetting transfer?** A listener forgetting related, untold detail is an effect in word-pair and conversation experiments. A small decay of related, unmentioned facets of a told event is a cheap test of whether rumours converge on what was told.
10. **Does a power law beat a hard time-out in perceived humanity?** RimWorld ships time-outs. T11-10 includes a time-out arm.
11. **What does the game port give as a cue?** Place and person are available; an active frame in the NPC's own context is the engine's. Check the cue set against the events the port can provide ([game events](../../../knowledge/game-events.md)).
12. **Is `uses` enough, or does the belief need its use times?** The closed form assumes even spacing. A longer record of times would be exact and larger. Measure the error on simulated play.
13. **What happens to `told` beliefs from a source V never met?** They should fade faster than first-hand ones, because evidence strength depends on the source (Ryan, read). The rate is unset.

## 12. Testing agenda

The evidence is thin, so the plan is to find out. It is ordered by cost: offline tests cost days and need no people, panels on transcripts need a prototype (M3) and consenting participants, in-game checks go into one booked session, and a longitudinal check follows the first testers. Thresholds are **[J]** and are written down before the run. Where a human sample is small, report the effect size with its interval and call the result exploratory. Data stay local and consented (D-09; dossier 12). Agents never run the game: the in-game items form one prepared session card for the maintainer, with the exact build and profile and versioned capture.
