## In brief

- **A line and a veil are two different mechanisms, and one ladder covers both.** Every content class gets four levels: 0 line, 1 veiled, 2 shown, 3 full. A *line* is an exclusion at production: the world never mints the class, the director never sows it, and V is never shown it. A *veil* is a limit at presentation: the world may hold the event, and V meets it as aftermath, news, a sound or a fade. The same ladder carries the violence-against-residents and sanction dials of S2 §2.17, the dread setting and `dreadMax` of §2.18, and the `intimate` scene's fade. Packs, districts and seasons may only lower a ceiling, never raise it (TAX-02 to TAX-04, TNE-01).
- **The stance is the base game's.** The players are adults. Violence, dark themes, drugs and the rest are the player's choice, every dial defaults to what the base game itself does, and nothing is restricted for taste. Adult themes are genre material (sex work, braindances, bodies as commodities, exploitation) and get the same depth as violence, at the level of systems and non-explicit treatment: how they enter stories, dials and tags, representation, and the consent of the characters involved (ADT-01 to ADT-05). Only explicit sexual material is left to human pack authors, and the engine owes it a tag and a fade (INT-01 to INT-03).
- **Only three floors, each justified by law or the platform.** No sexual content involving a minor (statutes reach drawn and computer-generated depictions); no real, identifiable person (the publisher's fan-content terms); no real-world hate content in the system's own voice (the same terms, drawn narrowly). They are enforced by structure: age is a required attribute of every person and absent means minor, so a minor cannot be bound to a sexual role in anything the engine or a pack can express. Text filters stay advisory, since the AI Dungeon episode shows they miss and misfire in both directions (FLR-01 to FLR-03, ENF-02).
- **Warnings do not protect; the player's own controls do.** A meta-analysis finds that content warnings raised anticipatory anxiety and did not reduce distress. So notices are off until the player flags a class, descriptors are always one input away, and a surprise can never cross a ceiling the player set. A shipped precedent (Dead Space's two opt-in features, both off by default) shows a notice and a hide-the-scene option are buildable (NTC-01 to NTC-05).
- **The tabletop tools become conveniences.** Session zero is an optional first-run setup whose skip means "as the game". Lines, veils and "ask first" become the ceiling and notice per class. The X-Card becomes a one-input retract that is neutral, immediate and never a model input. A study of one in-game assault finds that explicit-refusal tools miss indirect refusals, so leaving, skipping or going quiet always counts as a plain decline, and the engine never infers distress from it (TLS-01 to TLS-08).
- **Representation is audited like a census, with tests a machine can run.** Protected attributes never enter role selection. The build tabulates attribute by role with effect sizes, recasts with an attribute swapped and compares, measures proxies, simulates feedback loops, and prints a placement report for human sensitivity readers. Name pools are hand-curated with datasheets. Counts measure presence, not dignity, so a warmth and competence read stays human (REP-01 to REP-10).
- **The lens's violence-effects claim did not survive.** The APA's 2020 resolution finds a reliable association between violent game use and aggressive outcomes, and rejects the attribution of lethal violence to games; it asks ratings to describe levels and characteristics of violence. That supports a graded, described dial, not "violence is harmless" (VIO-01, §2.3).
- **Most of the evidence is thin.** Tabletop safety tools have practice and criticism but no outcome comparison I could find. The warning meta-analysis is lab work. The representation audits are my own hypotheses on top of census and fairness methods built for other settings (§2.3, §11).

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

The engine generates and fits quests, gigs and encounters in a violent, adult city, and the player can see only what comes through the arbiter. Two things can go wrong without any single bad line of authored content. A procedural layer can produce harm no author wrote (a minor in a bar scene, a lethal event nobody weighed, a stereotype in the casting distribution). And a player can meet content they did not choose, with no way out that costs nothing. This dossier asks what the engine must never produce whatever the setting, what the player may dial and how, how the tabletop world's consent tools carry over to one player and a machine game master, how stereotyping in procedural casting is measured, and how notices reach the player before a scene.

### 1.4 What is already settled and used as given

- **The maintainer's stance on content** (a correction to this dossier's brief). There is no nudity policy; the players are adults. Adult content (sex, nudity, violence, drugs, dark themes) is the player's choice, as in the base game. Dials default to what the base game does, not to something stricter. Hard floors are kept to the genuine minimum, where law or the platform requires them, and each is justified. The consent and safety tools are player conveniences, not restrictions the engine imposes. Adult themes are part of the cyberpunk genre (sex work, braindances, bodies as commodities, exploitation) and are treated as genre material with the same design depth as violence, at the level of systems and non-explicit treatment. Only explicit sexual material is left to human pack authors, for which the engine gives a content tag and a fade option. The hard floor stays: no sexual content involving minors, ever.
- **D-09 and D-22.** V is observed, never owned. Content offers rather than presumes. The engine never speaks for V; an NPC may voice its own belief about V's motive under the D-22 conditions.
- **D-02.** Four layers, authored content above procedural. Dials and exclusions are the only mechanism by which a setting changes what is produced (STY-05).
- **The `intimate` scene class** (S2 Appendix A, L-11). It may be filled only by a human-authored pack scene, always with a fade alternative.
- **D-20 and D-21.** No patent remarks were made. The engine's concepts are first-party; the tabletop tools and fairness methods below are comparisons, never sources of those concepts.

### 2.3 Where the advice itself fails, and what did not survive

- **Safety tools are reactive and need a voice.** The X-Card is called "reactive, not preventive" by one designer, and was first attacked and later defended by another (Wikipedia, page). The one study of a failure argues the tools over-rely on explicit refusals (Klein and Voorhees). For a single player the engine can lower the cost of a refusal to zero, but cannot read minds; it must not try (TLS-05, TLS-06).
- **No outcome evidence for tabletop safety tools.** A search for a comparative or survey study found none; a forum reply says the same, which is a lead and not a finding. Every tool principle in §5.6 is [practice] with that gap stated.
- **Warnings are not useless and not protective.** The meta-analysis is lab work with mostly non-clinical samples, as the lens notes. "They do not reduce distress" must not become "no descriptors": informed choice is a separate job, and a player who raised a ceiling wants the notice.
- **Audits can pass a stereotype.** A balanced roster can still be written as caricature (F-07), census shares do not fit a fictional city, and independence erases real structure that the genre's own critique concerns. The honest fix, from the lens, is to make any correlation an authored, visible, reviewed parameter (REP-02).
- **Four-fifths is a screen, not a test.** The US selection-rate rule treats a group rate under four-fifths of the highest as evidence of adverse impact, and the same text says smaller differences can matter, larger ones can be noise at small numbers, and small samples may be pooled. I use it only as a coarse screen at small counts and prefer effect sizes with intervals at large ones (REP-03).
- **Every floor is a cost, so there are three.** A rule about minors collides with art, a rule about real people and hate collides with satire, and an over-broad term in a licence can reach dark fiction. The floors therefore concern depiction and role only (FLR-01), are kept to what the law and the publisher's terms leave no choice about, and flag the one term drawn broadly (FLR-03) for dossier 19 to confirm.
- **Over-warning is a route to blandness,** and over-editing silently brings its own backlash (the lens cites the 2023 Roald Dahl edits, [sec]; not re-read). A public changelog is cheaper than either (REP-10).

## 4. What the game and the sources show

### 4.1 The game's own content, the baseline for the defaults

- **Descriptors.** The ESRB lists six for Cyberpunk 2077: Blood and Gore, Intense Violence, Nudity, Strong Language, Strong Sexual Content, Use of Drugs and Alcohol, with an M rating (*summary* of the listing). Its summary text mentions mutilated corpses, a nailing scene, optional brief first-person sex scenes with partial nudity, and character customisation that can show genitalia. This is the baseline the engine's defaults are measured against (TAX-06).
- **Nudity is a native game switch.** Body options carry a censorship flag; whether nudity is allowed is native (`IsNudityAllowed`), and with it off the game dresses V in its own censorship underwear ([body rendering §3](../../../knowledge/body-rendering.md)). The engine has no policy about it and neither reads nor writes it (INT-03).
- **Content facts in the quest data.** Scenes read `censorship_homosexuality` and `censorship_oversexualized` 71 and 30 times; what sets them was not traced ([dossier 2 §2.4](d02-relationships.md)). Whether the engine should mirror them is an open question (§11).
- **Children exist in the population records.** `isChild` is set on 40 records; the records hold no age, name or job ([population §7](../../../knowledge/population-and-affiliations.md)). So the engine cannot read an age from the game and must carry its own attribute (FLR-01).
- **Romance scenes.** Choices come before the intimate scene: Judy's pier scene has 19 choice nodes and the intimate scene has none; "had sex" and "is my partner" are separate facts ([dossier 2 §2.4](d02-relationships.md)).

### 4.3 Warnings and content features in shipped games

- **Dead Space** (Motive remake) ships two options, both off by default, under accessibility: a short pop-up before a disturbing section (example text: "CONTENT WARNING: The upcoming section contains depictions of self-inflicted death") and a screen effect that obscures the scene while the audio plays. Scope: intense violence against humans, strong psychological horror and self-harm; not violence against monsters (EA page).
- **Gears of War 4** removes gore from encounters, not from cutscenes; **Sunset Overdrive** bleeps profanity and asterisks subtitles; **Doki Doki Literature Club!** opens with four warning screens and an age and consent confirmation (AbleGamers page).
- **The guidelines** list, among others: "Provide an option to disable blood and gore" (Cognitive, Advanced) and "Avoid flickering images and repetitive patterns" (Cognitive, Basic), whose limits are three flashes a second over 25 per cent or more of the screen, or any red flash (Game Accessibility Guidelines). The W3C criterion counts general and red flashes separately, uses a combined flash area of 0.006 steradians within any 10-degree field, and exempts fine balanced patterns such as noise (WCAG 2.3.1).

### 4.4 Representation

- **Stereotype content.** Fiske and colleagues propose that stereotypes organise on warmth and competence, status predicting competence and competition predicting low warmth, with four emotional quadrants: pity, envy, admiration and contempt (*summary*). The contempt quadrant (low on both) is the one a cyberpunk underclass is most likely to fall into.
- **Generated demographics.** Horswill's point is that when expectations about combinations exist, accidental violations can be jarring or offensive, and a system that always produces thief characters who are Black or have racialised names plays into prejudice. His system samples from census tables; he reports its limits (§2.1, F-06) and advises hand curation for a commercial game.
- **Fairness tests.** Counterfactual fairness asks whether a decision would be the same in a world where the individual belonged to a different group (Kusner and colleagues, abstract). The US four-fifths rule is a long-used coarse screen with stated exceptions. Datasheets propose documenting a dataset's motivation, composition, collection and recommended uses (Gebru and colleagues, abstract).

### 4.5 Law and platforms, as orientation

- **Fiction can be unlawful.** The US statute reaches drawings, cartoons and computer-generated images of minors in sexually explicit conduct, and the minor need not exist; one limb requires obscenity and another does not. The UK offence covers "an image" of a child that is pornographic, within a stated list of acts and grossly offensive; the page says nothing limiting it to photographs.
- **The mod's own licence.** CD PROJEKT RED's fan-content rules bar content that is "racist, xenophobic, sexist, defamatory, illegal or offensive", allow real people and brands from the games only with permission, and have no rule that mentions sexual content or minors.
- **Platforms.** A mod host has said it must add age verification for some adult content under UK and EU rules (*summary*). Dossier 19 owns this.

## 5. Principles

Grades are P1 §4's. Every principle is a [hypothesis] for this engine even where the underlying observation is [practice] or [empirical]; the grade says what the observation rests on. Scale letters: E encounter, G gig, A arc. Layer numbers are the plan's; C is player configuration, X expression, T tools, P9 evaluation. Lint numbers `L18-nn` are provisional and the coordinator renumbers at integration (§13).

**Vocabulary used throughout.**
- **Class:** a content class from a closed hierarchical vocabulary (`harm.sanction`, `sex.intimate`). §6.1 lists the starter set.
- **Intensity:** how much of a class a piece of content carries: 1 reported or implied, 2 shown, 3 sustained or graphic.
- **Ceiling:** the highest intensity of a class that may be produced, offered or shown. Level 0 is a *line*, 1 a *veil*, 2 *shown*, 3 *full*.
- **Floor:** a rule no setting, pack, profile or seed can override.
- **Authored-only:** a class whose content must come from a human-authored fragment placed by an author.
- **Envelope:** the highest intensity per class over every beat a seed can reach.
- **Implied form:** an authored lower-intensity alternate of a fragment.

### 5.1 The floors: only what law or the platform requires (ETH-09, ETH-01)

### 6.2 The effective-ceiling gate

**Tags.** A fragment declares `content: [{class, intensity}]`. A seed's envelope is the maximum per class over every beat it can reach, including implied alternates only where no fuller form is reachable.

**Effective ceiling.** `E(c) = min( player[c], profile.contentMax[c], hardCap[c], 2 if the seed is procedural else 3 )`, minus any pair the player has paused (a soft exclusion lowers `E` for that class to one below the paused level).

| Gate | Where | What it does when `intensity > E(c)` | Records |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Production** | S1, before `EventCanon` | `E(c) = 0` for a class: every event kind in it is refused. Veils do not act here | The refusal, with reason `excluded` |
| **1. Sowing** | S2 §1.3 and §5 | The seed's envelope exceeds `E` for a class it `requires`: not sown. Otherwise sown, with substitutions to come | A fit-report line with the class |
| **2. Fragment choice** | S2 §7.2 | Choose the most specific applicable fragment whose tags are all within `E`; else its `implied` form; else, if the beat is `droppable`, drop it; else the seed steps down the `onUnfilled` ladder and withdraws quietly | `provenance.gated: {class, from, to}` |
| **3. Release** | S2 §8.3 | Re-evaluate with the settings in force at release. A change the player made since `prepare` wins over the frozen offer | The same |
| **4. Display** | S2 §8.5 | A last check at `displayed`; the delivery carries its descriptors | The descriptors shown |
| **Player action** | any time | `retract`: the running beat plays its implied form or ends; the offer is withdrawn; the exact pair is paused | A neutral `retracted` ledger entry, no model event |

**Floors** run beside the gates and are not parameterised by settings: FLR-01 at compile time (role reachability) and at casting (a candidate whose `ageClass` is not `adult` is removed from any barred role); FLR-02 and FLR-03 at build.

### 6.3 The settings object

```ts

  v: 1;
  preset: 'as-the-game' | 'lighter' | 'custom';      // 'as-the-game' when setup was skipped

  notice: Record<ClassId, boolean>;                  // absent means false
  softExclusions: Array<{ class: ClassId; level: 1 | 2 | 3; at: GameTime }>;
}
// Recorded as SettingsSet.content; marked sensitive; stripped from exports.
```

There is no field for orientation, mood, tolerance or "what V likes". There is no nudity field: the engine has no policy about it (INT-03).

### 6.4 The representation audit

**Inputs.** The reference model, run over at least 20 world seeds for several game-years each, with the standard settings vectors; the faction, district and lexicon registries; the protected-attribute registry (S18-S4-06).

**Procedure.**
1. **Tabulate.** For every protected attribute `a` and role class `r` (offender, victim, informer, enforcer, love interest, comic relief, rescuee, professional, helper, ordinary resident), count persons cast. Report share of `a` in each `r` against its share in the population.
2. **Association.** Cramér's V per `(a, r)` with a bootstrap interval. Subtract declared codings (REP-02), which are printed beside. Flag `V > 0.05` **[tunable]**. Below 30 per cell use the four-fifths screen instead and print the counts.
3. **Swap.** Sample 1,000 casts per seed. Flip `a` on the cast candidate, keep everything else, recast with the same draw. Any change in role is a defect unless declared.
4. **Proxies.** Mutual information between each protected attribute and each role-driving feature (district, name pool, look name, voice set, trade).
5. **Loop.** Regress incident rate per district on declared variables and demographic share; flag a non-zero share slope.
6. **Support.** For every tag, the number of role classes spanned, with at least one professional or helper role.
7. **Text.** Draw a blind sample of generated character descriptions per tag for the rater panel; compute descriptor polarity from the annotated lexicon (REP-07).
8. **Placement report.** Print the coded-faction by district-tier by role table for sensitivity readers (REP-08).

**Output.** A report with each result, its threshold, and a line "not checked": dignity of prose, whether a coding is a stereotype in meaning, texture erased by over-correction, and anything outside the registered attributes. A finding is a warning below `ready` and needs a recorded reason at `ready`.

| # | Tension | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| T-1 | An unrestricted default against the engine's own craft limits | The defaults are the game's. The only limits that bind everyone are the three floors. The procedural cap and authored-only rules are craft rules about weight and framing, and a pack author can lift them for what they write |

## 10. Lateral links

- **The retract is a circuit breaker.** It stops a running routine at once, with no questions, as the bridge's kill switch stops a running behaviour. Same shape: one input, immediate, neutral, logged.
- **A line is production filtering; a veil is redaction at display.** The same split exists between not collecting a datum and masking it when shown. The engine does the first at S1 and the second at the arbiter.
- **The floors are a type system.** A minor that cannot be bound to a barred role is an illegal state made unrepresentable, not a rule checked afterwards.
- **A placement report is a code review of the data.** A reviewer reads a table, not every instance.
- **Safe-reporting guidance in journalism** is the same problem as news text on `harm.self`: reporting can help or harm, and the guidance is about how, not whether (WHO, overview).
- **Session zero is a settings screen with a conversation attached.** The conversation does not survive into single player; the screen does.

## 11. Open questions

1. **The defaults table.** Is ceiling 3 right for every class? `harm.minor` at 0 and the classes marked "parity unchecked" (`harm.self`, `harm.torture`, `exploit.body`, `intrude.v`) need the corpus: which of these does the game itself show, and how? A research request for the queue.
2. **Harm to children.** Not a floor under the maintainer's correction, so it is an authored-only class defaulting to 0. Confirm that default (TAX-09), or say the engine should simply not support it.
3. **The hate floor.** FLR-03 rests on the publisher's fan-content terms, which are broad ("offensive"). Does a mod that ships dark fiction read that term as barring a character's bigotry? Dossier 19 should confirm; this dossier assumes not.
4. **The game's own content facts.** Scenes read `censorship_homosexuality` and `censorship_oversexualized`. Should authored packs be able to read them, for parity with the game in a region where they are set? What sets them is untraced (a P2 request).
5. **Retract persistence.** Paused pairs stay until revived. Should they lapse after a time or a load? Staying is safest for the player who retracted, and costs an item on a list.
6. **Is a first-run setup a screen or only a settings entry?** The rule "defaults first" argues for no screen; the tool's value argues for an offer. A UX call.
7. **Settings and shared saves.** The record keeps content settings for replay, and a shared save would reveal them. Stripping them breaks replay on another machine. S0 owns the answer (S18-S0-01).
8. **Does a skip exist for game-run cutscenes?** The bridge answers (S5). The retract can only change engine content.
9. **Which attributes are protected?** The list in S18-S4-06 is a start; some (class, wealth) are the genre's own subject and are deliberately left as setting variables. A sensitivity reader should review the list.
10. **Do the audit thresholds mean anything for a city this size?** Cramér's V of 0.05 and 30 per cell are tunables I chose; the first runs should set them.
11. **Language-model text.** Every floor is stated so it would bind such text, but no classifier is designed here (dossier 22).

## 12. Testing agenda

1. **Floor fuzz and fault-injection** (M18-01, M18-02): the cheapest, and the one that must be zero before anything ships.
2. **The settings matrix** (M18-03, M18-04) on three reference seeds, as soon as S2 sowing runs.
3. **The first population audit** (M18-06 to M18-10) on the first S1 population, to set thresholds from data and not from my guesses.
4. **A retract fixture** (M18-11) in the harness, with a played R1 seed.
5. **A blind panel read** (REP-07) of fifty generated descriptions, to find out whether the lexicon screen predicts the panel.
6. **Lint calibration** (M18-16) once the vanilla text set exists.
