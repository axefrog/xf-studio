## In brief

- **People should be a pure function of a block, an index and the world seed, and so should most of their ties.** The kernel already makes members of a place lazily addressable by `H("gen", owner, slot, index)`. Residents can therefore be `(block, i)`, with attributes drawn from a joint table that was fitted offline to the district's field values. Ties follow the same pattern: whether `a` and `b` are tied is a symmetric keyed draw on the pair, with the odds set by shared foci (home, workplace, stall row, crew) and by a rank-based neighbourhood term. Only ties V observes, hears of, or a seed uses become edges. This keeps a city of residents out of the retained set, and makes the same stall hold the same vendor with the same neighbours after any reload. One kernel addition is useful for casting through ties (S15-S0-01).
- **The stored state of a norm is a gap: what people privately support against what they believe others support.** Compliance depends on two expectations (what others do, what others think one should do). A district can hold a norm most of its members privately reject, and one public act can end it in a step, while the same act done privately changes nothing. The dossier gives the norm record, the three slots per district and norm, the rule that separates a public from a private act, and the conditions under which an unpopular norm can enforce itself. In the one model I opened in full, those conditions were local visibility and *clustered* enforcers; bridging ties made false enforcement harder, not easier.
- **Reputation is not a score. It is the aggregate of an audience's own beliefs, per axis, judged through the audience's own view of the target.** The kernel's `aggregate` hook gets a concrete policy: independent evidence counted once, hearsay discounted by the teller, forgetting by *displacement* (older evidence is discounted per newer independent item, never by the clock, which agrees with dossier 11's rule that standing does not decay by absence), and a verdict rule that scores an act against a target the audience already holds in bad standing differently from one against a target it respects. Two audiences must be able to read the same acts differently.
- **Order is supplied, not assumed.** Each district has a protection gap (distrust minus state supply), and whoever can credibly threaten fills it. A gang's order has a regime: how it stands to the state (collude or confront), to the community (cooperate or abuse) and to rivals (monopoly or contest). One study I read only in summary found the state and gang rule can be *complements*: gangs nearer to state presence built more governance where drug rents were high. So "more police" cannot be a simple dial that lowers gang power. The engine gets a regime label per district and group, because policing interventions have regime-dependent effects.
- **Trust between a principal and an agent is three levers: watch, reward, bond.** A fixer, a gang officer and a corporate handler all delegate. Each contract has a monitoring strength, a pay structure and a bond (collateral, or a hostage such as a secret the other side holds). Betrayal is a hazard on *gain minus (detection times sanction plus continuation value plus bond)*, and it clusters where the shadow of the future is shortest: the last job, the outside contractor, the cornered agent. It is the rare tail of a distribution whose body is delay, shirking and quiet exit.
- **Defection is mostly exit, and exit is mostly quiet.** Where studies of gang leavers exist, most exits were non-hostile, push-driven and slow, with ties lingering. Informing and betrayal are the tail. A cross-pressure the kernel detects as a dilemma is far more likely to produce a stall, a fake compliance or a warning than a clean betrayal. A betrayal that does happen must carry its recorded causes (the dilemma, the offer, the threat), or it reads as a trope. A small budget caps how many allies turn in a campaign.
- **Nothing here needs a new theory of the engine.** The kernel already has the shapes: members, edges, beliefs with independent evidence, `knows`, dilemmas. What dossier 15 adds is S1 policy, one optional kernel mechanism (implicit edge types), one clarification, three fixtures, and a list of predicates for S2. The evidence is for mechanisms and signs, not for numbers: nearly all of it comes from real cities, prisons and one pirate era, and gives no constant for a fictional city (§2.3).

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

Layer 1 must say who the people of Night City are, how they hold together, and what keeps (or breaks) order among them, at the cost of a city that is mostly unobserved. [Dossier 14](d14-city-events.md) says *what happens and where*. [Dossier 1](d01-motivation-drives.md) says what people and groups *want*. [Dossier 2](d02-relationships.md) says what a *tie* is made of. [Dossier 11](d11-saves-memory-forgetting.md) says how beliefs *fade and survive a reload*. This dossier asks: *who exists at each tier and how are they generated from fields; how is the social graph sampled without storing it; what norms hold a district and how do they tip; what is reputation and how is it aggregated; how do gangs, fixers, corporations and police keep or lose order; how does trust work between a principal and an agent; and when does a membership, under cross-pressure, end in defection or betrayal?*

### 2.2 What they have in common

Three things recur. First, **a measured or modelled stand-in replaced the thing** (F-5, F-6, F-10): police-found ties for ties, a stereotype for a motive, an incentive model for a person. Second, **depth with no surface** (F-1, F-2, F-3, F-11): a state that nothing the player can see reflects, or a twist with no path. Third, **one dial for a plural thing** (F-3, F-8, F-9). The design below answers each: the tie, norm and reputation functions are the world's own; every one has a street cue and a recorded cause; and each plural thing (audience, axis, tie kind, protection kind) is kept plural.

### 2.3 Where the advice itself fails

- **Almost every evidence base is a real, non-fiction setting at one scale.** Sicily, prison systems, Medellín, Rio, Chicago, a US campus, a university e-mail network, an online journal community, eleventh-century traders, eighteenth-century pirates. They give shapes, signs and mechanisms. None gives a constant for a city with chrome, corporate sovereignty and a vertical plan. All constants here are [J].
- **Reputation theory was built for small, repeated interactions.** The Maghribi coalition and the pirate crew were tens to hundreds of people. A city of millions needs the carrier-based form of REP-01 or the theory does not scale at all.
- **The group-size numbers are extrapolations.** The layer sizes of about 5, 15, 50 and 150 and the ratio near 3 are results from a few datasets (Zhou and colleagues, lens, summary); 150 itself is the broader social-brain figure, not a fitted layer, as L7 already notes. Stiller and Dunbar (summary) tie two inner layers to different abilities; I use the layers as budgets, not as laws.
- **Norm models assume homogeneous, memoryless agents** on lattices of eight neighbours. They show *what is possible*, not what a given district does. The "less is more" result is a property of that model; the lens states it as "more zealots made cascades less likely", which is not quite what the paper says (§3.4).
- **Game-theoretic reputation results are contested.** Image scoring is simple and has been shown not to be evolutionarily stable in some analyses; "standing" does better there; human experiments have been read as consistent with image scoring (Leimar and Hammerstein; Milinski and colleagues; summaries). The engine takes the *sign* (judge through the target's standing when you hold one) and leaves the rule a pack parameter.
- **Criminology is built on samples of the caught and the willing to talk.** Gambetta, Skarbek, Varese and Decker rely on trials, indictments, confessions and interviews; the Chicago networks rely on police records.
- **Agency theory's own critics** say it explains incentives and not norms, and that its "hostage" arguments rest on contested game-theoretic points (a reader's critique of Williamson, summary). It is a source of three levers, not a theory of why people keep faith.
- **Protection-and-governance claims are partly contested:** a reader of Gambetta objects to a libertarian reading; a reader of Skarbek says race is underplayed; Edwards and Ogilvie challenge Greif's account of the Maghribi coalition (summaries).
- **A leader-removal effect from one country.** The Mexican beheading study (Calderón and colleagues, summary) is one country, one period. I saw one replication listed and did not read it.
- **Population synthesis assumes a census to fit.** A fictional city has none; the targets are the world's field values, so field errors flow into people (§4 A).
- **Life-course regularities are contested:** the age-crime hump is disputed by invariance arguments, and turning-point effects are mixed (L7; Moffitt; Sampson and Laub; Hirschi and Gottfredson, lens). Night City may not have these life stages.

## 4. Principles

Grades are P1 §4's. "Produce" says how a procedure could make the principle true; "Check" says how a procedure could test it. Layers are the plan's numbers (1 simulation, 2 seeding, 3 procedural engine). Scales: *enc* (encounter), *gig*, *arc*, *city*. **[J]** marks a starting value to be replaced by a measurement (§7). Principle IDs are `<area>-NN`. Instances are invented for this dossier unless a source or a game fact is named; none is content. For each principle that touches groups or perspectives, "the model gives" says what the engine's own concepts already provide.

## 5. Models the engine could run

All of this is S1 policy over S0's mechanisms. Constants are **[J]**. Every decision uses only `+ − × ÷`, `sqrt`, comparisons and `ln` ([S0 §1.3](../spec/kernel.md#13-arithmetic)); where a smooth curve is wanted the shape is a rational function or a table. Draws are keyed `rand(key…)`; there is no stateful generator.

### 5.2 The population recipe and the residents

**The recipe.** For each district the engine holds a *joint table* over a small set of attributes, such as age band (8), role (12), income band (4) and affiliation class (6): 2,304 cells, below `N_enum` (4096). The table is fitted by iterative proportional fitting. Start from a seed table (a pack's prior, or flat); then, for each marginal in turn, scale every cell by `target ÷ current` for that marginal's value; repeat for a fixed 20 iterations. The marginals come from the world index (W1): age structure, role and income shares, and the district's affiliation shares ([population §5 and §6](../../../knowledge/population-and-affiliations.md)). A floor on every cell stops zero cells (POP-02). The fit costs about `2,304 × 4 × 20 ≈ 184,000` operations per district per refit, which the process budget counts.

**Households.** The same style of table serves the household: a joint over household type (single, couple, family with dependants, shared flat, crew house), tenure and wealth band. A household is drawn first and its members are drawn under constraints on ages, kin edges and headcount. Dependants are a count; a pack kind opens one when a story needs it (POP-03). A fit that controls person and household attributes together uses the hierarchical form (Müller and Axhausen); the first cut can fit the household table and the person table separately and check the match in M15-02.

**The resident.** Block `b` has a `residents` slot whose count derives from the block's headcount. Resident `i` is `H("gen", b, "residents", i)`. Its slots are drawn by the flat Gumbel-max ([S0 §5.4](../spec/kernel.md#54-the-draw-flat-gumbel-max)) over the cells of the block's table; its household is a member of a `households` slot whose size and composition the household draw sets. Name, appearance and voice are other bundles ([foundation §1](../foundation.md#1-the-idea)); the game's casting type fixes the body class.

**What the game does and does not give.** A character record says faction, attitude group, kind, permanence and template path, but not gender, age, job, personality or relationships ([population §7](../../../knowledge/population-and-affiliations.md#7-what-a-character-record-tells-us)). The engine's resident supplies everything the game leaves out, and a mirrored character (a fixer, a quest NPC) is a pin that overrides the draw.

**Binding to a body (POP-07).** The game re-rolls street population on sector load. Binding is therefore by key: `(community instance, entry, spot, ordinal)` selects a resident of the block whose casting type fits, by a keyed draw on that tuple. A resident bound to a body is pinned `observed` once V notices it; unobserved bodies are never identified.

### 5.3 Lifetimes as keyed spells

A person's life is a set of overlapping *spell tracks*: residence, work, partner, health. Each track is a renewal sequence. The length of spell `k` is drawn from a keyed inverse-cumulative *table* (no `exp`): `len_k = table[track, ageBand](rand("spell", person, track, k))`. The state at time `t` is found by walking spells from the person's anchor to `t`. With spells of weeks to years the walk is a dozen steps at most, and it is cached by revision and `t` ([S0 §5.5](../spec/kernel.md#55-caching)). The lifetime is one draw, `rand("life", person)`, through a life table; a story event (a killing) pins a death over it. Because every value is a pure function of keys and `t`, a 20-day wait costs nothing (dossier 11 ABS-02) and a reload cannot change a spell (M15-03).

Two cautions. **Aging.** The kernel's `age` is a sampled integer and does not advance with `t`. Across a playthrough of months this rarely matters; if it does, a derived `age-at(t)` slot replaces it (open question 4). **Population flow.** Arrivals and departures are spells too: a block's headcount field changes slowly, and the people whose residence spell ends or starts are those whose keyed boundaries fall in the interval.

### 5.4 The implicit tie function and its foci

A tie is a symmetric keyed draw on a pair. For `a` and `b`:

```
p_geo(a→b) = (1 − f_far) × c / (H_n × rank_a(b))      rank by walking-order cumulative headcount
p_geo      = ½ (p_geo(a→b) + p_geo(b→a))               made symmetric
p_foci     = 1 − Π over shared foci f of (1 − p_f),   p_f = min(1, k_f / (n_f − 1))
p          = 1 − (1 − p_geo)(1 − p_foci)(1 − p_far)
tie(a, b)  = rand("tie", min(a,b), max(a,b)) < p
```

- **Foci** are rosters the engine already has: a household, a `core:member-of` group, a workplace or a market row (a `core:place` with a workforce), a clinic, a bar. `k_f` is a table by focus type: a household gives near-certainty, a crew cell about 0.3 of the cell, a market row a few neighbours (NET-03).
- **Geography** is by rank, using cumulative resident counts of blocks ordered by walking time from `a`, so dense and sparse blocks give comparable reach (NET-02).
- **Far ties** (`p_far`, about a third of ties) need a symmetric enumeration. A keyed involution on the city's resident index space gives one: `partner_j(a) = σ_j⁻¹(σ_j(a) xor 1)`, where `σ_j` is a keyed pseudo-random permutation of the index space (a small Feistel network with cycle-walking, a standard construction for permutations on arbitrary ranges [mem: Black and Rogaway]). Both ends see the same partner, and each person has exactly `J` far partners, `J` scaled by `sociability`. The simpler first cut has no far ties and lets city-wide foci (origin, scene, a famous bar) supply the bridges.
- **Layers** follow the kind of the strongest shared focus: household is inner, crew and workplace are middle, neighbours and far ties outer. Budgets of about 5, 15, 50 and 150 (NET-04) cap how many ties are *opened* for one person, not how many exist.
- **`neighbours(a, layer, cap)`** enumerates a's foci rosters (each capped), then blocks outward by rank until the expected count is met, testing each candidate with the pair function. The cost is counted in the shared allowance ([S0 §10.7](../spec/kernel.md#107-work-hard-limits-and-worklists)).

**When a tie becomes an edge.** V observes it, a belief about it is formed or told, a goal frame uses it, or a seed casts through it. The edge is a `core:knows` or a pack tie edge with cause `sampled` and the pair as its key. Un-ended edges are retained roots ([S0 §7.3](../spec/kernel.md#73-retention-and-forgetting)), so the engine keeps a per-person cap on materialised ties (about 20, the inner two layers).

**Casting through ties.** A query that asks for "someone who knows X" must test the implicit relation. Without a kernel change the engine can only test materialised edges, so casting sees a tie only once someone has opened it. S15-S0-01 proposes implicit edge types (§11.2).

### 5.5 Position, brokerage and the fixer's offers

For a person `p` with contact mass spread over foci `f`, shares `s_f` summing to 1:

```
constraint(p) = Σ_f s_f²              (1 when all contacts sit in one focus)
bridge(p)     = 1 − constraint(p)     (near 0 inside one cluster, large when spanning several)
```

This is a Herfindahl form in the spirit of Burt's constraint, computed from the foci rosters with no graph search. A **fixer** is a person (usually a pack character or a mirrored game fixer) with high `bridge` across at least three clusters of different class (a gang, a corporation, residents, police). Their offer set is the pool of available jobs, filtered and ranked by:
1. interest alignment: a job that helps another of their clients scores up;
2. owed favours and open obligations;
3. position: access to the job's counterpart through their foci;
4. what they withhold (every offer states one withheld fact, discoverable on inspection, per NET-06 and [dossier 3](d03-fitting-casting.md)).

Two fixers given the same pool therefore differ in what they show. The mirrored game fixers are pins: the engine does not re-author them, but it can model *why* an authored fixer's gigs cluster in a district, from the same foci.

### 5.6 The norm record and the district's norm state

**A norm record** (pack data):

```
norm { id, behaviour: selector on acts or effects, sector, enforcers: group selectors,
       thresholds: { empirical: hist8, normative: hist8 }, cost K,
       ladder: [ { rung, who, sanction, public: bool } ], domain: 'district' | 'sector' | 'group' }
```

Example norms, all invented here and all pack data: *do not talk to the uniforms*; *pay the Friday fee*; *no fighting in the market*; *neutral ground at the bar*; *do not shop at a rival's stall*.

**State per district and norm**, three slow slots: `priv` (the share who privately support), `beh` (the share who comply) and `perc` (the typical resident's belief about `beh`: a perspective on the district, not world truth). Each period `order.norms` runs a fixed four iterations:

1. `q_e = (1 − φ) × local(beh) + φ × signal`. `local` blends block-level behaviour for embedded districts (embeddedness κ, NRM-03) with the district's `beh`; `signal` is the last public event's implication; `φ` is the public-signal weight (**[J] 0.1**, jumping to **0.6** in the window of a public event).
2. `q_n` from `perc` and the visibility of the enforcers.
3. `beh` = the share of resident types whose thresholds are met, summed over the 8-bin histograms: `Σ share_bin × [q_e ≥ τ_e(bin)] × [q_n ≥ τ_n(bin)]`.
4. `perc ← perc + η (q_e − perc)`, with `η` about 0.3.

The state changes by *period and event*, not by the clock. A private act updates only its witnesses' beliefs. A **public** act (place `public`, `witnesses ≥ 5`, and the event's own flag that onlookers saw each other) sets `φ` high for one window. That is the whole of NRM-04.

**Enforcement.** The enforcer set draws part of itself from compliers with low `priv` (NRM-05). Each rung's use is a minted event (a word, a share withheld, an exclusion), so every enforcement leaves a cause. Metanorm enforcement raises compliance and shortens recovery, and is brittle to a public shock (an M15-12 test).

**Tipping.** A norm moves toward its high-compliance state when embeddedness κ and enforcer clustering are both above a threshold **[J]**, and cannot be reached by dispersed enforcers however many there are (NRM-03). The reverse transition, collapse, follows a public event that raises `perc` toward `priv`.

### 5.7 Efficacy, cynicism and the report chance

Per district, two stocks, updated by retention and gains (no clock decay):

```
E ← ρ_E × E + gains(visible remedy, stable ties) − losses(turnover, a failed remedy, rule by fear)
Y ← ρ_Y × Y + gains(police failure seen by many, unfair procedure) − losses(visible fair remedy)
reportChance = r0 × (1 − Y) × (0.5 + 0.5 E) × (1 − fear) × (1 − noSnitch) × (1 + 0.1 × witnessBonus)
```

`ρ` is a per-period retention constant near 0.99 for cynicism (it persists, NRM-07) and 0.98 for efficacy. `fear` is the retaliation fear from the local gang's presence and its sought-versus-imposed mix (ORD-02). `noSnitch` is the `beh` of the norm "do not talk to the uniforms". Dossier 14 owns the *report draw* (S14-S1-13); this is the policy it calls. A fair, visible remedy (a gang or the police acting fairly and in public) lowers cynicism; a seen failure raises it; both are events.

### 5.8 Reputation: the ledger and `aggregate`

**The ledger** is the set of evidence items about a subject, kept per (subject, axis). An item `e` has: the originating event, the axis valences (a signed value on each axis it bears on), the salience rated at formation (dossier 11), the carrier (who knows it: a group, a district, an ego-net), the channel confidence, and the teller if it is hearsay. Items per (subject, axis, carrier) are capped at 32; the cap keeps the memorable and the most diagnostic.

**The verdict of an act** is `v_e = judge(act, audienceViewOfTarget) × diagnosticity`, where `judge` is the table of REP-03 and `diagnosticity = clamp(−ln p_district(act kind))` (REP-06). The audience's view of the target is the audience's own belief (REP-03).

**The aggregate**, for audience `A`, subject `s`, axis `a`:

```
w_e   = salience_e × channel_e × tellerDiscount_e × reach_A(e)
for each item e in order of (time, Id):
    scale = 1 − reach_A(e) × (1 − λ)              // expected displacement of older items
    r, s  = r × scale, s × scale
    r += w_e × max(0,  v_e);   s += w_e × max(0, −v_e)
E     = (r + 1) / (r + s + 2);   mass = r + s
label = 'little known' if mass < m0, else band(E)
```

Reading it: `reach_A(e)` is dossier 14's reach for the item in the audience (the fraction who know it), so the aggregate estimates the audience's mean belief without visiting residents. A resident's own belief is the same sum over only the items they know (`rand("knows", resident, item) < reach`), so the two agree in expectation (M15-18). `tellerDiscount` is the teller's own `E` on the same axis. The expected value and mass are the standard beta form (Jøsang and Ismail's structure; I did not check the equations against the paper). `λ` is **[J] 0.9**, `m0` is **[J] 2**, and the label bands are `E ≥ 0.7`, `0.4 to 0.7` and `< 0.4`.

**A worked trace** (axis `reliable`, audience Kabuki stallholders; reach taken as 1 for every item, so the weights below are already final). The label survives an absence and yields to independent evidence:

| Step | Item | r | s | E | Mass | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delivered a parcel (+0.5) | 0.50 | 0 | 0.60 | 0.5 | little known |
| 2 | Kept a second promise (+0.5) | 0.95 | 0 | 0.66 | 0.95 | little known |
| 3 | Missed a deadline, seen by two (−1.2) | 0.86 | 1.20 | 0.46 | 2.06 | mixed |
| | 30 game days with no events | 0.86 | 1.20 | 0.46 | 2.06 | mixed (unchanged) |
| 4 | A second independent report of a miss (−0.8) | 0.77 | 1.88 | 0.38 | 2.65 | unreliable |
| 5 | A third (−0.8) | 0.69 | 2.49 | 0.33 | 3.18 | unreliable |

In the engine none of this is shown. The street's reaction (prices, who greets, who lends) is the cue (dossier 10 VOB-02).

### 5.9 Telling, blacklists and testimony

`onSocial(event, teller, listener)` picks what the teller tells, the chance of telling, and what changes in transit ([dossier 11](d11-saves-memory-forgetting.md) S11-S0-07 owns the transformation). This dossier supplies the *selection*:

```
score(item) = protectiveRelevance(item, listener's frames) × severity(item) × closeness(tie)
p_tell      = base × max over items of score
```

`protectiveRelevance` uses the kernel's `threatenedBy` and `protects` matches on the listener's frames, which is the engine's own relevance concept; the gossip studies are a comparison. A lie is an item whose content differs from the world and whose teller knows it; it keeps the teller's `evidence` lineage and is down-weighted when independent evidence contradicts it. A **blacklist** is a high-severity, high-reach item told across the allied set of a group (AGT-04). It lowers `reliable` and `discreet` for every audience in the set, and the event is minted so the street can see it.

### 5.10 The protection market and regimes

**The market.** For district `d` and sector `k` (stalls, bars and clubs, net services, the drug trade, transport):

```
D = T_k × (1 − trust_k)                         demand: transactions needing enforcement times distrust
S = presence × responsiveness × legitimacy      state supply (dossier 14 police pressure; ORD-08)
G = max(0, D − S)                               the protection gap
share_g ∝ strength_g × presence_g(d, k)         suppliers' shares (dossier 14 strength stocks)
income_g = rate_g × share_g × T_k × protected
```

`T_k` comes from the world index (entry quantity, crowd density by period; [dossier 14](d14-city-events.md) OPP-03). `trust_k` is the complement of cynicism for the sector. The effect of more policing on governance carries a **pack-set sign by rent level** (ORD-01).

**Payment edges.** `xf:protection` (payer to group): `kind` sought or imposed, `rate`, `since`, `last`. At coarse tiers these are counts per block; opened, they are edges.

**Regimes.** A label per (district, group) from three flags. The source names five regimes; the table is *my* reading of the three axes it names, not its definitions.

| State | Community | Rivals | What V sees | What stories find in it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colludes | Cooperates | Monopoly | Quiet blocks; police look away; locals defend the gang to outsiders | A crackdown that hurts residents; a clean officer who disrupts a working order |
| Colludes | Abuses | Monopoly | Fear, tolls, silence; police absent when called | Exposure; a protector who turns on the block |
| Confronts | Cooperates | Monopoly | Barricades, lookouts, locals who hide gang members | A raid that turns a street against the police |
| Confronts | Abuses | Contested | Violence, cut-throat extortion, no trust in anyone | Everyone wants out; the bidding war for protection |
| Colludes | Cooperates | Contested | Truces, brokered by a fixer or an elder | A broken truce; an arbiter's price |
| Confronts | Abuses | Monopoly | A predator with a free hand | Slow collapse from within (DEF-03) |

A transition is an event with a cause (a rival's entry, a sweep, a legitimacy collapse, a leader's death). It ends one regime and starts another ([dossier 14](d14-city-events.md) S14-S1-14).

### 5.11 How each kind of group keeps order

| Order kind | Who sanctions | Record | Source of legitimacy | How it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gang cell | Cell lead, then the parent (ORD-04) | A ledger when the group seeks legitimacy (ORD-05) | Services residents value; a fair rule | A leader removed (ORD-07); legitimacy collapse; a defection cascade (DEF-03) |
| Collective or clan | Elected officers, a vote or a council; articles in `terms` (ORD-05) | Shared and public | Consent and shared risk | A split; capture by a faction |

Each kind uses the group drive catalogue of [dossier 1 §11](d01-motivation-drives.md) (`legitimacy`, `cohesion`, `secrecy`, `compliance`, `succession`). The sanction ladders are pack data per kind.

**Succession and removal (ORD-07).** On a leader's removal the group's `discipline` slot is set low for `N` windows **[J: 12 periods]**, its `succession` drive is raised, and rivals see an opening: dossier 14's pair tension rises by a table step. After `N` windows discipline recovers if a successor has been accepted. Otherwise the group splits into two groups (an event with cause), each inheriting memberships by strength.

### 5.12 Contracts, hazards and the fixer chain

A **contract record**: principal, agent, task, pay (`fixed`, `share`, `bonus`), monitoring `m_p` (what the principal can see and how fast), `bond` (collateral, hostage, information), sanction ladder, expected repeats, visibility (open or deniable). It is a `core:member-of` edge with `terms: contract` and `until`, plus data.

**The hazard per window** (opened or storied contracts):

```
G        = skim + bribe + protection of kin             the gain from deviating
P_detect = m_p + (1 − m_p) × cover                      cover = overlap of the agent's and principal's foci
L        = P_detect × sanction + continuation + bond    continuation = expected future rents × shadow
x        = max(0, G − L)
h        = h_max × x / (x + κ)                          saturating; no exp
```

**Two cases** (abstract units; all **[J]**; `h_max` 0.3, `κ` 0.5):

| | G | m_p | cover | P_detect | sanction | continuation | bond | L | x | h per window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A long-run runner for a fixer, many repeats | 1.6 | 0.2 | 0.5 | 0.60 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 0.5 | 4.70 | 0 | 0 |
| The same runner on a one-off job for an outsider, nothing to lose after it | 1.6 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.28 | 1.0 | 0 | 0.5 | 0.78 | 0.82 | 0.19 |

The shadow of the future is why the second row turns and the first does not.

**Unopened contracts** are counted. Skims, deviations and betrayals per group and window become summary counts of dossier 14's kind, and their items resolve lazily when V looks.

**A fixer's two contracts.** A job brokered through a fixer is two contracts: client to fixer (outcome, plus the fixer's standing on `reliable`) and fixer to runner (a cut, plus the fixer's standing on `discreet`). The fixer's own hazard uses her continuation value, which is large while she keeps a flow of clients (AGT-03). The runner's grievance if she sells him out carries the betrayal multiplier (AGT-06).

**Bonds.** `bond_B(A)` is the sum over A's beliefs about B that would hurt B if told, times audience reach; it counts in B's `L` (AGT-05). A mutual hostage lowers both hazards. When one hostage is exposed, the other's bond is released and that hazard rises.

### 5.13 Defection and exit

When the kernel emits a `Dilemma` for member `m` (an order from a group violates a protected interest in another frame), the member's options are drawn from a prior and then modulated:

| Option | Prior weight **[J]** | Raised by | Lowered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delay | 0.30 | Ambiguity of the order; absent monitoring | Tight monitoring; a deadline |
| Avoid or be absent | 0.20 | Distance from the cell | Exit cost; being watched |
| Fake compliance | 0.20 | Weak monitoring; `deniable` visibility | Detection chance |
| Warn the target | 0.12 | Closeness of the tie at risk | Fear of the cell |
| Comply | 0.10 | Fear; identification | Closeness of the tie at risk |
| Refuse openly | 0.04 | Status; allies in view | Fear |
| Betray the group (turn) | 0.02 | The hazard `h` of §5.12; an offer; a visible leadership failure | Bond; exit cost; the turn budget (DEF-06) |
| Run or leave | 0.02 | `strength` below threshold; push motives | Exit cost; kin tied to the group |

The weights are renormalised after modulation. Exit is a staged state (withdraw, leave, residual ties), and `strength` falls first, by [dossier 1](d01-motivation-drives.md) DRV-38's rule.

**Group-wide cascade.** Per group, a keyed histogram of defection thresholds `θ_m` (8 bins). Each period `v` is the share of members visible as discontented to each other (ties inside the cell). Members defect when `v ≥ θ_m`. A public failure of the leadership adds `δ` to `v` for one window (DEF-03). A fixed number of iterations settles it. A group whose members cannot see one another (secret cells, NET-07) cascades slowly, which is the price of that safety.

**The informant** (DEF-04) is a contract with the police as principal, a bond (an arrest, a kin tie) and a leak process. When a belief held by a set `H` surfaces where it should not, the group compares `evidence` lineage with `H` and suspects the members who held it.

### 5.14 The reload, partition and arithmetic contract

- **Keys.** `("proc", processId, window, scope, kind, role, index)` as in dossier 14, plus `("res", block, i)`, `("tie", min, max)`, `("spell", person, track, k)`, `("life", person)`, `("knows", resident, item)`, `("contract", id, window)`, `("defect", member, window)`.
- **Order.** Evidence is processed by `(time, Id)`. Contract draws are in contract `Id` order. Loops are fixed-order.
- **State.** `ProcessState` holds the norm slots, stocks, regimes, ledgers and contract states, with each process's outstanding batch ([S0 §9.4](../spec/kernel.md#94-evolution)). The recipe tables are state too, versioned with their function versions.
- **Partition.** A process's history does not depend on how ticks fell (I-15). The ledger gist is a function of items in `(time, Id)` order, so it is partition-independent too.

### 5.15 Cost

The expected per-period cost is dominated by the recipe refit (rare), the norm iterations (small: eight bins, four iterations, per district and live norm), the ledger gist step (carriers, not residents) and the contract hazards (opened or storied contracts only, and counts for the rest). A `neighbours` call costs at most the sum of its foci rosters' caps plus a bounded geographic walk, all inside the shared allowance. I did not measure any of this; M15-28 does.

## 8. Tensions

- **Lazy ties against story that needs a particular tie.** A casting query that needs "someone who knows X" cannot see an unopened tie. Either the kernel gains implicit edge types (S15-S0-01) or casting opens a bounded set of candidates first. The second costs allowance and weakens "find the best".
- **Symmetric ties against enumeration cost.** The far-tie involution gives exact enumeration at the price of a permutation scheme the team must implement and test. The no-far-ties first cut is simpler and loses the bridges that rumours and brokers need unless foci supply them.
- **Realistic reputation against legible reaction.** Per-audience, per-axis reputation is richer than the one meter players read. The answer is cues (prices, greetings, who lends), never a number (VOB-02), and the labels are for the engine's use.
- **Absence-proof labels against changing people.** Displacement forgetting means a label survives a long absence; it also means a V who changes course is re-judged only when independent evidence arrives. That is the intended behaviour (CTY-06) and it makes redemption slow. Dossier 10's VOB-03 gives the other half.
- **Betrayal as drama against betrayal as budget.** The best twists are betrayals, and a budget of turns caps them. DEF-06 sets an honest default and a cap per cast; the director (dossier 7) may want a way to ask for a turn. It should *ask the cast's own hazard to be raised by a cause*, not force one.
- **Norm models against districts.** The eight-neighbour lattice is a toy. The engine uses its two parameters (embeddedness and clustering) as dials on a district; that is a model of a district, not a district.
- **State-gang complement against the player's instinct.** If more policing can raise gang governance in some districts, a player who "cleans up" a district sees a result that reads as perverse. The regime label and the reputation of the police are the explanation; the story should make the cause visible.
- **Group intent against internal splits.** Players attribute one intent to a group (MND-03); the engine's groups have leaders who decide and members who diverge. Show the single intent in scenes and the split through individuals.
- **Protecting allies against realistic betrayal.** A protected set (dossier 14 TIE-09) blocks harm to live seeds' casts; it also blocks turns. Seeds that *want* a turn must declare it.
- **Population from fields against stereotype.** A fitted table can make Heywood's residents look like Heywood's tags. POP-06's lint and M15-27's audit are the guard; dossier 18 owns the policy.
- **Simplicity against completeness.** Eight processes and many pack kinds are more than a first cut needs (§11.1 S15-S1-28 gives an order). The finish-before-widening instinct applies.
- **Real-world sociology against a fictional city.** Every constant is [J]. The panel measure P15-01 and the in-game session are the only ways to tune them.

## 9. Lateral links

- **A norm's `perc` is a belief about a district's `beh`.** The "false belief about others' beliefs" of the norms literature is the engine's ordinary belief record with a district as its subject. No new mechanism: a perspective already is a pin in the believer.
- **Reputation is `aggregate` over carriers; a resident's belief is `knows` over items.** They are two readings of one ledger, kept consistent by dossier 14's reach and the same keyed uniform.
- **The tie function and the kernel's draw are the same coupling.** Noise depends only on what is being asked (the pair), so changing a weight changes an answer only where it matters ([S0 §5.4](../spec/kernel.md#54-the-draw-flat-gumbel-max); dossier 14 §9).
- **Forgetting by displacement is dossier 11's accessibility, applied to the gist.** Detail fades by `uses` and time; the gist yields to evidence.
- **A contract is a membership with `terms: contract`.** Foundation §5.2's deniable job (a gang taking a job for one corporation against another) is AGT-08.
- **A hostage is a belief and an `expose` effect.** The kernel's effect vocabulary and belief record already express it.
- **The protection gap is routine activity theory's guardian, seen from the market side.** [Dossier 14](d14-city-events.md) OPP-01 places and times acts; ORD-01 says who supplies guardianship and for how much.
- **A fixer's clients are a roster, and so is a gang's cell.** `bridge(p)` is one formula for both.
- **Exit, voice and loyalty** (Hirschman; dossier 1 DRV-38) meet the cascade: voice is a signal that raises others' visible discontent, so voice is not only a cost to the voicer but a risk to the group.
- **Divergence between belief and world** (NAR-20) is the same quantity as dossier 14's "why now": a cause chain ends at a gap somebody holds.

## 10. Open questions

1. **How many blocks, and how big?** Every count here is per block. The block unit (a level-0 streaming cell of about 219 m, per dossier 14 open question 3) and the number of residents per block are unknown.
2. **What do mirrored characters do to the draw?** A game-placed vendor is a pin. Whether the engine should also assign her a household and ties (and with whom) is a content question for P6 and dossier 20.
3. **Is enumerating far ties from one side good enough?** If the first cut has none, bridges come from foci; if it has some, the involution scheme needs a check against reload stability and cost.
4. **Does age need to advance?** The kernel's `age` is fixed per person. A game that spans in-game years would need a derived `age-at(t)`.
5. **How should the engine score an act against a target no audience has a view of?** REP-03 falls back to image scoring. A district's prior on the target's kind may be better.
6. **How many norms are live per district?** Eight bins, four iterations and a handful of norms is cheap; fifty is not.
7. **Do the regime labels need five, or three flags?** I mapped three axes; the source's five named regimes may carve the space differently.
8. **What is the right betrayal budget?** A cap per cast and per campaign window is a guess. The panel (P15-01) and an in-game session are the way to find out.
9. **Who authors the role table, the norms and the sanction ladders?** Pack authors need defaults and a tool (P10).
10. **How does the engine treat the game's own dynamic events and gigs as evidence?** Gigs V completes for a fixer are evidence on `reliable` in the fixer's audience. The reading of the game's gig outcomes is dossier 14's mirror (S14-S1-20).
11. **Can the player see why a fixer shows one gig and not another?** The withheld fact (NET-06) is a cue; a "why" on request is dossier 20's.
12. **Should the first cut's reputation have more than two audiences per subject?** The engine needs the district and the nearest group; a third (the subject's own group) may be enough for V.
