## In brief

- **Agency is a perception, and it is cheap to fake.** In small studies, immediate acknowledgement of a choice kept most of the felt agency of a branching story, and a magician's forced choice gave a strong feeling of free choice that survived repetition. So the engine cannot treat player-reported agency as its test. It needs structural tests (does each option leave a different record? does the world differ if V declines?) and a firm line between *acknowledging* an act, which is always done, and *deciding the outcome regardless*, which is never done (quality bar 4).
- **Declining is a legal act with its own consequence, and the consequence is the offer's stake resolving without V.** The client's goal does not vanish when V says no. It stays blocked and the client's own drives carry on: they find another way, give up, or are hurt. That needs no punishment mechanism, only the continuing frames the foundation already has. The craft says the world moves in two steps, a visible portent first and the hard step only after V has had a chance to answer (soft move, hard move). The research on reactance and controlling framing says why punishment is the wrong tool.
- **A consequence V never perceived gives V no agency.** The keystone record is an *exposure ledger*: what V was actually shown, as well as what happened. It decides three things: whether an offer was declined or simply not noticed, whether a hard step may fire, and whether a callback may cite an event.
- **Causal attribution needs a cue, and delay hurts it unless the delay was expected.** Lab studies find that causal judgements fall as cause and effect separate in time, and that the fall shrinks when people expect a delay. For hours or days of game time, the engine must plant the expectation at the moment of the act (an NPC who says they will be in touch) and name the act when the consequence lands.
- **Name the act, not the reason, and name it once.** People confabulate reasons for choices they did not make (choice blindness), and explanations are contrastive and selective (Miller's review). So a callback cites one recognisable act of V's, never V's motive or feeling (D-09), and the full causal chain stays in the trace.
- **Two kinds of response need two rules.** A *consequence* (V did X, so the world changed) is attributed and named where the cause is not otherwise visible. An *adaptation* (the engine believes something about V, so it chose which true offers to make) stays silent and is never dressed up as a consequence.
- **Tabletop practice gives the engine a small, usable set of moves:** situations before plots; "what the opposition does if V never turns up"; portents before hard steps; clocks as progress that is visible when V can act on it; three independent routes to every conclusion; a shared record for continuity; and adjudication in the open. The engine can do some of this better than a human (perfect memory, a full trace, no hidden fudging) and cannot do other parts at all (reading the table, live taste, improvised prose).
- **The evidence is thin where the claims are boldest.** I found no experiment that tests "name the act" against "do not name it" in a game, none on how long a consequence can be delayed before attribution fails, and none on the effect of punishing a decline. The principles that rest on those gaps are graded `[hypothesis]` and listed as open questions with a proposed test.

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

Quality bar 4 says: *V's choices change outcomes that the world remembers. Content never offers a choice and then decides the result regardless.* [D-09](../decisions.md) says V is observed, never owned, and that content offers rather than presumes. [FIT-49](d03-fitting-casting.md) already requires that every offer has a decline path. This dossier asks what makes agency real and what makes it look real, how a world should answer a "no", an ignored offer or a walk-away, how to show V that a later event was caused by an earlier act, and which parts of a tabletop game master's practice a procedural engine can adopt.

| Layer or concern | What this dossier gives it |
|---|---|
| 1. Simulation | The exposure ledger (what V perceived) as a first-class record; a decline as a recorded event; clocks as derived views |
| 2. Seeding | The offer record (client's blocked goal, stakes, null path, lapse form); promise and debt entries for announced consequences |
| 3. Procedural engine | Soft-before-hard gating on the ledger; the callback finder's attribution checks; the rules for declined, ignored and abandoned offers; steering through world state only |
| 4. Authored content | What an author must write beside each offer: the null path, the lapse form, the warning, the early cue for a delayed consequence, the callback hook |
| Player configuration | The proactivity dial's relation to consequences; a player-readable record of what the world has noted |
| Evaluation (E) | Fourteen measures (§7), including the decline audit and the two-policy replay |
| Expression (X) | Which words are forbidden (reproach, statements of V's feelings) and what a named-act line may say |

It does not cover the director's pacing, arbiter and budgets (dossier 7), relationship rules (dossier 2, though BND-15, BND-18, BND-19 and BND-26 are used), quest shape (dossier 4), or how the channels deliver a line (dossier 20 and P8).

## 4. Principles

Grades: **[empirical]**, **[practice]**, **[shipped]**, **[corpus]**, **[analysis]**, **[hypothesis]**; **contested** where sources disagree. Scales are encounter (E), gig (G) and arc (A). "Operationalise" says whether a procedure could **produce** the property, **check** it, or neither, and points at the layer and the kernel section where one applies. Comparisons with the engine's own concepts are comparisons only ([D-21](../decisions.md)).

### A. What agency is, and what fakes it (MOT-04, CRA-07)

**AGY-01. Felt control has four inputs: the intended action is supported, the outcome can be foreseen, the outcome is wanted, and the link from act to outcome is perceived.** `[analysis]` for the first; `[empirical]` for the other three (laboratory work, small samples).
- *Source:* Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009 (agency occurs when the actions players desire are among those they can take, as supported by an underlying model); Church 1999 (intention, and perceivable consequence: a clear reaction from the world, with the reason for failure visible); the "control heuristic" of Thompson and colleagues, whose four factors (foreseeability, ability, desirability, perceived connection) are described in Fendt et al. 2012 and Thue et al. 2011. I did not open Thompson.
- *Instance:* A gig that can be played as talk, sneak or fight (QST-24), where each route is visibly handled in its own way.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4, E.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** Each offer record carries four things a test can read: the supported actions (affordance predicates, QST-17), a statement of what the fiction tells V about where each option leads, the engine's belief about what V cares about (a belief only, D-09), and the link rule for consequences (AGY-33). A missing field is a lint failure. The first two are authorable and the third and fourth are the engine's.

**AGY-02. Options should lead to situations that differ, and V should be able to foresee that they differ.** `[empirical]`
- *Source:* Cardona-Rivera and colleagues 2014 (88 participants in a choose-your-own-adventure game reported more agency when options differed in situational content, including when they also differed in other ways); Mawhorter et al. 2014 (a *blind choice* gives no basis for expectation and can leave the player lost or frustrated; it is legitimate for exploration or a deliberate loss of bearings).
- *Instance:* Three ways into a building (the front desk, the service tunnel, the roof), each hinting at a different scene before V commits.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Each option's hint is generated from its predicted effects (the kernel's effect vocabulary, [S0 §10.8](../spec/kernel.md#108-effects-and-cross-pressure)). Flag option pairs whose predicted effects and cast are identical: by the engine's own measure that is a false choice (AGY-05). Allow a declared blind choice where the author marks it.

**AGY-03. Perceived agency is higher when the outcomes that follow are ones V wants. Use that to decide where consequences land and what is offered, never to shield V from bad outcomes.** `[empirical]`, one study; the second sentence is `[hypothesis]`.
- *Source:* Thue et al. 2011 (141 undergraduates in a Dragon Age toolset story; players on event sequences their model recommended rated agency higher than players on sequences it did not, 4.07 against 3.78 on a five-point scale, confidence 96.1 per cent; the adaptive-against-fixed contrast, 4.05 against 3.89, was 74.9 per cent, not significant; no effect on fun). The control heuristic says good outcomes raise felt control and bad ones lower it.
- *Instance:* A consequence of a past gig lands on a fixer V has dealt with several times, not on a stranger.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce, with guards.** The fitter weighs candidate consequences by the director's belief about what V has engaged with ([S0 §11.3](../spec/kernel.md#113-the-directors-beliefs-about-v)). Under [D-09](../decisions.md) that belief is never assigned and the offers must hold across Vs the engine cannot tell apart. The finding is about felt control, so the response to a bad outcome is to make it foreseeable and attributable (AGY-15, AGY-33), not to avoid it. A rule that picked pleasant outcomes would break quality bar 1.

**AGY-04. Acknowledge every act at once, in the fiction. Acknowledging is not deciding: never let an acknowledgement stand in for a consequence on a choice that matters.** `[empirical]`, small and short-range.
- *Source:* Fendt et al. 2012 (a text story with six two-way decisions, two of them true branch points; participants were recruited by snowball sampling and told it was a choose-your-own-adventure). A non-branching version with immediate textual acknowledgement matched the branching version on four of five story-level agency items. The branching version scored higher on "the story would have been different if I had chosen otherwise" (p below 0.05). The acknowledgement version beat a minimal-feedback version on only one item, so acknowledgement alone was not clearly better than the plain presence of choices.
- *Instance:* A flavour choice (which weapon to carry) gets a line that registers it and nothing more. A choice about whether to hand over a witness must change something that persists.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 4, E.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Acknowledgement is cheap: any V act that a character could witness gets an in-scene response (AGY-10). The authoring format marks each choice *flavour* (acknowledge only), *consequential* (writes a persisting record) or *unchoice* (a deliberate single option). Bar 4 applies to the second, and a lint rejects a consequential choice whose options write the same record. The result is also a warning: the cheap route works in a short session, and a system that relies on it alone is the quantum ogre.

**AGY-05. Name the fake choices and test for them: false choice, blind choice, and the quantum ogre.** `[analysis]`, `[practice]`
- *Source:* Mawhorter et al. 2014 (a false choice has all options lead to the same outcome, or to variations disproportionately small for the expected difference; they cite Mass Effect dialogue options that lead to the same line); the quantum-ogre sources in F-01.
- *Instance:* A fork where either path leads to the same fight.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3, E.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** The two-policy replay (§7, M-2): at each fork run V policies that take different options from the same saved state and compare the persisted records, the cast and the scene content. A fork whose records are identical is flagged unless declared flavour or unchoice. A separate rule for the engine's own sowing: it must not place a prepared set piece on every route (AGY-53).

**AGY-06. Different routes may reach the same destination if each route leaves its own mark.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Shea (Sly Flourish): an encounter that changes with the party's route is not a quantum ogre; his example gives the woods route wild creatures and a lost site and the road route the corrupt guards. Mawhorter et al.'s definition turns on the variation being *disproportionately small*, not on there being a shared endpoint.
- *Instance:* A heist that ends with the vault open whether V talked, hacked or fought, but with different witnesses, injuries, debts and records of what happened.
- *Scales:* G. *Layers:* 2, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** Each route variable must have at least one declared consumer ([dossier 4, R-9](d04-quest-structure.md)): a debrief, a reward, a later seed, a belief. Reconvergence (QST-11) is allowed because the marks differ. Authors do not have to write a separate ending per route, which is how the cost stays bounded.

**AGY-07. Felt agency can be induced without real control, so player-reported agency cannot be the only test of the engine.** `[empirical]`
- *Source:* Pailhès, Kumari and Kuhn 2021 (abstract: three experiments with the equivoque force produced a strong illusory sense of agency over the outcome, including after two repetitions and when participants had prior preferences; people missed the inconsistencies; the forcing did not change preference for the chosen item); Langer 1975 (summaries: cues of choice, involvement, familiarity and competition make people act as if chance outcomes are controllable); Fendt et al. 2012 (AGY-04).
- *Instance:* A magician's choice where every option leads to the same card. The spectator reports a free choice.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* E.
- *Operationalise:* **Neither produces nor checks the engine's output: it is a rule for evaluation and a prohibition.** Pair every perceived-agency survey with the structural tests (M-2, M-3). Forcing techniques may frame a low-stakes payload, meaning one that writes no persisting fact V could later be shown to have decided. They are never used on outcomes. The prohibition is mine `[hypothesis]` and follows from bar 4.

**AGY-08. A world that looks more capable than it is lowers agency. Keep the surface honest about what the model will honour.** `[empirical]`, one study; `[analysis]`
- *Source:* Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009 on Dow's Façade study (higher presence and realism raised expectations, made it harder for players to act in terms the model supports, and reduced agency; voice input felt natural but its hidden limits dissatisfied players); their argument that building on the Eliza effect, a system seeming more capable than it is, points the wrong way.
- *Instance:* A vendor who looks like a shopkeeper, invites a conversation and has nothing to say beyond a greeting.
- *Scales:* E. *Layers:* 1, 3, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** Capability honesty: every affordance the world shows (a person who can be spoken to, a door that looks usable) must have a response of at least the minimal kind (AGY-10), or must not be shown. The game port reports what it cannot express ([FIT-51](d03-fitting-casting.md)); the seed is linted against it.

### B. Responding to what V does (CRA-02)

**AGY-09. V's unplanned acts are offers. The world answers them with its own moves, not with an error.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Improwiki ("Accept": an offer is anything a partner says, does or implies; accepting it as fact is called the most important rule); Hocking's "fault tolerance" talk on Far Cry 2, as relayed in Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009 (I did not open the talk), which treats play as planful improvisation, not execution of a plan; Wardrip-Fruin et al.'s own argument that goals and plans are resources for action, not its cause.
- *Instance:* V sets off an alarm on purpose to pull the guards away. The guards respond to the alarm; the situation does not wait for the sneaking route.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** Obstacles state their affordances as predicates over general capabilities (QST-17, QST-18), so an unforeseen use that satisfies the predicate counts. Any act that is not matched is still an event with effects, and every other actor's frames can match it ([S0 §10.5, §10.8](../spec/kernel.md#105-changes-and-matching)), so the world has something to say about it. **Check:** scripted off-plan moves, with a pass when at least one actor frame responded.

**AGY-10. Accept the fact of V's act; do not necessarily grant its goal.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Improwiki: accepting is not agreeing, because a character can argue or resist while the player accepts the fact behind the offer. The lens's repair claim (acknowledging a move without granting its goal repairs most of the "stage set" feeling) is `[hypothesis]`, untested.
- *Instance:* V tries to bribe a guard. The bribe happened, the guard remembers it and refuses it, and the refusal costs the guard something.
- *Scales:* E. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** The act is pinned `observed` as an event ([S0 §9.2](../spec/kernel.md#92-events)), which is the "accept". What follows is decided by the other actors' frames, which is the "do not necessarily agree". The two are separate steps in the engine, so the world cannot skip the first. **Check:** for a sample of V acts, an event exists and its `witnesses` list matches who was present.

**AGY-11. An unacknowledged act feels like a stage set. There are three ways to fail to acknowledge one: soft refusal, ignoring, and steamrolling.** `[practice]` for the taxonomy; `[hypothesis]` for the size of the effect.
- *Source:* Improwiki (soft refusal: nominally accepting and then qualifying the offer until little remains; ignoring: not picking it up; steamrolling: accepting and overlaying your own idea); lens L1 and L2 (unacknowledged moves are the largest source of the feeling; untested).
- *Instance:* V rescues a courier and the next conversation proceeds as if the rescue had not happened (ignoring). The fixer says "thanks for dealing with it" and then changes the subject to a job that assumes V failed (soft refusal).
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** A scene lint flags a witnessed V act with no response in the same scene, a response that contradicts the act's recorded outcome, and a response that substitutes the author's intended outcome. These are the quantum ogre at small scale.

**AGY-12. When the world must refuse, it refuses honestly: the reason is in the fiction, comes from world state, and the act's real effect is still shown.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* The map's own note that "yes, and" cannot hold in a system that must refuse (CRA-02); Dungeon World's "tell them the requirements or consequences and ask" (the price must be clear in the fiction); [BND-29](d02-relationships.md) (no hidden roll over what V decided).
- *Instance:* "The steel door will not open to a card swipe. The swipe did flag your badge at the desk upstairs."
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** A refusal cites a world-state predicate (locked, occupied, forbidden by a rule the actor holds). A refusal with no cited predicate is rejected. The act's side effects are still written as events.

### C. Offers and the freedom to decline (MOT-01, CRA-12, CRA-14, CRA-04)

**AGY-13. Frame an offer as information, not as an order or a threat from the offerer.** `[empirical]` in the laboratory; transfer to games is untested.
- *Source:* Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999 (128 studies: engagement-, completion- and performance-contingent rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation, d = -0.40, -0.36 and -0.28; positive feedback enhanced it, d = 0.33); Rains 2013 (summary of the abstract: reactance, an amalgam of anger and negative thought, follows a freedom threat); Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski 2006 (autonomy and competence predicted enjoyment and later play); McLaren et al. (abstract: in 1,120 players' accounts of D&D 5th Edition, one theme was supporting or restricting player agency, and the game master can meet players' needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness).
- *Instance:* "My supplier's skimming from a shipment and I can't prove it" invites a decision. "You need to deal with this tonight" is an order. The Minutemen jobs of F-09 are the order form.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 2, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** The offer record states a situation: who needs what, what is at stake and who bears it, and what is on the table. A lint flags imperatives aimed at V, deadlines attached to V instead of to a world fact (AGY-29), reproach (AGY-27) and a failure message on lapse. Pay is part of a job's nature (QST-13), so the lint does not forbid it. Whether paid offers lower later voluntary engagement (the reward literature) is an open question (§10).

**AGY-14. Offer once, then withdraw. Offer again only when the world has changed.** `[hypothesis]`, with `[empirical]` support from reactance and a `[shipped]` failure
- *Source:* The reactance sources in AGY-13; F-09 (the giver offers whenever V is near); [QST-10](d04-quest-structure.md) (an unwanted offer needs a cap and a memory); Ignatius, Annotation 6 (before changing the exercises, the director asks whether the person has been doing them at all); lens L1 on Montessori practice (a repeated prompt cuts uptake), which is untested.
- *Instance:* A fixer calls once about a gig. After silence he texts once. He calls again only when something has changed: the pay has risen because the first crew was caught, or a rival has moved in.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Each offer stores when, through which channel and in what wording it was made. A re-offer needs a recorded *cause* that post-dates the decline or lapse. The cadence arbiter ([FIT-48](d03-fitting-casting.md); [DIR-13, DIR-24, DIR-30](d07-direction-pacing.md): one arbiter, one request released at a time, caps per hour) holds the rate, and every request expires quietly and by a named form ([DIR-25](d07-direction-pacing.md)). **Check:** re-offers per declined offer; any re-offer with no new cause is a defect.

**AGY-15. Before a consequence lands on something V cares about, tell V in the fiction what it will be if V does not act.** `[practice]`, `[corpus]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World ("show signs of an approaching threat" shows that something bad is coming unless players act; "tell them the requirements or consequences and ask" makes the price clear); the gig files (24 send a warning text before failing when V strays, [open-world activities §4.4](../../../knowledge/open-world-activities.md)); Cardona-Rivera et al. (foreseeability raises agency).
- *Instance:* "If you walk away from this, he takes the Maelstrom's offer" said by the client while V is still in the room.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** The stakes line comes from the client's frame (what it wants, what blocks it, what it will do instead). A consequence that can land on a person or thing V has shown care for must have an earlier *exposed* portent (AGY-23). **Check** with M-1.

**AGY-16. Only an offer V perceived can be declined. An offer that was not perceived has not yet been made.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Ignatius, Annotation 6 (when no response is seen, first ask whether the exercises are being done and how); lens L11 (record whether the player demonstrably perceived a beat; callbacks may cite only perceived events); [S0 §11.4](../spec/kernel.md#114-knowing) (an observer knows only what it saw, was told or believed).
- *Instance:* A missed call is not a refusal. A call picked up and cut short is.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 1, 2, 3, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** An offer's delivery status is one of `delivered`, `perceived`, `answered`, `declined`, `lapsed-after-perceived` or `withdrawn-unperceived`. Only the third and later states count as V's act. An unperceived offer is retried once through the next rung of the channel ladder ([FIT-47](d03-fitting-casting.md)) and then withdrawn with no residue ([FIT-46](d03-fitting-casting.md)). Which channels can report perception is a P8 question; where none can, line of sight and dwell time stand in, and the status is recorded as inferred. Dossier 7 adds two cautions: moment detection is imperfect even when state is explicit ([DIR-16](d07-direction-pacing.md)), and unknown attention stays unknown, so a stretch of no input marks V as away and an offer delivered then is `delivered`, not `perceived` ([DIR-60](d07-direction-pacing.md)).

**AGY-17. Declining takes several forms, and each is an act the world answers.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Synthesis of the improv taxonomy of responses (AGY-09 to AGY-11), the forms in dossier 4 (QST-24, a non-combat mode), and [BND-15](d02-relationships.md) (a path with exits at every step, the decline authored with the same care as the acceptance).
- *Instance:* see the table.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 1, 2, 3, 4.

| Form | What V does | How the engine knows | What the world does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit no | Chooses a decline option in dialogue | A dialogue event | The client's frame stays blocked (AGY-26); the client reacts in character, once |
| Silence | Perceives the offer and does nothing within its window | `lapsed-after-perceived` (AGY-16) | The same, with a longer, quieter reaction |
| Walking away | Leaves a scene or a started thread | Distance or exit event | The world continues from the last state (AGY-31) |
| Counter-offer | Takes the job on other terms | A dialogue event with terms | The client accepts, refuses or bargains by its own frame |
| Partial | Does one part and not the rest | Objective states | The unmet part follows AGY-26 |
| Reinterpretation | Does something else that touches the stake | Effects of V's act | The stake resolves by its new state; the act is an offer (AGY-09) |
| Delay | Says "later" | A dialogue event | A window is set by the client's urgency ladder ([DRV-15](d01-motivation-drives.md)) |
| Acceptance and turning | Takes the job and acts against the client | Objective and act events | The client's frame reacts; this is not a decline but a different act |

- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Each form writes a typed event. Authors need not write all of them: defaults per form come from the client's frame, and the author adds only what is distinctive. **Check:** every offer has a recorded response type for each form, either authored or defaulted.

**AGY-18. Offer a span of stances. Do not trim the number of options for fear of overload.** `[practice]`; the overload evidence is `[empirical]` and weak
- *Source:* [D-09](../decisions.md) (where content gives V a voice it offers choices spanning the stances a V could plausibly take); Sasko via Game Developer (offer options the player and the protagonist would expect); Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd 2010 (meta-analysis of 63 conditions, N = 5,036: the mean effect of choice overload is virtually zero, with large variance between studies).
- *Instance:* A decision beat with options to help as asked, help on different terms, refuse and leave, refuse and warn the target, and act against the client.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 2, 4, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** A decision beat declares which stance classes it covers (help, bargain, refuse, oppose, ignore). A lint warns when it covers fewer than three or has no refusal. The count of options shown at once is a P8 and UI matter; the meta-analysis says no number is known to be safe or unsafe.

**AGY-19. Unrequested help can reduce autonomy and enjoyment. Offer help on request, or when V is demonstrably stuck, and once.** `[empirical]` for the effect; `[hypothesis]` for transfer to non-player characters
- *Source:* Kim, Chen and Zhang 2016 (six experiments across three games: a humanlike computerised helper lowered enjoyment through lower perceived autonomy, and lowered motivation to continue). The helper was a game assistant, not a story character, so the transfer is untested.
- *Instance:* A companion who keeps volunteering where to go next, against one who answers when asked.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** Proactive hints from characters pass the same cadence arbiter as offers (AGY-14). They are tied to the proactivity dial: at lower settings the engine volunteers nothing. A hint is a fact in the world (a note left, a lead mentioned) before it is a spoken nudge, and it waits a grace delay and points before it tells ([DIR-19](d07-direction-pacing.md)).

**AGY-20. The engine's levers are constraints and affordances in the world, not instructions to V. Every steer is traced.** `[analysis]`, `[practice]`, weak evidence
- *Source:* Newell 1986 and Davids, Button and Bennett 2008 (the constraints-led approach shapes behaviour by changing the task, the environment and the performer, and leaves the solution to the performer; lens-level for both books); Pinder et al. 2011 (abstract: practice tasks should be faithful to the real setting's information, "representative learning design"); Ignatius, Annotation 15 (the guide should not incline to either side, "standing in the centre like a balance"); Montessori (lens-level: a prepared environment, then step back).
- *Instance:* To bring V to a clinic, the director does not say so. The clinic's shutters are up, a van is outside, and a fixer mentions the owner is looking for someone.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** The director's levers are changes to world state: who is where, what is scarce, what is on offer. Each lever is recorded with its reason in the trace ([S0 §13](../spec/kernel.md#13-provenance-and-explanation)). A lint rejects any text that instructs V. Dossier 7 ranks the director's verbs by how much a player would notice ([DIR-02](d07-direction-pacing.md)), asks that its help be explicable in the world ([DIR-06](d07-direction-pacing.md)) and keeps its meter out of sight ([DIR-53](d07-direction-pacing.md)). **Limit:** the same text (Annotation 16) tells the director to push against an attachment the person has, which is a kind of steering the engine does not copy, because it would need a model of V's disposition that D-09 forbids.

**AGY-21. Deliver offers through the world, not through an interface marker, and let V pull.** `[hypothesis]`, with `[practice]` precedent
- *Source:* Lens L1 on alternate reality games (content with no interface marker rated more caused; authors pace by release, not difficulty); McGonigal's 2003 paper (not opened; the lens's account is unverified); [FIT-47](d03-fitting-casting.md) (a ladder of in-world channels). Majestic (calls on the system's schedule, lens L2) is the lens's counter-example and I did not verify it.
- *Instance:* A job posted on a board V walks past, or an overheard complaint, before any call.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** The offer's first rung is an in-world event that V may or may not notice. A tracked quest marker appears only after V accepts. **Check:** offers whose only channel is an interface message.

### D. Declining has consequences, never punishment (CRA-01, MOT-01, CRA-13)

**AGY-22. The world does not wait for V. Threats and plans advance on their own, whether or not V is there to see.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World, Fronts (a front's portents are the steps a danger takes toward its doom; fronts keep moving whether or not the characters are present; the game master should "think offscreen"); Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots" (define what the opposition does if the characters never intervene).
- *Instance:* A corporate buyout of a district's clinics proceeds in steps. V has never heard of it. Three weeks in, V's usual ripperdoc has a new owner.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** A front is a group's goal frame with its steps ([S0 §10.2](../spec/kernel.md#102-frames), `step` level; [foundation §5.2](../foundation.md#52-groups-and-membership)). It advances through its members' frames, which the simulation already runs. Authors supply a front's trajectory as a seed (AGY-46). **Check:** a run in which V does nothing still produces front events (liveness), and a share of them become perceivable to V at some point (M-7).

**AGY-23. Move in two steps: a portent V can perceive, then the hard step. A hard step that lands on something V cares about waits for the portent to have been seen and for V to have had a chance to answer.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World (soft moves have no immediate or irreversible consequences and leave time to react; hard moves have immediate consequences; a soft move that is ignored becomes a golden opportunity for a hard one; even when a hard move is available the game master may choose a soft one).
- *Instance:* Soft: a fixer's runner is found beaten outside V's flat. Hard, later: the crew V had been courting is hired away.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** The scope matters. Steps that land elsewhere in the world proceed off-screen regardless. A step that would land on V's own interests or on people V has dealings with (a hard step) is gated on three records: an *exposed* portent exists for it (AGY-32); a response window has elapsed, sized from the client frame's urgency ladder ([DRV-15](d01-motivation-drives.md)); and V's response, if any, has been applied. If the portent was never perceived, the engine re-stages it once through another channel, or lets the step happen off-screen and delivers it as news (AGY-27). It does not spring the step on V with no earlier sign. Dossier 7 supplies the matching mechanics: announce a threat before it acts ([DIR-41](d07-direction-pacing.md)), and warn about 8 seconds before a disruptive offer ([DIR-18](d07-direction-pacing.md)). **Check:** M-1.

**AGY-24. A clock is progress toward an outcome, shown in steps. It is a view over the record, not a counter that is ticked.** `[practice]`; derivation `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Blades in the Dark SRD (clocks of 4, 6 or 8 segments for obstacles of rising complexity; danger clocks, racing clocks, linked clocks, mission clocks that limit a window, tug-of-war clocks that events move both ways, long-term project clocks, faction clocks; a clock is named after the obstacle, not the method, and shows the state of the fiction without deciding it); [DRV-12](d01-motivation-drives.md) (derive urgency from timestamped facts).
- *Instance:* A pursuit is a racing clock between V's escape and the chasers' search. V's acts and the chasers' acts both move it.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** `progress(front, t)` is computed from the completed steps and the elapsed world time. Segments are a presentation grain (a count of 4, 6 or 8 is how a person reads it), not a stored value that ticks. **Check:** replaying from the seed and the pins gives the same progress at any time, which a ticking counter would not under the kernel's quantisation. This agrees with [DIR-48](d07-direction-pacing.md) (a hostile thread declares a clock with segments, tick sources and a visible sign per segment; a full clock happens to the world and not to V's progress).

**AGY-25. Settle the world's movement at the joints V can notice: arriving, resting, finishing a job. Not on a stopwatch that makes V's own time the cost.** `[practice]`, `[hypothesis]` for the engine form
- *Source:* Blades in the Dark SRD (the game master advances the faction clocks that matter to the characters during downtime, which makes the city feel as if it moves on without them); F-04 (a fixed day limit that, as reported, pushed players to skip side content).
- *Instance:* V finishes a gig and sleeps. On waking, two things in the city have changed, each with a trace V can read.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** Time always passes, and the simulation moves other actors as it does. What changes is when the consequences are *bound* for V: a front's progress over V's absence is resolved lazily, through summary refinement ([S0 §9.3](../spec/kernel.md#93-summaries-and-their-refinement)), when V arrives, rests or asks, at the level of detail the question needs. Pacing of pushes is a separate matter and runs on the play clock, so a night's sleep does not release a night's backlog ([DIR-34](d07-direction-pacing.md)). **Check:** no consequence that V must live with first appears in the middle of a scene V is playing, unless the scene's own events caused it.

**AGY-26. When V declines, ignores or abandons an offer, its stake resolves without V, by the client's own frame. That is a consequence. It is not a punishment, and the engine produces only the first.** `[hypothesis]`, resting on `[shipped]` studio practice and `[empirical]` laboratory work
- *Source:* Weber (studio developer, reported by Game World Observer 2019): quests cannot be failed in the classic sense; the designers ensure V still reaches the story's end and V lives with the consequences; the map's tension "declining: consequence or no penalty" and [QST-15](d04-quest-structure.md) (define the consequence as the world moving on); the reactance and reward sources in AGY-13; [S0 §10.4](../spec/kernel.md#104-lifecycle) (a blocked frame keeps listening for what would unblock it).
- *Instance:* A clinic owner needs a courier job done and V does not take it. She hires a cheaper crew, the delivery is late, and she loses a client. V hears of it when V next passes the street. Nobody blames V.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 1, 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Produce: the client's goal frame stays `blocked`; its drive ladder continues; it finds another means by the ordinary casting and query machinery, and the outcome (success, partial, failure) is decided by world state and recorded with causes. Check, as the **decline audit** (M-1): an effect is a consequence and not a punishment only if (a) its targets are inside the offer's stake set; (b) no text blames V; (c) its trigger cites the client's or other actors' frames and not V's absence; (d) a portent of it was exposed (AGY-23). Dossier 7's [DIR-38](d07-direction-pacing.md) states the matching rule from the other side: every offer V never takes must be complete without V, and declining must not feel like losing the main thing. A consequence that is *kinder* to V than the offer would have been is equally legitimate. World state decides, and there is no thumb on the scale in either direction.

**AGY-27. Deliver a consequence as news, in the third person, about the world. Do not reproach.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* [BND-26](d02-relationships.md) (no guilt on leaving or returning, no countdown on a relationship); [BND-19](d02-relationships.md) (meet a no with dignity; keep pursuit low in pressure and rate-limited); reactance (AGY-13).
- *Instance:* "The Biotech drop went out with the Tygers' crew. Two of them didn't come back." Not: "If you'd taken it, they'd be alive."
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 3, 4, X.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** A text lint for the second person with an evaluative verb, "should have", "if only you", and any claim about V's feelings or reasons. An NPC may state their own feeling once, in character, as a fact about them. M-9 counts violations.

**AGY-28. A decline is a fact the world remembers. It shapes the client's later behaviour. It closes no door unrelated to the offer.** `[hypothesis]`, with `[practice]` support
- *Source:* Lens L1 on reflecting a person's account back and on utilization (using a refusal as material, not as failure; not opened); the improv rule of treating what the partner does as established fact; [BND-18](d02-relationships.md) (a refusal comes from the NPC's own state and persists).
- *Instance:* A fixer who has been turned down for wet work three times stops opening with it, and V's other contacts are unchanged.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** The decline is an event whose witnesses form beliefs ([S0 §11](../spec/kernel.md#11-beliefs-and-perspectives)). The client's later offers adapt to that belief, which is an *adaptation* (AGY-42), silent, and changes only which true offers are made. **Check:** a lockout lint. No decline-triggered change to V's access to unrelated people, vendors or content unless an exposed portent and a world cause exist.

**AGY-29. A time limit is acceptable when it is a world fact V could perceive, it was part of the offer, and the lapse is a coda and not a failure message. A stated urgency is always paid.** `[hypothesis]`, answering the map's tension
- *Source:* The map (NAR-17: a clock needs a perceivable source and fires whether or not the player attends; a deadline that punishes a player who was elsewhere breeds resentment); [FIT-44 to FIT-46](d03-fitting-casting.md) (windows, a player-triggered close, the four forms at close); F-04 and F-06 (the punishing clock and the false clock); [BND-26](d02-relationships.md).
- *Instance:* "The shipment leaves at midnight" is a recorded departure. At midnight it leaves, and the offer lapses with a short coda about where it went. A fixer who says "tonight" and then waits three days without anything happening has told V a falsehood.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** A deadline must reference a window predicate with a source event. The lapse form is *coda* or *memory* (FIT-46), never a failed state. A deadline on a relationship is forbidden. Expiry follows [DIR-25](d07-direction-pacing.md) (quiet and by a named form) and lead time follows [DIR-55](d07-direction-pacing.md). **Check:** every stated urgency in a text or line has a matching recorded lapse; every lapse has a matching earlier statement (P-5).

**AGY-30. An unanswered front's worst outcome is bounded: it changes the setting and costs people and places. It does not end V's access to the world, and it does not take away someone V depends on without portents and a window.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World, Fronts (a danger's doom sets in after all its portents, the danger is then resolved, and the setting has changed); lens L5's rule that a doom should not be world-ending, which I did not find in the text (§3.3) and adopt as my own.
- *Instance:* A buyout completes and the district's clinics change hands. V can still get treatment, from a different, worse-run place.
- *Scales:* A. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** Doom classes with a severity cap, sharing the tension budget with dossier 7 ([DIR-30 to DIR-34](d07-direction-pacing.md)). A front proposed with a doom above the cap is rejected or must be marked an authored exception with its portent and window counts.

**AGY-31. V can leave any scene or thread at any time. The world continues from where V left it.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* [FIT-26](d03-fitting-casting.md) (never trap the player; allow retreat and rejoin); Improwiki on blocking; Czepeku on West Marches (each session ends at a safe base, so each is self-contained).
- *Instance:* V walks out of a negotiation. The other party keeps acting on its own frame: it makes the call it was about to make.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** A scene has an `on-abandon` rule. The default is *continue*: actors with time-critical stakes proceed, settled lazily (AGY-25), and the rest freeze at their last state until V returns. Physical constraints that stop V leaving (a locked room) are stated in the fiction. **Check:** replay leaving at every beat of a sample of seeds, and confirm the world has a legal state each time.

### E. Making consequences legible (MOT-04, CRA-07, CRA-09, CRA-13)

**What the evidence says about telling, showing and delay.** Read this first; the principles below rest on it.

| Question | What was found | Grade and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Does telling the player an act was noted keep felt agency? | In one six-choice text story, immediate acknowledgement kept most of the felt agency of a branching version. It did not clearly beat a plain, low-feedback version, and branching still scored higher on "the story would have been different" (Fendt et al.) | [empirical], short, snowball sample, primed as a choose-your-own-adventure |
| Does an announced consequence that is not paid cost trust? | Critics say so about "will remember that" and about Mass Effect 3's endings. No study measures it | [practice], critic |
| Does showing beat telling? | The studio's own quest director says consequences must be visible and that subtle ones are lost; Church makes the same point from the player's side. No experiment compares showing with naming | [practice], [shipped]; the comparison is untested |
| Do people explain by naming the whole chain? | No. Explanations are contrastive and selected: one or two causes, against a foil (Miller's review) | [empirical], review |
| Does delay break attribution? | In learning tasks causal ratings fall within seconds, and the fall is smaller when a delay is expected or its timeframe is stated (Shanks et al.; Buehner and May, by summary) | [empirical] for seconds; hours and days in play are untested |
| Are delayed consequences worth it? | The studio says a very late return of an early choice leaves the strongest impression; the choice-poetics paper says a delayed effect makes the choice feel important at the reveal and can breed frustration | [practice]; no measurement |
| Can players be trusted to remember what they chose? | Not reliably: in a laboratory task people missed swaps between choice and outcome and explained the swapped one (Johansson et al.) | [empirical], faces, not games |

The gaps matter more than the findings. There is no test of naming against showing in a game, no delay curve for play, and no study of discovered illusion with a trace to hand. Where a principle needs one of these, it is `[hypothesis]` and has an open question in §10.

**AGY-32. Record what V was shown, separately from what happened.** `[hypothesis]` on the form; the need is the map's MOT-04 finding
- *Source:* Map MOT-04 (an unseen consequence costs effort and gives no agency, so the system must log what was shown); lens L11 (record whether the player demonstrably perceived a beat; callbacks may cite only perceived events); Church 1999; the studio's remark in F-10; [S0 §6.1 and §9.2](../spec/kernel.md#92-events) (observed pins; events have `witnesses`).
- *Instance:* A bar is rebuilt after V's earlier act. Whether V walked past it and saw the new sign is a fact the engine keeps.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** An **exposure ledger** derived from `observed` pins and V-witnessed events, plus perception events from the game port. Each consequence has an exposure: `unshown`, `shown` (seen or heard in the world, with time and channel) or `named` (a line cited V's act). An unshown consequence may stay unshown, be delivered late through the cheapest in-world channel (rumour, a message, a closure ledger, AGY-41), or never be delivered. It must never be claimed (AGY-43). Count what V can see, not what exists ([DIR-33](d07-direction-pacing.md)). **Check:** M-7.

**AGY-33. V credits an event to V's own act when the act came first, the effect fits what the act meant, and no rival cause is more conspicuous. The engine checks all three before a callback names the act.** `[empirical]` for the experience of willing an action; `[hypothesis]` for world events in games
- *Source:* Wegner and Wheatley 1999 (summaries: apparent mental causation follows the priority, consistency and exclusivity of the thought with the act); Miller 2019 (§4: people prefer to explain by abnormal, intentional and controllable events, and undo controllable events in preference to uncontrollable ones); Church 1999 (players must be able to see why something worked or failed). Wegner's work is about the feeling that *I willed this*; applying it to *this happened because I did that* is an analogy.
- *Instance:* V's tip-off lets a rival crew hit a depot. Another crew's own scheme also exists. The callback names V's tip only if the record shows no more conspicuous cause.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Before a named-act line is emitted: (1) *priority*: V's act precedes the effect on the `causes` chain; (2) *consistency*: a recorded path of at most k controllable events links them (k is a tunable, **[J]**); (3) *exclusivity*: no other recorded cause by another actor is more proximate. If one is, name that cause or stay silent. The callback finder returns the candidate with the shortest controllable path from a V act.

**AGY-34. Plant the expectation of a delay when the act is made, and restate the act when the consequence lands.** `[empirical]` for delays of seconds; `[hypothesis]` for hours and days
- *Source:* Shanks, Pearson and Dickinson 1989 (summaries: in a learning task, causal ratings fell with a delay of two seconds and delays over four seconds removed the impression of causation); Buehner and May 2002 (abstract: causal judgements fell with delay, less so when the thematic context led people to expect a delay, and less again when instructions mentioned the timeframe); Mawhorter et al. 2014 (a delayed effect makes the choice feel more important at the reveal and can breed frustration); Sasko via Game Developer (delayed consequences leave the strongest impression and must be visible).
- *Instance:* When V lets a courier go, she says she will find a way to return it. Nine days later her sister gives V a better price and says why. The expectation was set at the act.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** A planned delayed consequence has an `expect` cue on the act's event, with a coarse timeframe class (soon, a few days, long) in the fiction ([DRV-19](d01-motivation-drives.md) gives favours a plausible lag). A delayed consequence without a cue must carry a stronger anchor in its own callback (AGY-35). **Check:** M-3, split by delay bucket, to find where attribution fails.

**AGY-35. Name what V did, plainly. Never name why, and never name how V felt.** `[empirical]` for the confabulation result; the rule is `[analysis]` under D-09
- *Source:* Johansson, Hall, Sikström and Olsson 2005 (abstract: participants often missed clear mismatches between the face they chose and the face they were shown, and gave reasons for the choice they had not made; detection figures in secondary accounts vary and I did not verify them), which the map holds as PER-12; [D-09](../decisions.md); [FIT-49](d03-fitting-casting.md).
- *Instance:* "You're the one who walked the courier out of the warehouse." Not: "You couldn't stand to see her hurt."
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 3, 4, X.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Callbacks are templated from the act record: verb, object, place, rough time. The record stores what V did, not intentions. A lint rejects any second-person claim about inner state ([FIT-49](d03-fitting-casting.md)).

**AGY-36. Give the one cause V can recognise, and the contrast if it helps. Leave the rest of the chain in the trace.** `[empirical]`, as a review of the explanation literature
- *Source:* Miller 2019 (explanations are contrastive: people ask why P and not Q; selected: people choose one or two causes from many; causes work better than probabilities; explanations are social, framed relative to what the explainer thinks the listener believes).
- *Instance:* "That's why they went with Kang Tao and not you." One cause, one contrast.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3, X.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** The explainer picks from the causal path ([S0 §13](../spec/kernel.md#13-provenance-and-explanation)) the event that is most recent, intentional, controllable and unusual, and, where it helps, the alternative V's act excluded. The complete chain stays in `trace`. This is the same idea as the staging dossier's explanation field (SEL-10, dossier 20). **Check:** M-3.

**AGY-37. Show first, and name when the cause is not otherwise visible. Name at most one act per scene, in proportion to the stakes.** `[practice]`; the numbers are `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Sasko via Game Developer (signal against noise: give important information in focused, isolated moments, and consequences must be telegraphed enough to be seen); Grice's quantity maxim, as summarised (be as informative as required and no more; Grice himself doubted the second part); [TEL-07](d06-sifting-tellability.md) (a callback at a distance needs a cue); the Telltale case (F-03).
- *Instance:* A rebuilt stall is shown. The vendor says one line about it. No second NPC says the same thing.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, X.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** A **telling budget** per scene: at most one named-act reference, except in closure scenes (AGY-41), and the line may be dropped if the world already shows the trace. The budget scales with the act's stakes **[J]**. A "quiet" setting lowers it. **Check:** references per scene; M-3 split by named and shown, to find whether naming adds attribution (open question 1).

**AGY-38. Announce nothing you do not intend to pay. Every announced consequence is a debt entry.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* F-02 and F-03; [QST-03](d04-quest-structure.md) (a promise ledger, each open promise paid or openly retired).
- *Instance:* "They'll remember this" creates a promise. If no consequence follows within its window, the engine pays it with a small one or retires it with a stated reason.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Any line or cue that promises a consequence writes a promise record at the moment it is emitted. M-8 counts promises that end with no event.

**AGY-39. Never contradict the record about what V did. Forget by rule, in character, or not at all.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Lens L12 (a single missing or contradictory memory generalises to "the world forgets everything"; search-summary level); [BND-28](d02-relationships.md) and map SAV-03 (a character half-remembers aloud; forgetting decays with the chance of being needed again).
- *Instance:* A character who watched V's act behaves as if it never happened.
- *Scales:* E, G, A. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** A witness's belief, once formed, may fade by a stated rule but may not be contradicted. A response from an actor who witnessed an event, and who holds no faded belief, that contradicts the event is a defect.

**AGY-40. Far consequences are the strongest. Give them a cue that grows with time, and use them sparingly.** `[practice]`; the weak evidence is flagged
- *Source:* Sasko via Game Developer (bringing an early choice back much later, when the player least expects it, leaves the strongest impression; callbacks to earlier story elements at unexpected moments reward choices); a practitioner essay on improv callbacks, seen in summary (a callback works best when the audience has half forgotten the earlier moment, and forcing one too early falls flat); [TEL-07 and R-15 of dossier 6](d06-sifting-tellability.md) (the cue grows with elapsed time and similar events since).
- *Instance:* The game itself reads fates written long before: its epilogue holograms read side-quest facts such as `sq011_patricia_dead` ([main-story state](../../../knowledge/main-story-state.md), fact meanings `[hypothesis]`).
- *Scales:* A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** The callback finder sifts V's chronicle for acts connected to the present ([dossier 6](d06-sifting-tellability.md)), prefers far ones at a low, tunable rate, and sets the cue's strength from elapsed time and the number of similar events since. **Check:** M-3 by delay bucket.

**AGY-41. Offer closure ledgers: a place where off-screen consequences are gathered, shown once and selected.** `[shipped]`, `[corpus]`
- *Source:* The game's own final boards, which read about twenty-five character facts as good-news and bad-news panels ([main-story state §4](../../../knowledge/main-story-state.md)); Fallout: New Vegas's ending slides (reported in search summaries: the designer said they took effort for variety and reactivity, and that side-quest slides were given for how much the developers liked the quest, not for importance); Mass Effect 3's Extended Cut epilogue, which varies with earlier choices (F-02). Fact meanings are `[hypothesis]` until confirmed in game.
- *Instance:* On leaving Night City or finishing an arc, V sees what became of the people V touched, in a short, selective list.
- *Scales:* A. *Layers:* 3, 4, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** A closure generator reads the record at a natural closure point, ranks the people and places V materially changed by stakes and by exposure, and composes a short ledger of news. Unshown consequences can be delivered here once. **Check:** every entry cites a recorded event; no claim is made that is not in the record.

**AGY-42. Consequences are named. Adaptations stay silent. Never present an adaptation as a consequence.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Map tension "hidden adaptation or visible responsiveness" (MOT-05, CRA-09, PER-06); lens L1 on hospitality (personalisation shown by changing the offer, as in a sushi counter that seats a guest by handedness and says nothing; film and review evidence only); Shostack's line of visibility (lens-level).
- *Instance:* A consequence: the fixer cites the gig V botched. An adaptation: the fixer stops opening with wet work because V has turned it down three times, and says nothing of it.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Two record types: a `consequence`, whose cause path starts at a V act; and an `adaptation`, whose cause is a director belief ([S0 §11.3](../spec/kernel.md#113-the-directors-beliefs-about-v)). Only consequences have player-facing naming. An adaptation never changes what is true, only which true offers are made. Citing an adaptation as a consequence would be a false causal claim and breaks quality bar 1. A player-readable view of what the engine believes can be offered (open player modelling, ETH-20) and is a P6 setting.

**AGY-43. The credit must be true. Name V as the cause only when V was, and name bad outcomes as readily as good ones.** `[analysis]`
- *Source:* Quality bar 1; AGY-33's exclusivity condition.
- *Instance:* A depot burns because of a rival's scheme. The fixer does not say it was V's doing.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** The exclusivity check of AGY-33. A symmetry check: the share of named-credit lines for good outcomes should match the share of good outcomes in the record; a skew is flattery.

**AGY-44. Reflect back what V did, not who V is. Link acts before calling a pattern.** `[hypothesis]`
- *Source:* Carey and Russell 2003 (a single event that contradicts a dominant story is vulnerable; it has to be linked to other events along a theme before it becomes the beginning of an alternative story; the practice is a conversation with the person); lens L1 on Playback Theatre and utilization (not opened).
- *Instance:* "That's the third time you've taken the quiet way in." The fact is stated by a character who saw the three. It is not "you're a careful person."
- *Scales:* A. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** A pattern line needs at least three recorded acts of the same kind **[J]**. It is spoken as an observation by a witness. A lint rejects identity claims ("you are the sort of person who"). The practice these ideas come from works with the person's own participation and aims at their wellbeing. The engine has no such standing, and the privacy and profiling risk is the map's ETH-20.

### F. The game master's practice, distilled (CRA-01, CRA-02, CRA-18, CRA-04)

**AGY-45. Prepare situations, not plots.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots" (a plot is a sequence of events and depends on the characters making each expected choice; when they deviate the game master abandons the prep or forces them back, which is railroading; a situation is a set of circumstances: factions, places, people, resources, and what happens next depends on what the characters do); Shea (prepare parts and assemble them to fit the table's choices).
- *Instance:* Reference scenario R1 is a situation: a man who is alive, allied and free for a window, a romance the author wrote, and a set of preferences. It is not a script with a time stamp.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** A seed is a partly resolved situation ([foundation §6](../foundation.md#6-story-in-the-same-space)). The sower proposes blocked goals with their people and resources, not event chains. Chains of causes are checked inside a unit ([QST-01, QST-02](d04-quest-structure.md)) and not across units.

**AGY-46. For every situation, define what the opposition does if V never intervenes.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots" (define what the opposition is trying to do if the characters never intervene, and let its goals drive events); Dungeon World, Fronts (dangers with impulses, portents toward a doom, and stakes questions that play answers).
- *Instance:* The clinic owner's plan if V never comes: hire a cheaper crew, miss a delivery, lose a client, sell up.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Each seed has an `unattended` plan: ordered steps (the portents) and an end state. It is a default trajectory that V's acts alter, not a script. **Check:** run the seed with a do-nothing V. It must reach a legal end state in finite time without stalling, and each step must be an event another actor could perceive.

**AGY-47. Give every needed conclusion at least three independent routes, treat the routes as the reason V may ignore any one lead, and guard the fallback.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Alexander, "The Three Clue Rule" (for any conclusion, at least three clues, because players miss a clue, ignore one or misread one; clues cost little and holding them back removes paths; red herrings are risky; keep a checklist of which clues have been found; if players stall, a proactive event such as an attack or another death can supply new clues, and a last fallback can end the scenario); the node-based design series (a node is a grouping of material, not a unit of playing time); [QST-19, FIT-23, FIT-24](d04-quest-structure.md).
- *Instance:* A missing-person gig whose location can be got from a witness, a camera log or a pawn-shop receipt.
- *Scales:* G. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Check and produce.** Dossiers 3 and 4 own the routes check and the clue-loss simulation (M-13). What this dossier adds: (a) the redundancy exists to protect V's freedom to ignore a lead without penalty; (b) a **ledger of which leads V has been shown** (the exposure ledger), so a missed lead shows early; (c) a stalled-thread fallback as a **world event that supplies a new lead**, allowed only if V has engaged with the thread, and traced like any steer (AGY-20); (d) the fallback waits a grace delay and points before it tells ([DIR-19](d07-direction-pacing.md)); (e) if V ignores every lead, the thread lapses with a coda (AGY-26, AGY-29) and does not turn into a chase.

**AGY-48. Keep mechanisms out of the fiction. Show a clock only when V can act on what it measures.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World ("never speak the name of your move"); the Apocalypse World review (the same principle); Blades in the Dark SRD (clocks show the state of the fiction and do not decide it, and players see them); Rascal News on *Citizen Sleeper* (the designer argues clocks make variables visible in an engaging way; the piece does not say what happens when a clock runs out).
- *Instance:* A pursuit V is trying to shake can have a visible meter. A buyout V has never heard of has none: it shows up as portents.
- *Scales:* E, G. *Layers:* 3, 8.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** Presentation rule for P8, agreeing with dossier 7 ([DIR-41, DIR-48](d07-direction-pacing.md): disclose a hostile thread, then run a visible clock on it). Order matters: the world's own plan that V does not yet know about is revealed first through portents (AGY-23); once it has been disclosed and V can act on it, its progress may be shown as a progress display. A clock for a thread V has not perceived would announce it by the back door. No mechanism is ever named in a line ("your standing fell by two"), and no director meter is shown ([DIR-53](d07-direction-pacing.md)).

**AGY-49. Use V's acts as the answers. Press on what V has done and shown they care about, never on a V the engine has imagined.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Dungeon World principles ("ask questions and use the answers", "be a fan of the characters"); lens L1 on the Apocalypse World instruction to press on what the characters care about; Thue et al. (AGY-03); [D-09](../decisions.md).
- *Instance:* V's repeated visits to one street vendor make the vendor a candidate for a later consequence. The engine does not conclude that V is sentimental.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** Sowing reads the V chronicle and the director's engagement beliefs. The engine asks nothing out of the fiction in the middle of a scene. Questions of preference belong to settings (AGY-51).

**AGY-50. Continuity can come from a shared record and V's own choices of where to go, with threads travelling by rumour.** `[practice]`, secondary sources only
- *Source:* Czepeku's account of Robbins's West Marches (no fixed calendar; players choose a destination from a map the game master updates; the game master prepares for the chosen place; sessions start and end at a safe base; continuity comes from the shared map and session summaries). The article says nothing on quest givers or rumour, so the rumour part is the lens's (CRA-18).
- *Instance:* V is told about a place by a person who heard of it. Nothing is prepared for it until V approaches.
- *Scales:* A. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** The V chronicle is the shared record. Beliefs travel ([foundation §5.4](../foundation.md#54-perspectives-everyone-has-their-own-model)) and carry threads. Nothing is bound for a place V has not chosen until V nears it (AGY-53). An outing that ends at a safe place is a natural joint for AGY-25. **Risk:** an empty sandbox. The engine must keep density up, which is dossier 5 and P5's concern.

**AGY-51. Agree the contract before play. The engine's version is the player's settings, set before and changeable at any time.** `[empirical]` in outline, `[practice]`
- *Source:* McLaren, Oades and Deery (abstract: in 1,120 players' accounts of D&D 5th Edition, an autonomy-supportive game master meets players' needs; one theme was supporting or restricting player agency); quality bar 8.
- *Instance:* Settings for how proactive the engine is, how fast the world moves while V is busy, how much the game names acts, and what content is allowed.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 4, config.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce.** P6 settings (R6-18). Each has a default that works untouched and an override. I did not verify the article's detail on how respondents described restricting agency.

**AGY-52. Adjudicate in the open. The engine can show that it did not steer, which a human cannot.** `[analysis]`
- *Source:* The Alexandrian's account of illusionism (F-11: secret fudging, hidden steering, discovered more often than assumed); [S0 §13](../spec/kernel.md#13-provenance-and-explanation) (every pin, contribution, edge, belief, event, frame and dilemma has a cause, and `trace` returns the complete record); [BND-29](d02-relationships.md); the foundation's rule that everything is inspectable.
- *Instance:* An agent samples ten consequences and gets, for each, the full record that produced it.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* all, E.
- *Operationalise:* **Check.** M-14: sample consequences and confirm each has a complete trace with no unlogged input. The no-fudge invariant: the same record and seed give the same consequence, and a reload reproduces it ([SAV-02](map.md)). The engine's policy: steering through world state is allowed and traced (AGY-20). A hidden override of an outcome V decided is forbidden. The trace does not settle whether V should be able to read it, which is a P6 and P10 matter.

**AGY-53. Keep a toolbox and bind late. Build no contingencies for choices V has not made.** `[practice]`
- *Source:* Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots" and its follow-up on tools not contingencies (contingency prep is a choose-your-own-adventure trap); Shea (prepare parts, assemble at the table); the kernel's late binding ([S0 §6](../spec/kernel.md#6-pins-time-and-propagation)); [FIT-01](d03-fitting-casting.md) (units that state what they need and change).
- *Instance:* "If V sides with the Tygers" is not prepared. A set of consequence units with preconditions on the record is, and the matching one is chosen when V's choice is in the record.
- *Scales:* G, A. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **Produce and check.** Consequence units are storylets with preconditions on the record. None is instantiated for an un-made choice, and the count of such objects at idle is zero. This also keeps cost from multiplying: a generator that multiplies variants without multiplying testing is the critique of branching recorded in [dossier 4](d04-quest-structure.md).

## 5. What the engine can and cannot take from the tabletop

| Practice | What a human game master does | What the engine can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare parts, assemble in play (AGY-45, 53) | Improvises from prepared material | Binds from storylets, casting and fitting at the moment of need | Invent material outside the authored vocabulary with a GM's taste. Generated text renders; it does not plan |
| Fronts and clocks (AGY-22, 24) | Decides when and how hard to tick | Derives progress from the record and runs every front at once, never forgetting one | Judge in the moment whether a step would be fun |
| Soft before hard (AGY-23) | Reads the player's reaction to the warning | Gates the hard step on the exposure ledger and a response window | Tell whether V was bored or irritated by the portent |
| Ask questions, use the answers (AGY-49) | Asks aloud, mid-scene | Uses V's acts and choices as the answers; asks only in settings | Ask out-of-fiction questions without breaking the scene |
| Be a fan of the characters (AGY-49) | Presses on what the player cares about | Weighs by the chronicle and an engagement belief | Know what V cares about. It only believes (D-09) |
| Three clues (AGY-47) | Tracks clues in the head | Static checks, clue-loss simulation, a ledger of leads shown | Judge whether a clue is obvious: the author's bias remains |
| Say yes or roll the dice (AGY-09 to AGY-12) | Improvises a response | Makes every act an event the frames answer | Respond well outside its vocabulary |
| Session zero (AGY-51) | Talks it through | Settings, content controls | Renegotiate mid-scene by conversation |
| Adjudicate in the open (AGY-52) | Can fudge, and a good one does not | A full trace and determinism, so non-fudging is provable | Make V believe it except by showing the trace |
| Read the table | Watches faces, posture, energy | Proxies only (dwell, engagement), and only as beliefs | Detect boredom, fatigue or distress |
| Shared record (AGY-50) | Keeps notes and a map | The V chronicle and travelling beliefs | Coordinate several players |

## 6. Two walk-throughs

These are invented illustrations for this dossier and are not content.

### 6.1 A declined gig

A clinic owner, Mara, needs a delivery of blood product moved across a district before a client's deadline. Her frame is `blocked`: the courier she used is in custody. A fixer passes it to V by call.

1. V's phone shows a missed call. The ledger says `delivered`, not `perceived` (AGY-16). Nothing is lost. After a quiet interval the fixer texts once, a second rung of the ladder.
2. V reads the text, opens the brief, and walks away. The ledger marks `perceived`; no act follows; the window (set from Mara's urgency ladder, DRV-15) elapses. The status is `lapsed-after-perceived`. V has declined by silence.
3. A portent is staged before any hard step: a courtesy text from the fixer says Mara is "talking to the Moxes' people." V has seen it (AGY-23).
4. Mara's frame finds another means. She hires the Moxes' runner at a worse rate. The delivery is late and a client walks. These are events with causes in Mara's frame (AGY-26). No one blames V (AGY-27).
5. Two days later V passes the clinic. The shutter is half down and a new, cheaper sign is up (shown, not named; AGY-32, AGY-37). V never sees a line about the lost client.
6. Weeks later, the fixer offers a different job, because the world changed (a cause after the lapse, AGY-14). He does not open with wet work, because V has declined it before. That is an adaptation: silent (AGY-42).

The audit (M-1) passes: every effect fell on Mara's stake; no text blamed V; each trigger cites a frame, not V's absence; a portent was exposed.

### 6.2 A delayed, named consequence

V lets a courier go in a warehouse gig instead of handing her over. At the act, the courier says she will find a way to repay it (the `expect` cue, AGY-34, with a coarse timeframe of "a while", and a favour lag from DRV-19).

Nine days later V walks past a stall. The vendor, who is the courier's sister (a recorded tie), offers a better price on a repair kit. The line names the act, plainly: "You're the one who walked my sister out of that warehouse." It does not say why V did it (AGY-35). The attribution check passed first: V's act precedes the effect; the path from act to price is two events; no rival cause (AGY-33). The ledger records `named`. The telling budget for the scene is spent, so no one else mentions it (AGY-37).

## 7. Measures

For P9. Each says what it detects.

| # | Measure | How | Detects |
|---|---|---|---|

| M-2 | **Two-policy replay** | From one saved state, run V policies that take different options at each fork; compare records, cast and scene content | False choices, quantum ogres (P-1) |

## 8. Tensions

| Tension | One side | The other side | How to settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consequence or no penalty (map) | A decline resolves into a consequence, never a null | Declining should carry no penalty; timed failure is controlling | AGY-26: the stake resolves without V; a four-part test separates the two |
| Forced choice or honesty (map) | Forcing gives a felt sense of control | It sours trust when found; bar 4 forbids deciding regardless | AGY-07: forcing only for low-stakes framing; structural tests as the measure |
| Hidden adaptation or visible responsiveness (map) | Personalisation works best unseen | Players feel noticed when they see a response | AGY-42: consequences named, adaptations silent |
| Acknowledge cheaply or deliver the real thing | Acknowledgement kept most felt agency in a short test (AGY-04) | Bar 4, and the cheap route is the quantum ogre | Acknowledge always; mark flavour choices; require records for consequential ones |
| Name the act or do not over-explain | A consequence is credited when the act is named | Telling too much costs relevance and discovery | AGY-37: show first, one named act per scene, proportionate |
| Show clocks or never name the move | Visible variables engage | Naming mechanisms breaks the fiction | AGY-48: visible when V can act on it, never named in speech |
| Fronts that press or freedom from being hounded | Fronts advance whether or not V is there | Pressure triggers reactance | AGY-22, 23, 14: advance in the world, warn first, offer once. Dossier 7's T-5 and DIR-37 agree: a full clock changes the world, and rest never blocks pull |
| Far, unexpected callbacks or attributable ones | A far consequence is the strongest | Delay weakens causal attribution | AGY-34, 40: plant the expectation early; cue grows with time |
| Neutral guide or counter-steering | The guide leaves the choice to the person | Even the Exercises push against an attachment | AGY-20: the engine does not counter-steer, as it has no model of V's disposition |
| Reflecting back or not presuming | Echoing a person's acts is powerful | It risks profiling and presuming | AGY-44: acts, not identity; at least three; observation by a witness |
| Perfect memory or human forgetting | A contradiction teaches "the world forgets" | Humans forget, and a little misremembering is charming | AGY-39: forget by rule, in character, never contradict a witnessed event |
| The world moves on or V's time is respected | Fronts should be real | A stopwatch makes V's time the cost | AGY-25: settle at joints, lazily |
| Calibrate to what V wants or do not shield V | Perceived agency is higher for wanted outcomes | Quality bar 1 | AGY-03: desire shapes where consequences land, not whether bad ones happen |
| Offer proactively or let V pull | Offers must reach V | Unrequested help can lower autonomy | AGY-19, 21: in-world first rung, once, tied to the dial |

## 9. Lateral links

Comparisons only ([D-21](../decisions.md)).

- A **front** is the engine's **group goal frame with its steps**; a portent is a step another actor can perceive.
- A **clock** is a **derived view** over the record ([DRV-12](d01-motivation-drives.md)). A tug-of-war clock is two groups' frames feeding one view.
- **Soft move, then hard move** is the **warning state before a failed state** ([QST-14](d04-quest-structure.md)).
- A **decline** is a typed event ([S0 §9.2](../spec/kernel.md#92-events)) and the client's frame stays `blocked` ([S0 §10.4](../spec/kernel.md#104-lifecycle)).
- The **exposure ledger** is the engine's form of what a service blueprint calls the **line of visibility**: observed pins against unobserved ones.
- **Accept the offer** is **pin the act as observed**; **agree** is another frame's goal being met.
- **Choice blindness** is the reason the record stores **what V did** and never why (map PER-12).
- The **three-clue rule** is a conclusion node with **three in-edges**; node-based design is a graph in which every node has several.
- West Marches' **session summary** is the **V chronicle**, and its rumours are **belief travel**.
- A **quantum ogre** is a **seed cast without regard to V's route**: a casting that ignores the observed state, which late binding forbids.
- The **trace** is the engine's form of **adjudicating in the open**.
- An **adaptation** is a **belief-caused** record and a **consequence** an **event-caused** one.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Does naming the act raise attribution without lowering immersion?** No study found. A/B at M-3: the same consequence shown only, and shown and named.
2. **How long can a consequence be delayed before attribution fails, even with a cue?** The laboratory delays are seconds. Test in a long session with delay buckets of an hour, a day and a week of play.
3. **What share of consequences must V see for global agency to be felt?** Local agency (within a scene) is cheap. Global agency is not measured.
4. **Does "a decline is safe" make stakes feel hollow?** The opposite risk to punishment. Measure whether players read declined offers as having mattered (M-1 with an interview).
5. **Do players detect silent adaptation, and does finding it sour trust?** MOT-05's open boundary. A prototype study.
6. **Which channels can report that V perceived something?** P8 and the game port decide it (dossier 20; see also DIR-60). Without it AGY-16 rests on proxies.
7. **Does pay in an offer lower later voluntary engagement?** The reward literature is laboratory-based. A withdrawal design could test gigs with and without pay.
8. **What grain should a clock have?** Four, six or eight segments is a human reading convention; the engine's progress is continuous. Test legibility at each grain.
9. **How much of a front's progress should V see?** AGY-48's rule is a guess. Compare visible and portent-only presentation.
10. **Should a non-player character ever voice disappointment at a decline?** BND-19 says rarely. How rarely?
11. **What do players do with a readable record of what the world has noted?** Does it raise trust or encourage gaming it?
12. **Does the decline path interact with save and reload?** Declining, reloading and accepting is the fishing behaviour SAV-01 describes. M-10 detects it; whether it matters is for the persistence dossier.
