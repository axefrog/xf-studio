## In brief

- **Most simulated events will never be seen, and that is the design, not a flaw.** If the city has about 7,000 block-sized cells (a guess; nobody has summed the streaming census), 300 small incidents a game day put one in V's cell about once in three game weeks on average, and about once a day in a cell twenty times hotter than average. The game's own authored events (dynamic events, scanner hustles, gigs, combat events) are what V mostly meets. The engine's city events earn their keep elsewhere: as traces, as state that the director and the sifter can read, as news and rumour, and as the causes of the few events V does meet. So the generator is judged by the *record* it leaves, not by scenes.
- **One generator can serve every tier: summaries first, items on demand.** The kernel already has the right shape: a summary (kind, period, scope, count) whose items resolve lazily and identically ([S0 §9.3](../spec/kernel.md#93-summaries-and-their-refinement)). If every incident is first a count in a cell and period, then fine detail is just item materialisation, the counts agree at every tier by construction, and a reload cannot change them. Fine tiers add *responses and witnesses*, never new counts. This needs no kernel change, only two clarifications (§11.2).
- **Use the cluster form of a self-exciting process, not the intensity form.** Hawkes and Oakes showed that a stationary self-exciting process can be read as immigrants plus their descendants. In that form each event carries its own offspring, with a delay, a place, a kind and a size drawn when it is born. That gives the sifter what it lacks (dossier 6, G-1 to G-7): explicit cause links, and chains of "because of X three days ago". The intensity form hides the parents.
- **Concentration is mostly a property of the place, so build it in as a stable propensity.** Across studies about half of incidents fall on about 5 % of street segments. A keyed log-normal propensity with spread σ ≈ 1.6 reproduces that (48 % of mass in the top 5 % of cells, 26 % in the top 1.25 %, which I computed). Hot spots that *move* come from the self-exciting term and from small-number noise: in the one model I read in full, wandering hot spots appear when there are few events, and fixed ones when there are many.
- **Spread has four channels with four speeds,** and the evidence supports different shapes for each. Space: near-repeat risk within about 200 m for at least two weeks in burglary data from five countries. Group ties: gun violence in one large study followed social ties more than streets. Information: a rumour reaches a bounded share of a district, about 80 % when mixing is complete. Systems: failures cascade through coupled networks. Every distance and time here is [J] until the game's clock pace is measured; nothing in our files gives the ratio of game time to real time.
- **Suppression has three outcomes and a trap.** After a block is cleared, the best-supported odds are about 26 % displacement, 27 % diffusion of benefit, and the rest no spillover, with displaced volume smaller than the gain. The trap is feedback: a system that sends police where records are, and records where police are, locks itself in. The engine keeps *occurrence* and *report* apart and bounds police attention (SPR-10).
- **Reload safety is mostly arithmetic discipline.** Draws keyed by (window, scope, kind, index); state changed only at window boundaries; V's acts entering at the next boundary; no `exp` in any decision (the kernel has none, §1.3), so slow state decays by a per-window retention constant, counts come from `ln`-based spacings and delays from tables; the pending-offspring calendar persisted as process state, and *read as the hot state itself*, so there is no second record of excitement to drift; knowledge of an event by a resident decided by a fixed per-resident uniform against a non-decreasing reach.
- **The evidence is for mechanisms, not for numbers.** The criminology is mostly burglary, shootings and gang violence in real cities. It gives shapes (concentration, decay, bounded cascades, displacement odds) and honest failure modes. It gives no constants for a fictional city, and the one gang-violence reading I found was a summary. Section 7 lists the checks that turn judgement values into measured ones.

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

Layer 1 of the engine ([plan §2](../plan.md#2-the-system-at-first-level)) must keep the whole city alive when V is not looking, cheaply, and in a way the other layers can read. The game helps less than it seems to. Its feed has about a dozen kinds of event, mostly about V ([game events §1](../../../knowledge/game-events.md#1-the-short-answers)); its ambient incidents exist only where the world is streamed in; and the gap between the promised living city and the shipped one is a known failure (§2, F-2). So the record of everyone else's life, and the causes behind it, are S1's to produce. This dossier asks: *what events happen, where, when and how often; how does an event raise the odds of the next; how do consequences spread across places and groups and how fast; what is kept at each tier; and what must survive a reload unchanged?*

### 2.2 What they have in common

Three things recur. First, **a model of records stood in for a model of events** (F-1, F-6). Second, **consequence without a visible path** (F-2, F-4, F-5): the world reacted at V, or to V, from nowhere. Third, **unpriced loops** (F-3, F-7): a reinforcing term with no sink and no cap. The design below answers each: the generator is the world's own, with causes recorded; realisation travels; and every loop has a cap and a sink.

### 2.3 Where the advice itself fails

- **Routine activity theory is silent on motive.** It says where trouble can happen, not why anyone wants to cause it. In the city, motive must come from blocked contexts, debts, feuds and group orders ([foundation §5](../foundation.md#5-context-and-relevance)); the opportunity model only places and times the acts that something else wants. "Capable guardian" is slippery too.
- **Crime pattern theory assumes a walkable plane** and a static awareness space. Night City is vertical and transit-dependent; polygons carry a floor and a ceiling ([places §3.2](../../../knowledge/places.md#32-how-the-game-knows-where-v-is)).
- **Self-exciting models fit real data and do not explain it.** The kernel shapes are fitted curves. Nothing says a fictional gang war follows them; they are a way to produce plausible clustering at a cost the engine can pay.
- **Mobility results come from phone records and banknote tracking** in particular countries and years. The 93 % figure is an upper bound on *potential* predictability.
- **Urban scaling is contested.** An England and Wales re-analysis found exponents shift with how a city is defined and that most indicators scale about linearly (Arcaute and colleagues, lens). Sub-districts of 0.5 to 3 km² are far below the metropolitan scale the law was fitted at.
- **Epstein's civil-violence model is a demonstration** that simple rules can give bursts; it has no groups, ties or leaders.
- **The hot-spot policing evidence is about policing,** a small but significant crime reduction with little displacement (Braga and colleagues 2019, abstract). It says little about gang turf.

## 5. Models the engine could run

Everything here is a candidate for S1. Values marked **[J]** are starting points. Times are in **game time**, because nothing in our files gives the ratio of game time to real time (open question 1). Rates are per game day for the whole city unless stated.

### 5.1 The processes and what each tier holds

Five S1 processes carry the city's events. All share the kernel's window, `W_sim` = 600 s ([S0 §9.4](../spec/kernel.md#94-evolution)), and all run once per six windows (one game hour), computing windows k to k + 5 **window by window** so that the period is only a batching choice (REL-02). Processes do not see each other's outputs within a span, so coupling between them has a one-span lag, an hour.

| Process | Tier | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `city.shape` | 0 | The clock; mirrored weather; the log | The hour-of-day and weekday shapes (pure functions of time); regimes (start, exit); the city budget gate for large events (STY-03); the flow matrix when populations change |
| `city.events` | 1 and 2 | Opportunity fields, propensity, shapes, regimes, tensions, police pressure (last span) | Per window and scope: summaries (m1) and events (m2 and above); the pending calendar; band pins; the log ring |
| `city.tension` | 1 | Events of the last span; group strength; the game feed | Pair tensions T<sub>AB</sub>; group strength stocks; district grievance, police pressure and unrest share; the slow district variables (weekly step) |
| `city.flow` | 0 and 1 | Flows, events, regimes | Rumour fractions per district and item; infrastructure loads; regimes from cascades |
| `city.mirror` | 1 | The game feed (S5): deaths, heat, combat, district, journal facts, hustle and gig facts | Events from game readings (TIE-05); the *game-owned* flag per cell |

**Tiers 0 to 2 run everywhere, always, and catch up after an absence** (TIE-07). They hold counts, states and the pending calendar. **Tiers 3 to 5 never catch up.** They hold nothing of their own about incidents: when a root's focus reaches a cell, S1 materialises the items of its summaries that fall inside the focus (silently, [S0 §9.3](../spec/kernel.md#93-summaries-and-their-refinement)), picks the people from the cell's cast, and runs the response policies. The people are drawn from the game's communities for that cell; named persons only if the protected-set rule allows (TIE-09).

### 5.3 Opportunity: the three fields from the place dimensions

For a cell c, kind k and period p, the **opportunity** is

```
A(c,k,p) = T(c,k,p) × ( b_k + O(c,k,p) ) × ( 1 − G(c,k,p) ) × ε(c,k)
```

where T is targets, O offenders, G guardians (each 0 to 1 except T), b<sub>k</sub> a small flat base term, the same in every district, for opportunists who belong to no group (it is the only offender term not tied to an affiliation, and it is deliberately uniform, OPP-05), and ε the stable propensity (§5.4). The **district total** for a kind is

```
Λ(d,k) = R_k × Σ_{c in d} A(c,k,p) / Σ_{c in city} A(c,k,p)
```

with R<sub>k</sub> the city's daily rate for the kind (a share of the m1 total, §5.2), times the hour shape and the city modulators. One number per kind sets the pace; the fields set where. The city dial of STY-05 scales R<sub>k</sub> and the mean offspring together.

Each field is a query over the place dimensions of [places §12](../../../knowledge/places.md#12-place-dimensions-for-the-world-index-w1):

| Term | Place dimensions (number) | How |
|---|---|---|
| Offenders O | Gang presence by area (16), affiliation roles (OPP-04) | The role-weighted share of the area's entry quantity; for group kinds the pair index Σ s<sub>A</sub>s<sub>B</sub> over groups present, raised on border cells |
| Targets T | Population by period (18), style and wealth (17), points of interest (21), services (24), spot supply (26) | Period density × a kind-specific weighting of style and of POI variants (a bar raises `assault`; a vendor, `shakedown`; a ripperdoc, `psycho-break`'s local modulator) |
| Guardians G | Police preset (10), police suppression (11), corporate share (16), security installations (20), ambient staging such as guard areas (27) | NCPD share and preset, corporate security share, installed security, and guard areas, times the hour shape for presence |
| Propensity ε | Points of interest (21), indoor flag and restrictions (7, 8), services (24), fast-travel and transit (24), spot supply (26), the cell's group border | §5.4 |
| Realisation | Spots and markings (26), community nodes (the registry), quest footprint (22) | §5.10 |
| Not used | Crime multiplier (12), level scaling (28), region gang list (15) | The multiplier scores V's crimes and is 1.0 almost everywhere. Enemy level is V's. The region list feeds only the map's gang panel |

### 5.4 Stable propensity, hot state and bands

**Stable propensity.** Each cell has a stable propensity, with the draw made in the world index and not at run time:

```
ε(c,k) = F(c,k) × L(c)        F: a bounded weighting of the cell's POI variants,
                               venue rules, services, transit nodes, spot supply,
                               and border share (per kind)
                               L: a keyed draw from a table of log-normal quantiles,
                                  spread σ ≈ 1.6 (constants computed offline, so no `exp`
                                  at run time)
```

With σ = 1.6 the top 5 % of cells hold 48 % of the mass and the top 1.25 % hold 26 % (computed from the normal distribution), against the published bandwidths of about half on 2 to 6 % and a quarter on 0.4 to 1.6 % of street segments (HOT-01). The top cells, at most 64 per district, are the **persistent hot list**; because ε is a function of (world seed, place Id, features), the list is the same after any reload and needs no storage.

**Hot state is read off the generative state, not tracked beside it.** Because offspring are scheduled when their parent is born (§5.5), the future of a cell's excitement is already in the pending calendar. So:

```
heat(c, t) = Σ over pending children due in (t, t+H] at c or its neighbours   of  weight(m, graph distance)
           + Σ over events in (t−H, t]  at c or its neighbours               of  weight(m, graph distance, age bin)
```

with H a week **[J]**, weights from tables, ages binned (no `exp`). A cell is on the **live hot list** if its heat exceeds a floor; the list is the sorted sparse set (by `Id`), capped at 64 per district. Nothing else needs retention constants except the slow states in `city.tension`, whose per-window update is `x ← ρ·x + input` with ρ set directly.

**Bands.** Heat maps to four bands, quiet, elevated, hot and flashpoint, with a higher threshold to enter than to leave and a minimum dwell of one game day. A band slot on the place holds the band; a pin is added only on a band change, valid from that window ([S0 §6.3](../spec/kernel.md#63-time-varying-slots)), and the previous pin ends. The band change is a `pin` Change and reaches interests (a seed that waits for a hot cell hears of it); the heat numbers never leave the process. This keeps the Change stream small and the band readable as an S2 predicate (STY-02).

**The calendar.** The pending children sit in a calendar keyed by due window and ordered by (due window, parent `Id`, index). At each window: drain what is due; for each, apply thinning (AFT-08); a surviving child becomes an item or an event (TIE-02), and draws its own offspring. A parent's size 2 or more makes its children **minted events with `causes` set to the parent** (the promotion rule). Children of an m1 parent stay items; the parent link is kept in the process record, so that when an item is materialised S1 can set its `causes` from the ring (REL-04). For items older than the ring, the cause is left open, which the kernel permits until something needs it.

**The sampling methods, with no `exp`.**
- **Counts.** To draw N from mean μ: take successive keyed uniforms u<sub>1</sub>, u<sub>2</sub>, …; add −ln(u<sub>j</sub>); N is the number of partial sums not exceeding μ. This is the exponential-spacing form of a Poisson draw and uses only `ln` and addition. Cap at 64 per (district, kind, window); a larger μ is split across scopes first.
- **Tables.** Offspring, delay, size and place use inverse cumulative sums over table constants, with exact comparisons and ties to the lower index.
- **Thinning.** Compare a keyed uniform with the ratio of the modifier now to the modifier at birth, capped at 1.

### 5.7 Suppression, displacement and the feedback guard

A **suppression** is any event with effect `protect` or `harm` on an offender group at a hot cell that police, V or a rival performs (a sweep, a clearance, a defeat). It does four things, all keyed by its `Id`:

1. Thins pending children at the cell and its neighbours for a lull of a few days **[J]**.
2. Lowers the relevant pair tensions by a table step.
3. Draws the spillover outcome: displacement 26 % (a fraction of the suppressed weight, below the whole, moves to the best-fit cell within two steps), diffusion 27 % (neighbours' weights also fall) or none.
4. Starts a vacancy timer for a group's removal from a district: while another group's presence in it stays high, a replacement appears after a delay **[J]** (this is a hypothesis for turf, outside the evidence of SPR-07).

**Police attention** (SPR-10) is a district state with a preset anchor. It rises with *reported* incidents above the expected number and returns to the preset. A report is a separate draw from an occurrence: the chance rises with witnesses and with trust in police (an S1 policy of dossier 15). Self-excitation acts on occurrences. The loop of Ensign and colleagues needs the record to feed the model, and here the record only feeds a bounded dial.

**Costs, as an estimate to be measured.** With the §5.2 rates, a game hour holds about 12 m1 incidents, 2 m2 events and a fraction of an m3 event for the whole city. A window's work is the drawn counts, the due children (a few) and the hot-list refresh. The pending calendar holds about 500 entries city-wide in steady state (a rate of about 100 children a day times a mean delay of about 5 days) and is capped at 256 per district. The expensive part is not the run but the **one-off world-index build** of the three fields and ε, which is static data.

**What V will actually meet.** At the §5.2 rate, one cell of about 7,000 sees an m1 incident about once in 23 game days on average, and a cell 20 times hotter about once a day. So most of what the engine simulates will never be seen. It will be read as news, traces and rumour; as the state behind a story window; and as the causes of the few events that do arrive.

**V's act, step by step.** V's act at time t in window k is an external operation stamped in k. It is applied at the boundary ending k, so first processed in window k + 1. Within the window of k + 1 the earliest child it can have is due at k + 2. That is the floor on how fast the city answers: 10 minutes at the default window, and the delay bins make most answers slower.

## 8. Tensions

- **The director would like a story slot; the city does not wait for one.** Coarse history is independent of V (TIE-03), so the director cannot summon an incident. It can choose which of the city's already-happened or pending incidents to realise, delay realisation, and offer around them. Large events are gated by a spacing rule, which is the one place the process reads a pacing policy (STY-03). The director's tension budget (dossier 7) governs offers, not occurrence.
- **Agency against retaliation.** Hazards must not turn into ambushes aimed at V (TIE-06). A group's grudge against V is a belief in the group's perspective, and acting on it is a seed offer V can meet, avoid or decline.
- **The game's spawns against ours.** The game decides what stands in a cell when V arrives. Our group-strength stock and propensities can shape events and news, but they cannot remove the game's gang population from a street until realisation can switch community nodes. The compromise to record: *the world index states what the game spawns; S1's dynamic strength modulates events, news and offers, not the spawned crowd* (open question 4).
- **Canon against drift.** Authored identity (Arasaka's Corpo Plaza, the Valentinos' Heywood) is frozen (SLW-01). Territory changes are events with causes and markers, not statistical drift.
- **Sifter volume against story-bearing events.** Minting everything floods the sifter (F-9). Minting only m2 and above, plus chains, leaves the sifter a world chronicle of manageable size, but a story hiding in a run of m1 incidents is not visible to it. Pairs of m1 incidents between named groups are promoted (TIE-02); beyond that, unseen texture stays unseen.
- **Forgetting against hot state.** Reach and beliefs fade on dossier 11's rules, while the ring keeps seven days of descriptors. A district's gist (reputation, "the Claws lost men here") outlasts the descriptors because it lives in beliefs and carriers, not in the ring.
- **Violence rates against content boundaries.** The kinds include harm. Dossier 18 (not yet written) will decide what a player may switch off. Dials and exclusions (STY-05) are the only mechanism here; the schema-level limits are for that dossier. How offenders are *represented* is a separate audit (OPP-05).
- **Learnable danger against farming.** Concentration makes dangerous corners learnable, which helps legibility. It also lets a player sit at the hottest cell for offers. The answer is realisation budgets, not changing the process.
- **Simplicity against completeness.** Five processes and a matrix is more than a first cut needs. §11.1 gives a priority order; the finish-before-widening instinct applies.
- **Real-city statistics against a fictional city.** The evidence is about burglary, shootings and gang violence in real cities with real policing. Night City's baseline is not that, and players expect it to be dangerous. Shapes transfer; constants are tuned by feel and by the panel measure M14-17.

## 9. Lateral links

- **Hawkes, ETAS, near-repeat, gang retaliation and an epidemic with a reproduction number below 1 are one process in four vocabularies:** immigrants plus offspring, with the offspring mean as n or R. The dossiers' lenses found it twice (L7 and L11); this dossier uses one implementation for all.
- **The pending calendar is a tabletop "clock" or "front" run off-screen** (map field CRA-01), with a draw in place of a visible countdown. The GM's habit of advancing threats while the players are elsewhere is the same idea.
- **The game's own combat events are a renewal process:** a community is killed, a random 12, 24 or 36 game hours pass, and it is re-armed ([open-world §6.4](../../../knowledge/open-world-activities.md#64-combat-events)). The shipped version of an aftershock is the retaliation chase after 16 gigs.
- **The lazy per-resident knowledge draw is the same coupling** as dossier 11's recall draw and the kernel's flat Gumbel-max with fixed per-value noise ([S0 §5.4](../spec/kernel.md#54-the-draw-flat-gumbel-max)): noise that depends only on what is being asked, so that changing a weight changes an answer only where it matters.
- **Bands with hysteresis are alarm-management deadbands** (map field PAC-06).
- **Cause chains are contact tracing,** the third idea L11 took from epidemiology.
- **The game's authored crowd lanes are what space syntax tries to predict** from a street graph; here the authored data replaces the prediction.

## 10. Open questions

1. **What is the ratio of game time to real time?** Every time constant here is in game time. The bridge can read the clock against the simulation clock ([game events §8](../../../knowledge/game-events.md#8-time-skips)); one measurement settles it.
2. **What hours do the named periods cover?** Morning, Day, Evening, Night and Midnight are 93 % of the game's periods and the clock times 7 %; the hour boundaries are not in our files.
3. **What is a cell?** The level-0 streaming cell (about 219 m) is the natural block-sized unit; the other candidates are community areas and clusters of points of interest. The count of cells is unknown.
4. **Can the engine steer the game's ambient incidents?** Whether the bridge can activate or suppress a named community or hustle, and under what capability, decides how much of the city's state can reach view (TIE-04, TIE-05).
5. **How much of this does a player notice?** A panel on transcripts and a short in-game session (§12) are the way to find out, and to decide which processes the first implementation needs.
6. **Is 300 small incidents a day right,** and what share involve named groups? Both are guesses.
7. **Who authors the role table?** The affiliation-by-district roles (OPP-04) are content. Pack authors need a tool and a default.
8. **How should added or modded districts get exposure and propensity** when no authored density exists? Space syntax is a prior (SLW-04), but the vertical city weakens it.
9. **Which of the five processes does the first implementation need?** §11.1 proposes an order and the reasons.
10. **Does the pair-tension state double-count the pending echoes?** Both raise the chance of an A–B event. The design keeps them apart by timescale (tension is slow background, echoes are scheduled children); a fit may show that one of them is enough.
11. **Is a "what has been happening here" view wanted?** A district's recent events as a player-readable panel is a P6 and expression question.
