## What this dossier concludes

1. **A relationship is two records and a shared one, never one number.** Each side keeps its own regard for the other (warmth, respect, trust in three parts, the three commitment variables). A third, shared record holds the tie's kind, public status, depth, history and contact rhythm. V's side is empty by construction ([D-09](../decisions.md)): the engine holds only what each NPC saw, felt and inferred about V.
2. **The incumbent, a visible approval meter fed by gifts and right answers, fails in ways that are well documented.** It makes closeness a purchase, invites optimising and reloading, and it hides the thing that actually builds attachment, which is the number and quality of specific moments. The replacement is not a cleverer number. It is a state that every change cites an act for, quiet numbers, and enough specific moments.
3. **The game's own romances are not meters.** In the quest data there is no affinity or approval fact, and one lone trust flag. Romance is a handful of boolean outcome facts written at named scenes, and *friend* and *lover* (and *had sex*) are separate facts. Judy's intimate scene has no choice nodes. All the choosing comes before it. This is the pattern to keep ([2.4](#24-the-games-own-data-corpus)).
4. **Declining is a first-class outcome, authored and equal.** Every rung of a ladder has named exits (decline, stay friends, lapse) that continue with dignity. A refusal derives from the NPC's own state and persists, and asking again never changes it. The NPC's pursuit of V is rate-limited and deniable.
5. **Absence is not a penalty.** What fades with weeks away is contact rhythm and shared currency, far more than regard, and reconnecting is welcomed more than people expect. The engine derives state from timestamps, gives V a reunion beat, and never uses absence as an engagement lever.
6. **Companions and NPC to NPC ties are story material and a check on realism.** Players read a party whose members have lives with each other as fuller. Ties are generated by closure, watched for imbalance, and tied to memberships, so cross-pressure falls out of the same records.
7. **For R1:** the author fixes the character, the ladder with all its exits, the boundaries and the explicit content. The fitter may choose timing, channel, place, hook and pacing between rungs. V may always decline, ignore, stay at a rung, take the friendship branch, retract, or leave, and no text may state how V feels.

## How to read the grades and the read-status

Principles carry the grades of [P1 section 4](../p1-research-method.md#4-evidence-grades-for-craft-knowledge): **[empirical]**, **[practice]**, **[shipped]**, **[corpus]**, **[analysis]**, **[hypothesis]**. A claim that is contested between sources says **contested**.

Section 2 says how far each source was read: **full** (read the text), **part** (read the relevant sections of the text), **page** (a web page read through the fetch tool's summary, so second-hand to a degree), **abstract**, **summary** (a search summary or a secondary article only), **memory** (not re-read; a pointer). A principle resting on a summary says so. Almost all the psychology here comes from abstracts and secondary summaries, and its effect sizes are not to be relied on beyond direction. Nothing in the psychology was gathered from game players, and nearly all of it was gathered from couples, students or office workers in ordinary conditions. Night City has no empirical base ([dossier 1, open question 8](d01-motivation-drives.md#7-open-questions)).

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

This dossier covers how the engine should **represent, pace, change and protect** the bonds between people: romantic, familial, friendly, professional, rival and mentoring. It also covers the bonds between NPCs, because a world in which every tie runs through V reads as hollow (BND-30). It asks four things:

1. what a relationship is made of, beyond one approval number;
2. how games and the craft around them structure romance, what players criticise, and how consent and the player's agency are handled;
3. how bonds change with time, memory and absence;
4. how companions and NPC to NPC bonds become story material through memberships and cross-pressure.

It informs these parts of the [plan](../plan.md#2-the-system-at-first-level):

| Layer or concern | What this dossier gives it |
|---|---|
| **1. Simulation** (S1, [P3](../plan.md#5-second-level-plans)) | The tie record; the event-to-state policy; decay and reunion policies derived from timestamps; the bid generator; closure and balance checks |
| **2. Seeding** ([P4](../plan.md#5-second-level-plans)) | The vocabulary for ladders, exits, boundaries, pacing, retraction and absence behaviour; the lint rules that keep V's feelings out of text |
| **3. Procedural engine** ([P5](../plan.md#5-second-level-plans)) | When a relationship offer may appear and how often; backoff after silence or a decline; reunion beats |
| **4. Authored content** (P6) | What an author fixes in a romance or a companion arc, and what the fitter may supply |
| Evaluation | Measures for pressure, attribution, balance of exits, and absence handling |
| Ethics | The consent floor as it touches romance (dossier 18 owns the rest) |
| Expression | The "because" slot: a response that shows what an act meant to the NPC |

**What it does not cover.** Group decision procedures (S1; [dossier 1](d01-motivation-drives.md#11-drives-of-groups)); reputation and gossip (dossier 15); the full consent and content-boundary design, including player-set lines and the schema-level limits (dossier 18, ETH-08); the player's own motivation (MOT-01); memory and forgetting in general (SAV-03). Each is cross-referenced where it touches.

**Ground rules from the foundation that bind this dossier.**
- V is observed, never owned ([D-09](../decisions.md), [foundation 5.3](../foundation.md#53-v-observed-never-owned)): the engine never declares, assumes or writes V's feelings, desires or orientation. Content offers.
- Everyone has their own model ([D-10](../decisions.md)): a tie is two perspectives, and a belief about someone else's feelings is a pin in the believer.
- Groups are actors ([D-08](../decisions.md)): family, crew and firm are memberships, and cross-pressure between them is where companion stories come from.
- The engine's concepts here are the project's own. Outside work (SEL-06's systems, Heider, Granovetter) appears as comparison or as evidence about people ([D-21](../decisions.md)).

## 3. Principles

Each principle is an atomic claim. "Operationalise" says whether a procedure could **produce** the property, **check** it, or **neither**. Layer numbers are the [plan's](../plan.md#2-the-system-at-first-level) four layers; "S1" is the simulation policy specification the kernel leaves open ([kernel 0.1](../spec/kernel.md#01-what-the-kernel-is)). Instances are invented for this dossier and are not content. Principle IDs use the prefix `BND` (bonds) so they do not clash with the map's field IDs.

### A. What a relationship is made of

**BND-01. A tie is two perspectives and a shared record. It is never one number.** `[analysis]`, with `[shipped]` instances.
- *Source:* Versu (part: many role evaluations that are multiple and asymmetric, plus one symmetric public relationship state); Comme il Faut (part: directional scalar networks, with an example of respect 30 one way and 85 the other); the RimWorld opinion page (a colonist can see a rival in someone who counts them a friend). Our own base: [D-10](../decisions.md).
- *Instance:* A fixer rates V's reliability highly and trusts V with a small job. V has said nothing about her. There is a fixer-to-V record and no V-to-fixer record.
- *Scales:* encounter, gig, arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Each actor holds its own *regard* for another (its state, true of it), and the tie holds the shared facts (BND-02, BND-09, [section 8](#8-the-tie-record-a-proposal)). For any tie with V at one end, only the NPC's regard for V exists, with the NPC's beliefs about V's acts. No field records V's regard. Unrequited ties are simply an asymmetry of regard. **Check:** a schema test finds no slot named for V's feelings or desires ([D-09](../decisions.md)).

**BND-02. The tie's *kind* decides the rules it runs on.** `[empirical]` for communal against exchange norms; `[analysis]` for the list of kinds.
- *Source:* Clark & Mills 1979 and the Yale lab's later pages (summary: in communal ties benefits answer need and repayment is received badly; in exchange ties comparable repayment is expected); [dossier 1, DRV-03 and DRV-19](d01-motivation-drives.md#a-what-drives-exist-and-how-they-relate); Roberts & Dunbar 2011 (abstract: keeping kin costs less than keeping friends); Comme il Faut (part: relationships are binary and non-exclusive, so a housemate can also be a rival).
- *Instance:* A ripperdoc who says "pay when you can" has moved a market tie towards a communal one. V pays at once with a gift, and the ripperdoc's tone cools a little: the repayment read as a refusal of the favour.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `kind` is one or more of: kin, partner, friend, professional (exchange), patron and client, mentor and protégé, rival, enemy, acquaintance. Kinds can co-exist on one tie. The kind selects the reciprocity rule, the decay and maintenance profile (BND-24), the bid library (BND-06), which public statuses exist (BND-09) and which exits are possible. **Check:** an untyped tie is a defect; a communal tie never takes a "repay at once" bonus.

**BND-03. Commitment has three inputs: satisfaction, investment and alternatives.** `[empirical]`; the evidence is mostly romantic and correlational, and transfer to other kinds is untested.
- *Source:* Tran, Judge & Kashima 2019 (abstract: 202 samples, 50,427 people; satisfaction r = .65, investments .53, alternatives -.43); Le & Agnew 2003 (citation); Rusbult 1980 (memory).
- *Instance:* An NPC partner's satisfaction comes from the recent run of good and bad evidence. Investment comes from time, history and a shared flat. Alternatives come from another person who has been making bids for months.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `commitment(A→B)` is derived from `satisfaction`, `investment` and `alternatives`, all A's own. Only the order of the weights is taken from the evidence (satisfaction above investment above alternatives); the numbers are **[J]** for fixtures. Satisfaction is a summary of recent evidence (BND-12). Investment is shared history, shared time and favours given. Alternatives are A's other ties of the same kind that are available and attractive. The same derived form feeds a membership's `strength` ([dossier 1, DRV-38](d01-motivation-drives.md#e-groups)), so a tie and a membership share one commitment model. **Check:** raising only alternatives lowers commitment; raising only investment raises it. **Caution:** sunk-cost hooks are an exploitation risk; commitment is never used to trap V (BND-26).

**BND-04. Trust has parts, and a breach has a kind that decides what repairs it.** `[empirical]` for the parts and the repair result; the *speed* asymmetry (fast to lose, slow to build) is `[empirical]` but **contested**.
- *Source:* Mayer, Davis & Schoorman 1995 (summary: ability, benevolence, integrity); Kim, Ferrin, Cooper & Dirks 2004 (summary: apology repairs a competence breach better, denial an integrity breach, and trust is hard to repair once a breach is read as an integrity failure); Slovic 1993 and the later work that found the asymmetry partly holds only (summary).
- *Instance:* A courier whose delivery V botched forgives V after an apology. A courier who learns that V sold the route to a rival does not, and later good acts count for less.
- *Scales:* gig, arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `trust(A→B)` is three scalars with an evidence count. A breach event carries the attribution (competence, benevolence or integrity) that A makes of it, through dossier 1's attribution policy, and that attribution decides which scalar falls and which repair acts work. A per-actor gain on loss against gain sits in a range such as 1 to 2 **[J]**; it is a trait, not a law. **Check:** the same breach under different attributions gives different recovery curves.

**BND-05. Closeness grows by reciprocal disclosure that the other side *responds to*, and by a lot of shared time. It is a pacing heuristic, not a ladder to be climbed.** `[empirical]` for the effect; `[analysis]` for the onion, with **critics**.
- *Source:* Aron et al. 1997 (abstract: escalating mutual disclosure raised closeness against small talk, and the closeness was temporary); Laurenceau et al. 1998 and 2005 (summary: perceived responsiveness partly mediates disclosure's effect on intimacy); Reis, Clark & Holmes 2004 (summary); Hall 2018 (summary: roughly 50, then 90, then 200 or more hours of shared time, with work time counting less); Altman & Taylor 1973 (memory) with Petronio's objection that disclosure and closeness are not the same (summary).
- *Instance:* A bartender volunteers that she once ran with a crew only after V has listened to a joke that fell flat and said nothing unkind. Disclosure plus a responsive reply moved the tie one rung. A disclosure two rungs up, with no such reply, would read as a script.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A tie holds `depth` (a small ordinal, five steps or fewer) and `breadth` (the topics opened). An NPC volunteers a disclosure at depth d+1 only after V's own act at depth d and an observed responsive reply; a crisis event may let it skip a rung (people skip rungs under stress). **Pacing governs what the NPC volunteers. It never limits what V may ask, decline or leave.** Shared time accrues by activity class, with leisure above work, so a fixer's gigs build professional trust (BND-04) faster than they build friendship. **Check:** an A/B test of earned against timed disclosure (lens L12); no NPC volunteers more than one rung up without a flagged crisis.

**BND-06. The daily currency of a tie is small bids and the replies to them.** `[empirical]` in outline, with the headline numbers unchecked and the predictive claims **contested**; transfer beyond couples is untested.
- *Source:* Driver & Gottman 2004 and the Gottman Institute (summary: a bid, and turning toward, away or against it); Heyman & Slep 2001 (abstract: prediction equations must be cross-validated, and accuracy fell when they were); Gable et al. 2004 (summary: active, constructive replies to good news went with better relationship outcomes).
- *Instance:* A bartender asks how a job went. A curt answer still turns toward her. Ten ignored questions later, she stops asking.
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3, expression.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Each NPC has a low **bid rate** (a trait) and a bid library by tie kind (a check-in, a request, news to share, an invitation). V's reply is classified toward, away or against from what V does, including doing nothing within a stated window. A bid that V has had no chance to answer (V was away, in combat, or the channel was shut) is not a rebuff (BND-26). **Check:** over a long run, a tie whose bids are mostly turned toward gains satisfaction; none is lost to a missed chat.

**BND-07. A new shared activity lifts a tie more than repeating a liked one.** `[empirical]`, short-term in the lab.
- *Source:* Aron et al. 2000 (summary: correlational and three lab experiments; a seven-minute novel, arousing task beat a mundane one; the boost was measured soon after).
- *Instance:* A heist or a trip into a district neither knows beats another routine delivery for the same pay.
- *Scales:* gig, arc. *Layers:* 1, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce** a `novelty` term: the time since the pair last did an activity of a new class, which decays. The procedural engine may prefer to offer a novel shared job to a flagging tie. **Check:** two ties with equal contact: the one with a recent novel activity has the higher satisfaction. Keep it small; the evidence is a short-term effect.

**BND-08. People feel understood when they see what their act meant to the other. A line that restates a log entry is data, not responsiveness.** `[empirical]` for perceived responsiveness; the second sentence is the map's own note on PER-06.
- *Source:* Reis, Clark & Holmes 2004 and Laurenceau et al. (summary); map PER-06.
- *Instance:* "You remembered I don't take sugar" is data. "You came when you said you would, and last time nobody did" shows why the act mattered.
- *Scales:* encounter. *Layers:* 3, 4, expression.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** A response template has a required `because` slot that names the NPC's own concern (dossier 1) that V's act touched. A response with no `because` is a defect in review.

**BND-09. Keep the tie's public status apart from the private variables, and let every change to it need the choices of both sides.** `[shipped]`, one rule from a research system and one from the game's data.
- *Source:* Versu (part: a single symmetric public relationship state, such as friends, lovers or enemies, apart from asymmetric evaluations; design rules that relationship state changes need active choice from both characters, that an NPC can never change its relationship to the player unilaterally, and that the change is marked clearly); the quest data (2.4: friend, lover and sex are separate facts).
- *Instance:* An NPC's warmth has been rising for weeks, but the tie does not become "partner" until V does something that says so, and the NPC's own state allows it.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 2.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `status` is a small discrete set per kind (acquaintance, friend, partner, ex, rival, enemy, mentor, and so on). A transition that involves V needs an observed V act and the NPC's acceptance by its own state. NPC to NPC transitions are autonomous. The NPC may offer V a fork (stay, or part), never a silent change. **Check:** no code path moves a status involving V without an observed V act.

### B. Approval systems and what replaces them

**BND-10. A visible number bought with gifts and right answers turns closeness into a purchase.** `[practice]` and `[analysis]` as criticism; **contested** as to the best replacement.
- *Source:* Murthy 2018 (page: affinity meters as vending machines; dialogue trees as pick-up logic); Clough, GDC 2022 and 2024 (summary: "kindness coins" against chemistry); Tomlinson 2024 (full: players object that romance is boiled down to an on-demand system, and that a "button press" reduces a character to the player's wish); Clark & Mills (summary: in communal ties, repayment is read as refusing the relationship); Stardew Valley wiki (summary: gifts are the lever and each heart is 250 points).
- *Instance:* A game where one preferred gift per week keeps a heart meter full is a ledger. A friend who treats a gift as a payment has stopped being a friend.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **check and produce.** No tie variable rises by a flat amount for an item. A gift is an act whose effect is read through BND-08: what it shows about V's attention to *this person's* concerns. In a communal tie, prompt repayment is a mild negative (BND-02). **Check:** a sweep of event policies finds no `+N for item` rule.

**BND-11. Attachment follows the number and quality of authored moments with a character, more than the character's stated traits.** `[shipped]`, as a developer's natural experiment.
- *Source:* Telltale at GDC 2013 (page: about 25 % saved Doug and 75 % saved Carley; Carley had many more chances to build a relationship with the player and Doug mostly stood aside; the developers' reading, not a measured test).
- *Instance:* Two NPCs of equal charm: one rescued V in a scene and shared a hard night, the other waved from across the room. V will choose the first when forced.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** The promotion ladder (NAR-18) gates how many *specific, tie-citing* moments a character gets: a character V has met three times has three remembered moments, not a trait list. A generated ladder must reach a minimum count of tie-specific moments before it offers an exit with weight. **Check:** count tie-specific moments per rung in a fit report.

**BND-12. Every change to a tie cites an act the player can name, and the numbers stay quiet.** `[hypothesis]`, from the lens and the map's agency field (MOT-04).
- *Source:* Lens L12 (untested predictions: a visible scalar produces optimising and reloads; changes the player cannot attribute to an act feel unfair); map MOT-04.
- *Instance:* "She's quieter since you left the party early" rather than "approval -3".
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, expression, tooling.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Each state change records its cause event (kernel provenance). The surface shows the NPC's behaviour and words, not the number, by default. Traces show the numbers to agents and authors ([DS-26](../design-seeds.md)). **Check:** a "why did she say that?" trace always resolves to an event V can recall.

**BND-13. Friction and rivalry are content, not a penalty.** `[shipped]`; the replacement has a **critique**.
- *Source:* Destructoid interview with Mike Laidlaw (page: Dragon Age II tried to remove "negative" standing, so disagreeing builds rivalry, both ends of the scale open content, and rivalry could still be romanced); the interviewer's worry that this made every choice weigh the same.
- *Instance:* A crew member who dislikes V's mercy but respects V's nerve becomes a sparring partner with her own scenes, not a locked door.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Disagreement moves the tie's *texture* (friction, rivalry salience, respect) and opens its own content. Only a breach that attributes to integrity (BND-04) or a crossed boundary (BND-18) closes anything. **Check:** both ends of a disagreement offer distinct content; nothing unique is lost for being disliked, short of a breach.

### C. Romance, consent and the player's agency

**BND-14. Who a character is open to is the character's own trait, declared by the author. The engine never infers V's desire, and a link to V's presentation is an explicit authored choice.** `[practice]`; players are **split** on the best model.
- *Source:* Tomlinson 2024 (full: Dragon Age II and Baldur's Gate 3 favour playersexual romance, Dragon Age: Inquisition added set orientations; some players call playersexuality inclusive, others call it flat; the paper concludes that most players want romance that feels real; the Veilguard comments are in a secondary report only); CD PROJEKT RED on Judy (page: the studio says Judy was only ever a romance for feminine V, its design intent, while recording both voices for production convenience); the game data (2.4: availability facts exist and their writers are not in the quest data).
- *Instance:* The author states that a character is open to a feminine or masculine V, or to either. Takemura's author writes that openness once, with a reason, and the engine does not guess.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 4, evaluation.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** Openness is a trait on the NPC's record with a declared dependency, if any, on V's *presentation* (an observed character-creator fact), never on a guess about V's orientation. The seed's capability lint flags such a dependency so the author sees it. Generated NPCs get openness from the population draw with no demographic stereotype (DRV-11). **Check:** an audit of generated openness by demographic tag; no code path reads V's body to infer attraction.

**BND-15. A romance is a path with exits at every step, and the decline is authored with the same care as the acceptance.** `[shipped]`, `[corpus]`, and `[hypothesis]` for the measured effect.
- *Source:* The quest data (2.4: sibling flags `friendship`, `romance`, `broken` for Judy; `friend`, `relationship`, `dislike` for Kerry); Dragon Age II (page, BND-13); Versu (BND-09); lens L12 (REL-04: offers a companion can plausibly decline read as more believable, and a point-of-no-return prompt raises reported pressure, both untested); Larian (page: romances no longer lock in during one party night, and a player can pursue some party members and others in the world).
- *Instance:* At the third rung V can say yes, say "not now", say "I value you as you are", or say nothing. Each has a next scene.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** A ladder declares its rungs and at least three exits: **decline**, **stay as friends** (or the current kind), and **lapse** (nothing was said). Each exit is a status with an authored coda and a continuation, and the seed's fit report lists all of them. **Check:** an exit with no authored content is a defect; no rung has a "confirm" dialog worded as a point of no return.

**BND-16. Intimacy is a beginning, not a prize, and "had sex" is a different fact from "is my partner".** `[shipped]`, `[corpus]`.
- *Source:* Inverse (page: Larian's lead designer says sex is where a relationship begins, so players can start early and watch it evolve); the quest data (2.4: `sq030_sex_done` separate from `sq030_judy_lover`, `sq029_river_had_sex` separate from `sq029_river_lover`).
- *Instance:* A night together does not make anyone a couple. The morning after can go several ways, each authored.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** An intimate scene writes a scene-level fact in the seed's namespace; tie status changes only through BND-09. **Check:** no seed sets `partner` from an intimacy fact alone.

**BND-17. Choices come before the intimate scene, and the scene itself is authored.** `[corpus]` for the pattern; `[practice]` for the rule.
- *Source:* The quest data (2.4: Judy's pier scene has 19 choice nodes and the intimate scene 0); lens L10 (principle: explicit content is authored, procedural templates may offer an intimacy slot, and no template emits explicit-tagged assets); Larian's intimacy coordinators (summary; Larian's own "one of the first" claim).
- *Instance:* All the asking, hesitating and declining happens in conversation. The scene that follows has nothing left to decide.
- *Scales:* encounter. *Layers:* 2, 4, ethics.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** The seed language has an `intimate` scene class that only a human-authored pack scene can fill, that carries a content tag the player's settings must permit (the game reads such settings itself, 2.4), and that has a declared fade or veil alternative. The procedural engine never generates one. This is the floor for [ETH-08](map.md); dossier 18 keeps the rest. **Check:** a static scan finds no generated scene with an explicit tag.

**BND-18. A refusal comes from the NPC's own state, persists, and is never flipped by asking again.** `[practice]`.
- *Source:* Lens L10 (principle 2: no code path where repetition changes the outcome); Inverse (page: Astarion steps back from sex because of his history; Karlach's discomfort with touch is written on purpose); lens L12 (a persistent boundary read as a trait).
- *Instance:* A character who has said "not like that" does not agree on the fifth request. Her boundary may change through an event the author wrote (she was given a reason to trust), not through a counter.
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 1, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A boundary is a pin on the NPC (a trait with a reason) or on the tie (`declined(rung)`), valid until an authored event revises it. Acceptance is a function of the NPC's state, not of a count of attempts. **Check:** a replay with the same V act repeated N times gives the same answer for every N.

**BND-19. An NPC's pursuit of V is a high-risk move: keep it low in pressure, deniable and rate-limited, and meet a no with dignity.** `[empirical]` (qualitative, one study); the mechanism of reactance is `[empirical]` elsewhere (MOT-01).
- *Source:* Tomlinson 2024 (full: persistence and perceived "thirst" steered many players away from Lae'zel and Gale; companions who initiate and become distressed if rejected felt uncomfortable; the paper calls a character's pursuit of an uninterested player realistic but uncomfortable); Inverse (page: Larian reduced some date talk about exes after player feedback); Versu (BND-09: no unilateral change).
- *Instance:* A character lets V know she enjoys V's company, once, in a way that reads as friendship if V wants it to. If V doesn't answer in kind she does not repeat it.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A pursuit budget: at most one romantic bid per rung per window, none after a decline or after silence, and each bid worded so that it can be taken as friendly. A decline or silence moves the NPC to a quiet standing openness ("the door stays unlocked") that V can open. Distress on rejection is rendered as self-contained sadness at most, never as a lever on V. **Check:** a count of NPC-initiated romantic bids to a V who has not answered one is zero after the first window; a reject-then-guilt template is a defect.

**BND-20. Never presume V's feelings. Offer a span of stances, and let the NPC read V's *act*, not V's heart.** `[hypothesis]`, under [D-09](../decisions.md) and [FIT-49](d03-fitting-casting.md).
- *Source:* [Foundation 5.3](../foundation.md#53-v-observed-never-owned); the reflection in Tomlinson 2024 (full: players role-play their own V, and what is flat to one is free to another); CD PROJEKT RED's choice to let V's side of a romance be chosen at scenes (2.4).
- *Instance:* The options at a rung are warm, wary, businesslike and kind-but-no. The NPC's reply is to what V did and may be wrong about why, and that is fine.
- *Scales:* encounter. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4, evaluation.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** A line set at a decision spans at least four stances including a decline. NPC beliefs about V are about acts, with a confidence and an optional guess at a reason. A text linter rejects player-facing text that asserts V's inner state ("you love him", "you feel safe"). **Check:** the linter plus a count of stances per offer.

**BND-21. Other romances are not a gate. They are a fact the NPC may have a view about.** `[shipped]`.
- *Source:* TheGamer (page: Mazur says the messages that acknowledge several love interests are intentional, with no long-term consequences, and that the team sets no target number of romances); the Witcher 3 coverage (summary: Triss and Yennefer make Geralt pay for romancing both, and CD PROJEKT RED added dialogue after launch because choosing Triss was under-represented); Inverse (page: Larian changed its system so a player can pursue several); the quest data (2.4: the roof scene reads all four lover facts).
- *Instance:* V already has a partner by the game's flag. A new character may react in her own way, but V is not locked out.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** V's other partner facts select a *variant* of the offer and may colour the NPC's reply; they never decide whether V may be offered or may accept. The author states the NPC's own stance (BND-14). **Check:** no seed gates an offer on a partner fact.

**BND-22. A one-input retract removes an offer or a thread without questions, and the player can see and edit what is excluded.** `[practice]`.
- *Source:* Lens L10 (the X-card, lines and veils; principles 2 and 3 of tabletop safety tools, with the criticisms that the X-card is reactive and that long checklists push players to opt out of anything interesting); the map's note on REL-04.
- *Instance:* V presses one key and a thread of NPC bids stops, with no prompt. The thread is listed in a settings page as "paused by you", and V can revive it.
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 2, 3, player configuration.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A `retract` action tags the current offer, thread or kind as soft-excluded, records it, and withdraws the seed quietly. Nothing is penalised. Dossier 18 owns the full design. **Check:** no confirmation dialog; effect immediate; the exclusion list is readable and editable.

### D. Time, memory and absence

**BND-23. Derive relationship state from timestamped facts. Do not tick it.** `[hypothesis]`, aligned with the kernel and with [DRV-12](d01-motivation-drives.md#c-urgency).
- *Source:* [Kernel 9.4](../spec/kernel.md#94-evolution): coarse tiers catch up after an absence; fine tiers never do and are "generated from the coarse state, consistent with the pins".
- *Instance:* V returns after a month. The friendship's currency is a function of the last contact, the elapsed time, the tie's kind and the events the NPC logged. Nothing needs to have been simulated minute by minute.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1).
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `quality(t)` is a sum of event contributions with a decay (a power law on time since the event, boosted by salience, in the manner of SAV-03), and `currency(t)` is a function of `t - last_contact`, the kind and the tie's maintenance style (BND-24). S1 writes a pin only when a bucket changes. **Check:** replaying from the seed and the pins gives the same state at any time; jumping and ticking agree.

**BND-24. Absence mainly wears down contact rhythm and shared currency, not regard. Kin ties cost less to keep than friendships, and effort offsets decline.** `[empirical]`, with a small, narrow base.
- *Source:* Roberts & Dunbar 2011 (abstract: 251 women; kin kept with less contact than friends) and 2015 (abstract: friendships weakened over 18 months across a life transition while family ties behaved differently, effort offset decline, and the protective effort differed by sex); Levin, Walter & Murnighan 2011 (abstract: dormant ties kept trust and shared perspective).
- *Instance:* V does not see a sister for six months and the sister still treats V as family. V does not see a drinking friend for six months and the friend is warm but has to catch up.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1).
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Two components: `regard` (slow to move, set by evidence) and `currency` (how current the pair are with each other's lives; falls with time at a rate by kind: slow for kin, faster for friends). A per-tie **maintenance style** (talking or doing together) says which acts offset decline. Do not key the style to gender, though the 2015 result did (DRV-11). **Check:** after a long gap, kin ties keep their regard and friends lose currency first; neither loses story-critical state.

**BND-25. Reconnection is welcomed more than people expect. An absence should produce a catch-up beat, not a penalty.** `[empirical]`.
- *Source:* Liu, Rim, Min & Min 2023 (abstract: senders underestimate recipients' appreciation, more so when the contact is surprising); Levin et al. 2011 (abstract); against it, a **shipped counter-example**: a farming game with daily friendship loss and a floor, and a mobile romance game with scheduled chat (Stardew wiki and guides, summaries).
- *Instance:* V walks into the noodle stall after six weeks. The vendor is glad, says what has happened since, and asks one thing about V.
- *Scales:* encounter. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** The first contact after a gap longer than the tie's expected interval (BND-27) runs a **reunion** template: a warm default, one item of the NPC's news, one question to V, and a pointer to what the NPC needed (a blocked concern V could unblock). The NPC's valence turns cold only for a recorded commitment that V broke (BND-27). **Check:** across long gaps, the share of cold reunions equals the share with a recorded broken commitment.

**BND-26. Never use absence, silence or leaving as an engagement lever.** `[empirical]` for the pattern in other products; `[hypothesis]` for the rule.
- *Source:* De Freitas et al. 2025 (summary: more than a third of companion-app farewells held manipulative tactics such as a pull to stay or fear of missing out; they raised later engagement and raised perceived manipulation, intent to leave and negative word of mouth); Zagal, Björk & Lewis 2013 (summary: playing by appointment and grinding as temporal dark patterns); map MOT-05 and MOT-01 (reactance).
- *Instance:* The vendor does not say "I thought you'd forgotten me", and V's reunion does not carry a streak.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 2, 3, ethics.
- *Operationalise:* **check.** Forbidden effects in templates: guilt on leaving or returning, countdowns on a relationship, loss of progress by absence, and any use of tie state to schedule offers for engagement. The director may not read relationship state for retention. **Check:** a template lint for those effects.

**BND-27. Expectation is a belief, and only a commitment makes an absence matter.** `[hypothesis]`; expectancy-violation theory is a lens and has been revised by its own author.
- *Source:* Burgoon's expectancy violations theory (summary: violation and its valence shape the reaction; later folded into a broader interaction model); [D-10](../decisions.md); [dossier 1, DRV-19 and DRV-20](d01-motivation-drives.md#a-what-drives-exist-and-how-they-relate) (obligation and delay).
- *Instance:* V who always shows up weekly and is gone for a month has changed the pattern. V who shows up twice a year and is gone for a month has not. Neither matters unless V promised to come, or a job was due.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1).
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** Each NPC holds an `expected_interval` belief about V's contact rhythm, updated from observed contacts, with a wide prior by tie kind. An absence matters in proportion to how far it exceeds the expected interval, **and** it has a cost only when an open commitment (a promise, a deadline, a debt) exists, in which case the cost is told in story terms. **Check:** with no open commitment, no negative reunion.

**BND-28. A character remembers specifics with their reasons, forgets the trivial, and half-remembers aloud.** `[practice]`, with the retrieval half in SAV-03.
- *Source:* Versu (part: characters remember the reason for each evaluation); map SAV-03 and lens L12 (power-law forgetting boosted by importance; one forgotten significant event can make the player conclude the world forgets everything; marked half-memory); [kernel 7.3](../spec/kernel.md#73-retention-and-forgetting) (memorable beliefs are roots and are kept).
- *Instance:* The ripperdoc remembers V paid late once because of a collapsed job, not that V bought a drink on a Tuesday. If she half remembers a name, she says so.
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** The tie holds an evidence list of event ids with salience and a recorded reason. Salience is raised when V's act touched one of the NPC's concerns (dossier 1) and when V showed it mattered to V (revisit, mention, protect). Unmemorable evidence fades by the kernel's rule. A callback may be half-formed on purpose, tagged as such. **Check:** an event V returned to is never dropped; a trivial one may be.

**BND-29. Relationship outcomes depend on saved state and V's choices, never on a hidden roll over what V decided.** `[practice]`.
- *Source:* Map SAV-02 (derive offers from the saved state, so a reload shows the same offer); lens L12; Inverse (page: in one Baldur's Gate 3 storyline a dice roll decides whether the player resists, which Larian's narrative designer says raises the stakes; this is a deliberate exception outside ordinary romance).
- *Instance:* V reloads at the third rung. The same offer appears, the NPC answers V's new choice, and no hidden random draw changes the NPC's reply.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 2, 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** NPC acceptance is a function of the NPC's state and V's act, with the injected random stream keyed so a reload reproduces it ([D-06](../decisions.md)). **Check:** the same save and the same V act give the same reply across reloads.

### E. Companions, NPC ties and groups

**BND-30. Ties among NPCs are story material, and a companion who exists only in relation to V reads as hollow.** `[empirical]` (qualitative, one study) and `[practice]`.
- *Source:* Tomlinson 2024 (full: players found Dragon Age: Inquisition's companions fuller, because they banter, pair off and grow with each other, while Baldur's Gate 3's party was "lonely" and "hollow" in comparison, with socialising centred on the player; two companions who can pair if neither is pursued by the player); Telltale (BND-11).
- *Instance:* Two crew members have a running quarrel about a debt. V hears it, can take a side, and has not been asked to.
- *Scales:* encounter, arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** NPC to NPC ties use the same record as ties with V. Banter and scenes between NPCs read those records. A companion's own arc includes its ties to others. **Check:** a fit report for a crew lists its internal ties and how many have a surfaced moment.

**BND-31. Generate NPC to NPC ties by closure, and watch them for imbalance. Imbalance is a story seed. It is not a law.** `[analysis]`, with models whose fit to real networks is limited.
- *Source:* Granovetter 1973 (summary: two strong ties tend to imply a third; later critiques say the "forbidden triad" logic has gaps and the weak-tie advantage depends on assumptions); Heider's balance and Antal, Krapivsky & Redner 2005 (abstract: "a friend of a friend is a friend" and the enemy rules; a finite network settles into balanced states; toy models).
- *Instance:* A is close to B and to C. B and C do not know each other, so at generation a B to C tie is likely, at least an acquaintance. Later, B is found to hate C: V's friend is V's friend's enemy.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** At tie creation, apply a closure probability for strong ties that share a third party. A cheap query finds triads with exactly one or three negative edges among actors V has met, and offers them to the procedural engine as candidate hooks ("V is the third vertex"). Do not simulate balance dynamics, which would flatten the city. **Check:** the share of unbalanced triads among generated groups stays within a band the fixtures set; they are surfaced rarely.

**BND-32. Tie strength is four things: time, emotional intensity, intimacy and reciprocal services. Coarse tiers keep one summary and fine tiers open the parts.** `[analysis]`.
- *Source:* Granovetter 1973 (summary: tie strength as a combination of time, emotional intensity, intimacy and reciprocal services).
- *Instance:* A district's population holds a summary strength per tie. When V befriends a vendor, the four parts are opened and filled from the shared history.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 1.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** At coarse levels a tie is `{kind, status, strength, last_contact}`. Opening a tie derives the four parts from the history, then continues with the full record of [section 8](#8-the-tie-record-a-proposal). **Check:** closing a tie keeps its pins; reopening it regenerates the same parts.

**BND-33. Rivalry is relational. It grows from similarity, proximity and a history of contests, and it can be one-sided.** `[empirical]`.
- *Source:* Kilduff, Elfenbein & Staw 2010 (abstract: similarity and prior contests predict rivalry's intensity, and rivalry raises the psychological stakes); the RimWorld opinion page (a pawn can be a friend to one who counts them a rival); Wildermyth community notes (summary: a hero can have many friends but one rival and one lover; already credited).
- *Instance:* Two couriers on the same routes, of the same crew rank, each timing the other. One thinks of the other as a rival. The other barely notices.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** `rivalry` is a derived salience in the regard of A for B, from similarity (rank, role, skills), proximity (shared places and audiences) and contest history. It may be one-sided. A rival tie is a kind, not an enemy: it can carry respect (BND-13) and can turn into a friendship or an enmity by events. **Check:** rivalry salience correlates with the three inputs in a sampled population.

**BND-34. Mentorship is a tie with functions and phases, and its ending is an expected beat.** `[empirical]` in outline; I rely on secondary summaries.
- *Source:* Kram 1983 and 1985 (summary: career functions such as coaching, sponsorship and protection; psychosocial functions such as acceptance, counselling and friendship; phases of initiation, cultivation, separation and redefinition; the later split of role-modelling).
- *Instance:* An old ripperdoc trains a street kid. The "separation" phase is the kid starting a practice, and the "redefinition" phase is them becoming colleagues.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A `mentor` tie has a phase and a set of functions. A phase change is an event with witnesses (DRV-43). Separation is an exit with a coda, not a failure. **Check:** a sampled mentor tie moves through phases without a forced breakdown.

**BND-35. Family and crew are memberships first. Their ties inherit the group's concerns, and the same person can sit in ties that pull against each other. That cross-pressure is where companion stories come from.** `[analysis]`.
- *Source:* [Foundation 5.2](../foundation.md#52-groups-and-membership); [kernel 12](../spec/kernel.md#12-groups-and-membership) and [10.8](../spec/kernel.md#108-effects-and-cross-pressure) (dilemma detection between frames of different origin); [dossier 1, DRV-41](d01-motivation-drives.md#e-groups) (kin help scales with relatedness, most when the stakes are high); Roberts & Dunbar 2011.
- *Instance:* A crew member's brother is on the other side of a job. Loyalty to the crew and care for the brother meet at one target.
- *Scales:* gig, arc. *Layers:* 1 (S1), 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A tie carries `via`: the membership edges it runs through (a kin edge, a crew edge). A tie above a commitment threshold contributes a `protects` interest on the other party to the actor's frames, which is how the kernel's dilemma check finds "my crew wants my friend harmed". Loyalty and exit follow the membership terms. **Check:** a seeded cross-pressure case produces a dilemma from the tie records alone.

**BND-36. Loss and departure are events with tie consequences, and a lost bond can weigh more than a lost thing.** `[practice]`.
- *Source:* TheGamer (page: Mazur says that in a Phantom Liberty ending, losing the people V has bonded with hits harder than losing cyberware).
- *Instance:* A crew member dies. The ties she had with others become grief, a grudge or a debt, and the ones nearest her show it.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **produce.** A death, exit or betrayal event propagates through ties above a threshold, raising the matching concern (grief, vengeance, atonement; dossier 1) in each dependent. **Check:** removing a high-commitment member always raises a concern in the ties that held them.

### F. Attachment, and a comparison

**BND-37. Players attach to NPCs who respond to them. The same mechanism can be used to exploit them.** `[empirical]` for attachment in outline, small self-selected samples; `[hypothesis]` for the line drawn.
- *Source:* Horton & Wohl 1956 (memory); Elvery 2023 (abstract: attachment to Undertale's characters, studied through reviews and Let's Plays); lens L12 (the predictions that small remembered responses raise attachment more than one large scene, and that a missed significant memory drops it; both untested); De Freitas et al. 2025 (BND-26).
- *Instance:* A vendor who remembers V's usual order becomes a stop V makes on purpose.
- *Scales:* arc. *Layers:* 1, 3, ethics.
- *Operationalise:* **produce and check.** The engine builds attachment through BND-08 and BND-28 and never through scarcity, loss threats, streaks or monetised return. The attachment measure (return visits to a named NPC) is used to evaluate, never to schedule engagement. **Check:** review of templates for BND-26 effects.

**BND-38. Relationship rules are written at the scale of a culture and a few tie kinds. They are not written per pair.** `[shipped]` (as a cautionary record) and `[analysis]`; **comparison only** under [D-21](../decisions.md).
- *Source:* Comme il Faut and Prom Week (part: relationships, networks and statuses; the lens and dossier 3 record several thousand influence rules and the authoring burden); Versu (part: practices, evaluations and one public state; its production was ended by its owner, per the lens). Both are comparisons for the *shape* of the tie record, not sources for the engine's own concepts.
- *Instance:* A norm "a fixer does not drop a client who is on a job" is written once. Particular fixers and clients fall out of it.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 1, 4.
- *Operationalise:* **check.** The tie model's rule surface is a few policies per kind (event to state; decay; bids; exits), not volition rules per situation. A count of authored relationship rules is a tracked measure. Where a comparison system separates directional scalars from discrete statuses (both did), the tie record does the same. **Check:** rule count per tie kind stays small enough for an author to hold in mind.

**BND-39. Findings from couples carry to other tie kinds only as hypotheses.** `[hypothesis]`.
- *Source:* The map's note on L12 (its sources are romantic and marital); the evidence tables in 2.1; the comparison in [8.3](#83-do-friendship-rivalry-and-mentorship-follow-the-romance-rules).
- *Instance:* Bids and responses are studied in newlyweds. Whether a fixer and a client keep a similar ledger is untested.
- *Scales:* all. *Layers:* 1, evaluation.
- *Operationalise:* **check.** Every tie-kind policy that borrows a couples result is marked as such, and gets a calibration fixture.

**Critiques of the sources themselves.**
- *Tomlinson 2024:* qualitative, from two Reddit communities, with no counts of players holding each view (the paper says so), and only the games the players compared. It supports the shape of the debate, not its proportions.
- *The effect studies:* Hall 2018 rests on self-report from movers and freshmen. Aron 1997 produced a short-term feeling of closeness. Aron 2000's lab boost was measured soon after a short task. Tran et al. is correlational, and effect sizes are not game effect sizes.
- *Gottman:* the 86 % and 33 % rates are repeated by the institute and in extension sheets, and the primary paper was not reached. The wider claim of accurate divorce prediction was criticised for lacking cross-validation (Heyman & Slep).
- *Trust:* the asymmetry (fast to lose, slow to gain) is contested, with replications finding it depends on the setting.
- *Social penetration:* its critics say disclosure is not closeness and that boundaries are not fixed. The onion is used here as a heuristic only.
- *Balance and tie strength:* Heider and Granovetter come from small studies and toy models. Later work doubts the "forbidden triad".
- *Design writing:* the vending-machine critique comes from a blog and a conference talk. The conference talk reached us through a secondary report. Their replacements are proposals, not tested designs.
- *Designer statements:* the quotes from studios are what they chose to say. Fan wikis and guides are used for mechanics only (a daily loss value, a route lock), never for intent.
- *CD PROJEKT RED:* what the studio has said about structure is thin. The structural findings in 2.4 are from the shipped data, not from design intent.

## 6. Lateral links

- **A tie's regard is a belief and a state at once.** A's warmth for B is A's state. A's idea of B's warmth for A is a belief in A ([foundation 5.4](../foundation.md#54-perspectives-everyone-has-their-own-model)). Unrequited love, misread friendship and false security are the gap between the two.
- **Bids are `reactsTo` interests.** A bid is something an NPC's frame watches for a reply to ([kernel 10.5](../spec/kernel.md#105-changes-and-matching)), the same mechanism as an if-then plan in dossier 1.
- **Reciprocity with delay is the same rule as debt** ([DRV-19, DRV-20](d01-motivation-drives.md#a-what-drives-exist-and-how-they-relate)); a favour in a communal tie is a gift, and in an exchange tie a loan.
- **A reunion is a callback after a gap** (FIT-42, DS-10): the strongest sign content is not random, and the lens's "callbacks land hardest after a gap".
- **A ladder's exits are a conclusion's routes** (FIT-23): at least three routes of distinct kind.
- **A retract is the engine's own X-card** and a short form of a seed's withdraw lifecycle.
- **A mentor's separation is the promotion ladder in reverse** (NAR-18): a character is promoted into the story's centre and later released from it.
- **Closure and balance are routine activity's missing link** in the social layer: triads are where "a suitable target meets a motivated offender" in social form (SYS-03).
- **Cross-pressure is membership against tie** ([foundation 5.2](../foundation.md#52-groups-and-membership)): Antigone's dilemma, found by an index hit.

## 7. Open questions

1. **How many rungs, and which?** The ladder's size (three to five) is a design call. Does one ladder fit friendship, romance and mentorship, or does each kind need its own?
2. **Should an NPC ever initiate romance with V?** The evidence says players are split and that persistence is the main complaint. Options: never; only as a deniable, once-per-rung bid; only where the author has marked a character as forward. The default here is the second.
3. **How is openness drawn for generated NPCs?** It must not be keyed to demographics (DRV-11), yet a population needs some. A candidate: independent traits with a flat prior, declared per pack.
4. **Is V's presentation ever an input?** Studios gate by body and voice. The default here is no; it is an authored dependency only (BND-14). Is that right for the vanilla characters a seed uses? (The seed does not change the vanilla gate; it declares its own.)
5. **Does the engine support polyamorous or multiple ties?** The game acknowledges juggling without consequences. Does a procedural NPC with a partner treat V's other ties as a cost, a neutral fact, or its own business? An authored stance per character is the default.
6. **Calibration.** Every number here marked **[J]** needs fixtures: bid rate by kind, currency decay times, reunion warmth, the pursuit budget. Suggested first measures: share of reunions that are cold; bids per tie per week; share of ties with a surfaced moment; share of declines that end in a continuing friendship.
7. **Time compression.** Hall's hours say closeness takes tens to hundreds of shared hours. A game gives V a few hours with any one NPC. The answer proposed here is denser, more specific moments, not faster numbers. Is that enough? Player research is needed.
8. **What decays for V's side?** The engine models only NPCs. V's own bonds (as V sees them) are V's. Does the engine ever "remind" V of a tie, and if so, how, without presuming V cares? The reunion beat from the NPC's side is the proposed route.
9. **Cyberpunk romance gating.** Where does the V-body or voice gate live (scripts, TweakDB, the character record)? The quest data has only the availability facts and test-scene writes. A game session could read them.
10. **Friend flags.** The quest data holds friend flags for Judy, Kerry, River and Johnny and none for Panam. Is there one in scripts? (Feeds [the main-story page's open question 1](../../../knowledge/main-story-state.md).)
11. **What does `mq055` really do?** The fact names suggest a partner's visits to V's apartment, thank-you texts and small gifts, in five apartment locations. The scene text was not read. A short read would confirm.
12. **Player research.** Almost every effect here is untested for games. The map's measures (MOT-01, MOT-04) and an A/B on earned against timed disclosure are the first experiments.
13. **Evaluation of exits.** What share of players who decline a ladder continue with the character? That is the measure of whether decline is truly "not punished".

## 8. The tie record, a proposal

This is the shape the findings point at, not a specification. It is written to fit the kernel's vocabulary (edges with attributes, enduring and time-varying slots, beliefs, memberships). S1 owns the policies; this dossier supplies the content.

### 8.1 Three records

| Record | Kernel form | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `regard(A→B)` | Slots on A's side (A's state), one per ordered pair that A has a tie with | A's own warmth, respect, trust, commitment inputs, bid rate, expectations, grievances |
| `tie(A,B)` | A symmetric edge, `core:tie`, with attributes and a structured data field | Kind(s), public status, depth, breadth, shared history, contact rhythm, `via` |
| `belief(A about B)` | The kernel's belief record ([11.1](../spec/kernel.md#111-the-belief-record)) | What A thinks B feels or intends, with confidence, evidence and source |

For a tie with V at one end:

| Record | Exists? |
|---|---|
| `regard(NPC→V)` | Yes. The NPC's state |
| `regard(V→NPC)` | **No.** It would be a model of V's feelings ([D-09](../decisions.md)) |
| `tie(NPC,V)` | Yes. It holds the history as events V did, the status, and the depth reached by *acts* (what has been said and shown) |
| `belief(NPC about V)` | Yes. What the NPC infers about V's acts and reasons, with confidence |
| The director's belief about V | Separate, in the kernel's [11.3](../spec/kernel.md#113-the-directors-beliefs-about-v) |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `depth`, `breadth` | tie | ordinal; set | Disclosure plus a responsive reply (BND-05) | Persists; can regress after a breach | Aron 1997; Laurenceau |

| `last_novel` | tie | time | A new class of joint activity | Decays | Aron 2000 |

## 9. Reference scenario R1, again

The Takemura seed is invented for [dossier 3's section 8](d03-fitting-casting.md#8-worked-example-reference-scenario-r1). The notation is illustrative only; the syntax is P4's job. This section refines how the romance part should be represented, using BND-01 to BND-39.

### 9.1 What the author must fix

| Part | Why fixed | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| The character, his traits and his **boundaries** (what he will not do, what he is slow to share, how he reacts to a no) | Refusal comes from the NPC's state; a fitter must not change who he is | BND-18, BND-14 |
| His **openness** and whether it depends on V's presentation | A character's own trait, declared with a reason | BND-14 |
| The **ladder**: rungs R0 to R3 as named states of the closeness fact, each with its scenes and the acts of V that move it | The spine stays authored | BND-05, FIT-43 |
| **All exits, authored**: decline, stay as friends, lapse (silence), and a retract exit; each with a coda | Declining is as authored as accepting | BND-15 |
| The **intimate** content, if any: authored, tagged, gated by the player's settings, with a fade or veil version, and no choice inside it | The floor | BND-17 |
| His **bids**: the few small things he would do on his own (a message, an offer of a favour) and how each can be read as friendly | Bids are deniable by construction | BND-19, BND-06 |
| What he **remembers**: the V acts that will move him and the callbacks he will use, each with a `because` | Responsiveness | BND-08, BND-28 |
| His **stance on V's other ties**, if V has a partner fact | A character's view, never a gate | BND-21 |
| What he does on **long absence**: reunion variant, and what a commitment would change | Absence | BND-25 to BND-27 |
| The must-nots: no line that states how V feels; no intimate scene before the second rung; no write to a game-owned fact; nothing in combat or while wanted; no repeated ask after a decline | From dossier 3, extended | FIT-49, BND-18, BND-20 |

### 9.2 What the fitter may decide

| Decision | Latitude | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| When each rung is offered and how long after V's last act | Within the author's minimum spacing, counted from V's act, not the clock | BND-05, BND-19 |
| Channel and place for each scene | From the author's ladder of channels and the quiet-place query | FIT-47, FIT-12 |
| Which variant of a bid to use | From the author's set | BND-06 |
| The hook: the link to V's history | A history-linked line, tagged fitter-supplied | FIT-42 |
| Connecting material between scenes | A message or mention within latitude; no new words for Takemura beyond the authored ones | FIT-43, FIT-51 |
| Backoff: how long the seed stays quiet after silence | A default of at least one window, longer if the author says | BND-19 |
| A reunion after a gap: which authored variant | From the author's set, picked by elapsed time and open commitments | BND-25 |
| Whether to offer a novel shared activity | A preferred, not required, step | BND-07 |

### 9.3 The tie in R1

| Record | Content |
|---|---|
| `regard(takemura→v)` | Warmth, respect, three-part trust, commitment inputs, expectations. Seeded by the main story's shared baseline and by V's observed acts since the window opened |
| `tie(takemura,v)` | Kinds: `friend` at first, `partner` possible. Status: acquaintance. Depth R0 to R3. History: V's acts as canon events |
| `belief(takemura about v)` | What he infers about V's acts and why, with confidence. Never a statement of V's feelings |
| The seed's closeness fact | In the seed's own namespace: `rung`, and `exit` once taken |
| Boundaries | On Takemura's record, authored, with reasons |

### 9.4 What V must always be able to do

| V may | What the seed does |
|---|---|
| **Decline** at any rung | The decline exit runs its authored coda. Nothing is locked. The NPC's regard moves only if the author wrote that it does, and then with the cause named |
| **Stay as friends** | The friend exit is a first-class status with its own scenes. The ladder does not "fail" |
| **Say nothing / ignore** | The lapse exit: the seed backs off; no nag; one authored quiet last message at most; the offer remains standing and can be revived by V |
| **Ask for more or less depth** | The NPC answers from his state and boundaries. He does not say more because V asked more often |
| **Change their mind** | Re-opening a rung after a decline needs a new V act and the NPC's authored acceptance; it is never forced |
| **Retract** (one input) | The seed withdraws quietly and records the exclusion; V sees it in settings |
| **Take the lead** when a partner fact exists | The seed offers a variant, not a block; the author's stance for Takemura applies |
| **Be away for weeks** | The seed pauses; a reunion variant runs on return; a commitment, if one was made, can be mentioned |
| **Reload** | The same offer returns; no hidden roll |

### 9.5 Six states, again

| State | What the seed does now |
|---|---|
| V accepts each rung in turn | R0 to R3 at the author's spacing; intimate content only if the author wrote it, the settings permit it and the second rung has passed; "partner" status only after V's act and his acceptance |
| V declines at R2 | The decline exit runs. Takemura keeps his warmth as the author wrote it. The seed offers friendship scenes and never repeats the rung unless V reopens it |
| V never answers a message | After the first window the seed is quiet. A last authored message by the lowest rung that works, then the standing offer |
| V is away for six weeks | No penalty. On return, a reunion beat and a pointer; the window's hard close may have passed, in which case the coda or the memory form (FIT-46) |
| V has a partner fact above 0 | A variant of the offer: a friendship ladder by default; V can take the lead; Takemura's own stance (authored) colours replies |
| V retracts | Withdraw quietly; record; the exits stay in the fit report |

### 9.6 What R1 cannot promise

- A new voiced line for him, or an intimate scene needing new animation ([DS-45](../design-seeds.md), FIT-51).
- A game-side change to the vanilla romance state. The roof scene tests all four lover facts and writes the chosen ones; the seed stays out of that.
- A detection of how V feels. The seed reads acts.
- Journal entries for the seed ([player channels](../../../knowledge/player-channels.md)).

## 10. Requirements for the seed language (P4)

The seed language must be able to express the following, in addition to the 41 in [dossier 3, section 9](d03-fitting-casting.md#9-requirements-for-the-seed-language-p4). Each is traced to a principle. This list is numbered afresh. Where an item extends one of dossier 3's requirements, it names that principle.

**Ladders and exits**
1. A seed that offers a relationship declares a **ladder** of named rungs, each a state of a closeness fact in the seed's namespace, and the acts of V (observed events) that move it. (BND-05, BND-09)
2. A ladder declares **at least three exits** of distinct kind: decline, stay as the current kind, and lapse. Each exit has authored content and equal status. The language refuses a ladder with fewer or an exit with no content. (BND-15; FIT-23)
3. Each rung has an **offer** (never an assertion), a decline path and a not-now path. There is no construct for a confirm step phrased as a point of no return. (BND-15, BND-20)
4. A seed can name an exit that V takes by doing nothing (**lapse**) and the backoff that follows. (BND-19)
5. A rung can be re-opened by V after a decline, only by a new V act, and the language says what the NPC's authored acceptance depends on. (BND-18)

**Character, openness and boundaries**
6. A character has declared **boundaries and openness** as traits with reasons. Any dependence on V's presentation is declared and flagged by the capability lint. The language has no construct that infers V's desire. (BND-14, BND-18)
7. A boundary is a persistent pin that only an authored event revises. There is no construct by which repetition changes an answer. (BND-18)
8. A seed can state its NPC's stance on V's other ties as a variant of the offer. It cannot gate an offer on a partner fact. (BND-21)

**Intimacy and consent**
9. An `intimate` scene class that a human-authored pack scene alone can fill, carrying a content tag that the player's settings must permit, with a declared fade or veil alternative and no choices inside it. The procedural engine cannot fill it. (BND-17; ETH-08)
10. The schema-level limits of dossier 18 cannot be overridden by any seed. (BND-17, BND-22)
11. Every offer is **retractable by one input**, and the seed declares its quiet withdrawal. (BND-22)

**Pacing and bids**
12. Pacing is declared as a minimum and a preferred spacing between rungs **counted from V's act**, not the clock, with a backoff rule after silence or decline and a limit of one romantic bid per rung per window. (BND-05, BND-19)
13. A seed lists the NPC's **bids** by kind, each deniable, each with its channels and a rule that an unanswered bid waits quietly. (BND-06, BND-26)
14. A seed can mark a **novel shared activity** as a preferred step. (BND-07)

**Text and honesty**
15. Player-facing text cannot assert V's inner state. A linter rejects it. A decision's line set must span at least four stances including a decline. (BND-20; FIT-49)
16. A response template has a required `because` slot naming the NPC's concern that V's act touched. (BND-08)
17. A seed names the V acts it will **remember** and the callbacks it will use, each linked to a concern. (BND-28; FIT-42)

**Absence, memory and reload**
18. A seed declares `on-long-absence`: reunion (the default), pause or coda. The language rejects guilt, streak and countdown effects unless a recorded commitment exists, in which case the cost is told in story terms. (BND-25 to BND-27)
19. The reunion variant is chosen by elapsed time, the NPC's expected interval and any open commitment. (BND-25, BND-27)
20. A seed's outcomes at a rung are keyed to saved state, and the language has no hidden random draw over V's relationship choice. (BND-29; D-06)

**Kinds, companions and cross-pressure**
21. A role that refers to another character states a **tie kind** with its exits (companion, rival, mentor, kin), and phases for mentors. (BND-02, BND-33, BND-34)
22. A seed can declare **NPC to NPC ties** and banter hooks that read the same tie records. (BND-30)
23. A seed's roles can name the **memberships** that pull on the character, so the dilemma check can find the cross-pressure. (BND-35)
24. A seed can give an NPC an authored **grievance, belief or debt** per direction, with a reason. (BND-01; DRV-18)

**Measures and reports**
25. The fit report for a relationship seed shows each exit and where it leads, the V act that last moved a rung, the tie-specific moment count per rung, and the why-now. (BND-11, BND-12; FIT-20)
26. Static checks: a ladder with fewer than three exits; an exit with no content; a gate on a partner fact; text that asserts V's feelings; a template with a guilt, streak or countdown effect; an `intimate` scene with a generated filler. (BND-15, 20, 21, 26, 17)

## 11. Requirements for the simulation layer (P3)

Each is traced to a principle. These sit in S1 and its policies.

**The tie record**
1. Represent a tie as two directed `regard` records plus a shared `tie` edge and `belief` records, with kinds non-exclusive. (BND-01, BND-02)
2. Hold **no record of V's regard or desire**. For a tie with V, hold the NPC's regard, its beliefs about V's acts and the history of V's acts. (BND-01, BND-20; D-09)
3. At coarse tiers a tie is `{kind, status, strength, last_contact}`. Opening it derives the four parts of strength and the full record from the history. Closing it keeps the pins. (BND-32)
4. Derive `commitment` from satisfaction, investment and alternatives on the NPC's side, with alternatives computed from the NPC's other ties. Only the order of weights is taken from evidence. (BND-03)
5. Hold trust as three scalars with an evidence count, an attribution on each breach event, and repair rules by attribution. (BND-04)
6. Keep `status` apart from the private variables. A status change involving V needs an observed V act and the NPC's own acceptance. (BND-09)

**Events to state**
7. Type the interaction events (bid, reply, disclosure, favour, conflict, repair, joint activity, novel activity, crisis). Each state change cites its cause event. (BND-06, BND-12)
8. Classify V's reply to a bid from acts, with no reply within a window as "no answer" and never as a rebuff when V had no chance to answer. (BND-06, BND-26)
9. A gift is an act read through the NPC's concern. No flat bonus per item. In a communal tie, prompt repayment is a mild negative. (BND-10, BND-02)
10. Generate NPC bids at a low trait rate from a library by tie kind. (BND-06)
11. Track novelty and prefer a novel shared activity for a flagging tie as a procedural-engine hint. (BND-07)

**Time, memory and absence**
12. Derive `quality` and `currency` from timestamped facts. Write a pin only on a bucket change. Fine tiers regenerate from coarse state after an absence. (BND-23; kernel 9.4)
13. Give each kind a decay and maintenance profile (slow for kin, faster for friends), with a per-tie maintenance style and an effort offset, not keyed to gender. (BND-24; DRV-11)
14. Keep a per-NPC `expected_interval` belief about V's contact rhythm. An absence has a cost only through an open commitment. (BND-27)
15. A **reunion policy**: a function of elapsed time, expected interval, the NPC's intervening events and open commitments, defaulting to warm. (BND-25)
16. Hold the tie's evidence list with salience and reasons. Raise salience for acts that touched the NPC's concerns and for acts V returned to. Let trivial entries fade. Allow marked half-memory. (BND-28; SAV-03)
17. Make acceptance a function of the NPC's state and V's act, with randomness keyed so a reload reproduces it. (BND-29; D-06)
18. Forbid, in policy, any use of tie state to schedule offers for retention, any loss by absence alone, and any guilt or streak effect. (BND-26)

**Consent and pursuit**
19. Store boundaries as persistent pins with reasons. No attempt count enters an acceptance function. (BND-18)
20. Apply a **pursuit budget**: one romantic bid per rung per window, none after a decline or silence, and an NPC's rejection response that is not a lever. (BND-19)
21. Support the one-input retract: soft-exclude a thread, record it and keep it visible and editable. (BND-22)
22. Never generate intimate scene content. Read the player's content settings before offering a tagged scene. (BND-17)

**NPC to NPC, groups and loss**
23. Create NPC to NPC ties by a closure rule on strong shared ties and detect unbalanced triads as hook candidates, without simulating balance dynamics. (BND-31)
24. Derive rivalry salience from similarity, proximity and contest history, allowing one-sided rivalry. (BND-33)
25. Hold mentor phases and functions, and treat separation as an expected exit. (BND-34)
26. Carry `via` on a tie and let ties above a commitment threshold contribute `protects` interests to the actor's frames so the dilemma check finds cross-pressure. (BND-35; kernel 10.8)
27. Propagate loss, exit and betrayal through ties above a threshold into dependants' concerns (grief, vengeance, atonement). (BND-36)

**Audit and tooling**
28. Audit generated openness, bid rates and loyalty by demographic tag. Count authored relationship rules per kind as a tracked measure. (BND-14, BND-38; DRV-11)
29. Mark each tie-kind policy that borrows a couples result, with a calibration fixture. (BND-39)
30. Keep numbers quiet on the surface and expose them, with causes, to traces. (BND-12; DS-26)
31. Calibration fixtures for: cold-reunion share, bids per tie per week, share of ties with a surfaced moment, share of declines that continue as friendships. (open question 6)
