## 1. The fields

**How the merge was done**

- A lens *proposes* a merged field when one of its candidates is mainly about it. A mention inside a candidate about something else is shown as "also". Only proposals count towards the saturation table.
- Candidates are merged when they describe one body of knowledge, and kept apart when the distinguishing principle differs. A lens number such as `L3:12` means that lens's candidate 12. L2's field list is numbered 1 to 25 here (its own F1 to F25). L4 and L5 are numbered by the 25 sections of their outputs. L12 is numbered by its F1 to F19.
- L2's 27 failure cases (its Part A) are an evidence bank, not candidate fields. They ride with NAR-11 and with the failure section of every dossier.
- Definitions and tier reasons are paraphrased from the lens outputs. Sources are carried over as the lenses cited them, trimmed of links.
- **Marks on sources.** `[sec]` means the claim rests on a search summary and the source was not read. `[mem]`, `[bk]`, `†` and "from memory" mean recalled and not rechecked (L1 writes `†`, L6 writes `[bk]`, L7 writes "from memory", L12 writes "(m)", shown here as `[mem]`). L2's field sources carry no marks, and its method note says canonical books were cited from memory unless a link was given. `[dev]` is L2's mark for a developer's own statement. L4 writes "(not re-verified)", shown here as `[mem]`; L5 writes `[bk]`, kept. L12 writes "(v)" for a source confirmed in the session. Where that confirmation was only a search hit or listing, it is shown here as `[sec]`, and where L12 fetched the page, "(read)".
- **Tiers.** *Core* means the field's principles would become requirements or mechanisms in a layer design (P3 to P8) or tests in P9. *Supporting* means it shapes details, supplies texture or magnitudes, or has other fields carrying its useful part. *Peripheral* means noted, not researched. Where lenses disagreed on a tier, the reason says so.
- **Informs.** Layers: 1 simulation, 2 seeding, 3 procedural engine, 4 authored content. After the dot, concerns: X expression, C player configuration, E evaluation, T tooling.
- **ID prefixes.** PER perception and memory. PAC pacing and attention budget. MOT motivation, agency and trust. REL relationships and romance. SAV saves, reloads and forgetting. NAR narrative form and scale. SEL selection, direction and planning. AUT authored content and production. EXP expression and staging. CRA live-craft traditions. SYS social, urban and economic systems. SET the setting. LOD levels of detail and scale (comparisons). EVA evaluation and testing. ETH ethics, representation and law.
- **D-21.** Fields that resemble context and relevance, the dimensional level-of-detail model, groups or perspectives are comparisons. Their reason says "relates to ... (comparison only)": PER-02, PAC-10, SEL-06, SEL-07, SEL-09, SEL-13, SYS-20, LOD-01 to LOD-03, and from L4 NAR-20 (possible worlds), NAR-21 (fictional minds) and NAR-22 (focalisation). They are capped at supporting, because the engine's concepts are first-party and these fields can only cross-check them. SEL-06 is the one L12 rated core (as a relationship field), and it stays capped. SYS-10, SYS-19, NAR-18 and SAV-03 also relate to those concepts in part but carry mechanisms of their own, so they keep their core tier.
- **D-20.** Two candidates (L9 #25 and L10 #24) were about patents only and are dropped. Patent source lines under other fields are not carried. L12's Nemesis candidate was not patent-only, so it merges into NAR-18, and its patent, expiry and legal-risk material is left out.

### 3.2 Tensions

| Tension | One side | The other side | How to settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden adaptation or visible responsiveness | Adaptation works best unseen, and one wrong personalised offer costs trust (MOT-05). Personalisation is shown by changing the offer, not announcing it (CRA-09). | Players feel noticed only when they see the response (PER-06), and open player models ask that the model be visible (ETH-20). | L3 calls this the least-studied boundary and the best candidate for a prototype study (EVA-01). |
| Congruence or distinctiveness | Setting-true texture is, by design, forgettable (PER-03). | Players count only perceptible difference (PER-08). | A tiering rule: a few isolated oddities, the rest congruent. Not a maximisation (L3). |
| Anti-streak rules or a predictable skeleton | Separation and fewer-repeat rules make a sequence feel more random (PER-09). | Rules on order make the skeleton more predictable, and players learn the skeleton (PER-07). | Vary structure, not only order. Constrain skeleton and skin separately (L3). |
| Peak-end or even quality | Spend authored craft on the peak and the end (PER-04). | CDPR gave all quest types equal quality and avoids checklist fatigue (SET-06, AUT-07). Peak-end is contested for long episodes. | Settle per scale in the evaluation dossier. |
| Declining: consequence or no penalty | A decline resolves into a consequence, never a null (CRA-01). Quests "cannot be failed, only consequenced" (SET-06). Clocks advance whether or not V attends (CRA-01, NAR-17). | Declining should carry no penalty, and timed failure is controlling framing (MOT-01; Fallout 4's offers in L2). A deadline that punishes a player who was elsewhere breeds resentment (NAR-17). | Define consequence as the world moving on, not as punishment. Test it with MOT-01's measures. |
| Forced choice or honesty | Forcing techniques give a felt sense of control, at low stakes (CRA-07). | Illusionism found mid-campaign sours trust (CRA-01, the quantum ogre; MOT-05). Quality bar 4 forbids offering a choice and then deciding regardless. | Allow it only for the framing of low-stakes payloads, never for outcomes. |
| Retroactive history or causes in the record | Generate events, then rationalise them afterwards (LOD-02, Caves of Qud). | Every link cites a recorded fact and staged traces are true to the record (NAR-01, EXP-01; quality bar 1). | Elaborate retroactively only inside what the record leaves open (P3 design). |
| Grain of the reusable unit | Phrase- or gesture-grain units give less perceived repetition than scene-grain units (NAR-10). | Storylets work at scene grain (SEL-04), and rule databases at line grain with the most specific match (EXP-03). | Use more than one grain. Compare them with PER-08's sorting test. |
| Causal chain or slack and mystery | Every link is "therefore" or "but", and "and then" is a defect (NAR-01). | Over-causal plots feel contrived and open-world players want slack (L2); unexplained specifics carry Night City (EXP-02); too much explanation kills mystery (SEL-10). | Causal for consequences, open for texture. |
| Which variable paces | Meter intensity with peaks and valleys (PAC-04). | Pace by leverage, where a quiet talk can be high-stakes (PAC-08), and note that delay makes reactive pacing unstable (PAC-07). | Choose the variable in the P5 design and test it against a fixed rhythm. |
| Authentic palette or stereotype | Typed setting data and recognisable factions make it read as Night City (SET-05, SET-01). | The genre's default palette is itself a liability, and role must not follow culture tag (ETH-01). Over-correction erases texture. | Separate role from culture tag by rule. Test with the audit (ETH-02). |
| Diegetic delivery or explicit boundaries | Content with no interface marker is rated more caused (CRA-04). | Pre-briefs and explicit boundary crossings raise belief and safety (CRA-15; L2 on Majestic). | Keep content diegetic and make opt-in and opt-out explicit at the edges. |
| Language model as expression | A symbolic layer holds state while a model proposes prose (SEL-11). | Safety-tuned models give bland, agreeable characters and homogenise output (SEL-11, ETH-10), model text loses commitments (L2), and disclosure norms bite (ETH-17). | The plan already limits generated text to detail. Test with EVA-01. |
| Player-set or inferred boundaries | The player owns the boundaries, and warnings do little where controls do more (ETH-06, ETH-07). | Detecting vulnerability or compulsion from play is itself profiling and paternalistic (ETH-20, ETH-21). | Player-set limits by default. Inference only with open models. |
| Plan, sift or manage | Plan for causal soundness and goal-driven acts (SEL-05), though its own sources doubt it for quests. | Find stories in the record after the fact (SEL-01), or steer towards author goals (SEL-03). | The P5 design chooses. The plan makes sifting the default, so planning may serve as validation. |
| Enterable alone or cumulative | Each gig or episode should be enterable alone, and some content resets (NAR-05, NAR-26, CRA-19's assumed continuity). | Quality bar 4 wants consequences that persist, and a world that resets everything reads as hollow (NAR-26). | Set persistence per thread: lasting for relationship and reputation facts, resettable for the incidental, with cosmetic traces allowed. Test: players can say what still holds. |
| Closure or open threads | Every thread needs a closure state, and the ending matters more than the length (PER-04, PAC-09, NAR-05). | A dystopian tone leaves some things open, and unresolved subplots are an asset while enough threads are alive (NAR-05). | Closure means a stated status (resolved, abandoned or transformed), not resolution. Retire threads gradually with a closing trace. |
| Promote on engagement or limit what is modelled | Promote what the player returns to, not what was authored first (NAR-18). | Watching engagement is player modelling and a retention engine (MOT-05, ETH-20, ETH-12). | Count only returns the player makes in play, keep the count local and legible, and make each promotion gate visible in the record. |
| Deserved outcomes or a world that does not pay out | Outcome valence is read against the player's disposition, and bad actors should meet bad ends (NAR-15). | The genre's critique is that the oppressor is concrete and wins cost something, and some things stay open (SET-01, SET-14). | Use disposition to choose how an unjust outcome lands (as insult, tragedy or fuel), not to repair it. |
| Scene wants or low-want texture | Every scene needs a want, an obstacle and urgency (NAR-16). | Atmosphere, rest and glances are low-want and carry much of Night City (EXP-10, EXP-02, SET-10). | Apply the test from encounter scale upward. A glance needs one implied change, backed by a recorded fact. |
| Earned closeness or free entry | Depth is paced by reciprocity: a disclosure is earned by the player's own prior act, and a betrayal is climbed back down (REL-01, REL-02). | Romance as a loot box for correct answers is the incumbent's failure, and the player must be able to enter or leave at any rung with no point of no return (REL-03, REL-04, MOT-01). | Pacing governs what the character volunteers, never what the player may do or decline. Test both with MOT-01's measures. |
| Same offer after a reload, or a fresh draw | Derive offers from the saved state so a reload shows the same offer and the world feels real (SAV-02). Regenerated content lets players fish for the best version (SAV-01). | Fully deterministic content looks scripted (SAV-02), and players compare branches to find fake consequences (MOT-04). | The offer is deterministic given the state. Variety comes from differing states and from sowing, not from re-rolls. Test with EVA-02's replay and EVA-01's blind panel. |
| Forgetting for realism, or total recall | Power-law decay boosted by importance makes recall feel human, and a minor in-character misremembering is charming (SAV-03). | One forgotten major event makes players conclude the world forgets everything, and attachment falls when a character misses what the player counts as significant (MOT-04, PER-06, REL-05). | Forget the trivial, never what the player has shown matters, and mark a half-memory instead of dropping it silently. |
| A fair threat or a helpful director | A threat that must find the player is rated fairer and scarier, though a director may hint to it in secret (PAC-04). | Help the player detects ("cheating") lowers rated fairness and trust (PAC-04, MOT-05). | Keep what the director knows apart from what the threat knows, and let hints show only as traces in the world (EXP-01). |
