## In brief

- **A staged scene is a projection of the record, in three layers, and only the first has to survive every arrival.** *Residue* (marks, props, absences) is true in every phase and is what a late or wrong-side arrival reads. *Routine* (what people do there on a cycle) is true in every phase and shows what the place is for. *Event* (the transient performance) happens on the record's clock whether or not anyone watches. Environmental-storytelling practice asks for a discernible chain of events and a world that keeps its rules (Carson; Smith's talk). The engine adds a rule no studio has: every placed element cites a recorded fact or event (STG-01), so the player-side reconstruction test and the record-side audit are the same test. The cost of this rule is real: CD PROJEKT RED's own account is that Night City's detail was placed by hand and that procedural placement fell short (STG-07), so the engine places authored *kits* with slots, never scattered props.
- **Legible agents are a small budget spent on few people, using the cues perception reads for free.** A change of speed and direction, a body turned along its motion, and a reaction that follows its cause at once are read as life, intention and causation (Scholl and Tremoulet, text). What marks an *intention* is that a blocked agent tries another way (equifinality, in Miller's account of Heider). So a focal passer-by needs only a goal, an obstacle, an alternative and a short list of tells, and the engine must stage the *obstacle* and the *second attempt* to make the goal readable (AGT-01 to AGT-06). Shipped barks do more than the mechanism behind them (F.E.A.R.'s "I need reinforcements" asserted help that did not exist, by the developer's own account); a truth-bound engine splits barks into *colour*, which may be free, and *claims*, which must cite the record (AGT-09).
- **"Why did this happen?" is a pull-only, contrastive answer of one or two causes, drawn from the event chain, in one of two registers that must never be mixed.** People ask why P rather than Q, select one or two causes, prefer intentional and abnormal ones, and are not helped by probabilities (Miller, text). So the player-facing answer is `because` (at most two causes, intentional first) plus an optional `rather than` foil, built from the record's `causes` and `served` slots (S0 §9.2). It speaks either as a *speaker's belief* (an NPC, a scanner clue, a news item; it can be wrong, as D-10 requires) or in the *settings voice* (the record's truth, plain, citing only exposed events, and saying so when the chain runs off-stage). The kernel's `explain` (draw-level, runner-up) is a *generation* account and stays out of the player's reach (XPL-02 to XPL-12).
- **Staging for passers-by is invisible theatre with a degrade ladder.** A street scene needs its premise in seconds and a second layer for those who stay, must be complete if nobody joins, and must end by dissolving with a trace and not by freezing (BYS-01 to BYS-04). The realisation ladder is *performance, routine, residue, report, record*: the engine falls down it as V's distance and attention fall, never skips a rung, and never lets a lower rung contradict a higher one (§5.2). Every rung above `report` costs bridge capacity that is small today (on the NPC-direction branch, at most three clone NPCs within 30 m and 24 props within 60 m; no decal route known; clones are friendly and immortal), so §12 names what the port must add.
- **The game already has a stage vocabulary the engine should speak.** 1,645 workspot files are named by posture, prop and action; `WorkspotCategory` has a `Nervous` entry; 4,035 conversation areas and 2,764 community markings (`vendor_stand`, `client_sit_barstool`) say who belongs where; the scanner's 1,470 clue records and their conclusions are the game's own "ask what happened here" grammar ([places](../../../knowledge/places.md), [player channels](../../../knowledge/player-channels.md)). Staging by that vocabulary keeps authored kits small and makes the diegetic "why" a scanner conclusion instead of a new interface.
- **Almost all of this is practitioner talk and simple-display perception.** I found no controlled study of environmental-storytelling legibility under random approach, none of whether explaining NPC behaviour changes player understanding or trust, and none of staged scenes for bystanders in a game. The principles are a testing agenda (§11), and §7 gives sixteen measures P9 can compute from the session ledger plus short probes.

## 1. Scope and why it matters here

Everything the simulation and the seeding layer decide is invisible until it is put where V can read it. Dossier 13 says what reaches V's awareness and how to count it; dossier 8 says that a large thing shows from the street only as projections of its state; dossier 14 says most simulated events will never be seen. This dossier is the missing middle: **how a recorded situation becomes a place, some people, some sounds and some words that read as caused, whichever way V arrives; and how a "why" is shown when V asks.** It is about what the realisation layer stages and what the explanation tooling shows. It is not about whether V notices (dossier 13), when a push is allowed (dossier 7), or the craft of lines (dossier 21).

| Layer or concern | What this dossier gives it |
|---|---|
| 1. Simulation (S0, S1) | Nothing new in the kernel. It asks S1 to keep `causes`, `served`, `witnesses` and the `place` of events for every event kind a stage kit can depict, and to expose per-prop and per-person *intent records* only for focal people (§5.3) |
| 2. Seeding (S2) | A stage specification (residue, routine, event; phases; approaches; ignored, watched and joined forms; interference; dissolve), bark kinds, an answer object for "why", and lint (§12.1) |

**What it does not cover.** The channel classes and the arbiter's gates (dossier 7). The grades of exposure and noticing rates (dossier 13, used as given). Variety and template detection (dossier 5). Content ceilings and floors (dossier 18; staged violence in public obeys them, §8). The wording of lines (dossier 21). Whether the engine models V (dossier 12). Sound as music and mixing: only what a script can request is treated, in §4.E.

**What is already settled and used as given.**
- **V is observed, never owned** (D-09). Nothing here infers what V attends to or wants explained. Whether "Why?" is offered is a setting, and the answer never mentions the director's model except through the existing "why this?" entry (S2 §8.8).
- **Everyone has their own model** (D-10). A diegetic answer is a speaker's belief and may be wrong. The settings voice is the only non-NPC voice allowed to say what the game has noticed (S2 §8.8).
- **Traces are borne by the world and cite a fact or event, never a pending one** (S2 §2.15, L-32, L-49). A constructed cause is pinned when displayed.
- **Release is not exposure** (S2 §8.5). A staged scene earns `ambient`, `presented` or `confirmed` by the tests of dossier 13 §5.2.
- **The engine reaches the game only through the bridge's command catalogue** (D-18), and the release route is the XF Core plugin (D-04). What the bridge can drive today is in the [runtime requirements](../runtime-requirements.md) and §5.6 below.
- **The engine does not run the game.** Every in-game question here is a prepared ask for a booked session (§11.3).

### 2.3 Where the advice itself fails

- **Environmental-storytelling advice is practitioner testimony.** Carson's article is a designer's essay from theme-park practice. Smith's talk is known to me through a news summary and a slide mirror. Jenkins's four modes are known through search summaries. None is a controlled study, and the one claim most useful here (that a scene reads from any approach) is not made in the sources I read: Carson's text does not discuss approach direction, and the lens's "detail reveals with distance in layers" did not appear in it either (§3.3). The approach rules below are [hypothesis], supported by the 80.lv remark that Night City's scripted scenes became 360-degree spaces whose backgrounds had to support the story from any angle.
- **Animacy and causality results use simple shapes and brief displays.** Scholl and Tremoulet review them as fast, automatic and largely perceptual, and also say they come from 2-D shapes. Whether the same cues carry on a rendered crowd, in a first-person camera, under load, is untested here. Players who learn the trick may collapse the impression (the lens's F.E.A.R. critics; not verified).
- **Explanation research is about human explainers, abstract tasks or lab systems.** Miller's review is explicit that it surveys how people explain to each other and argues AI should build on it. Lim and colleagues used arbitrary integer scales so that domain knowledge would not interfere. I found no study comparing explained with unexplained NPC behaviour on player trust or understanding (the closest hit was a system description), so every explanation principle for games is [hypothesis] on top of its [empirical] base.
- **Invisible-theatre sources are activist and practitioner pages.** The Beautiful Trouble entry is a how-to; the founding case is contested (F-13). The lens's three-behaviour frame (ignore, watch, join) is not in the pages I read.
- **Theme-park staging controls the viewing direction.** Visitors ride past in order. A walkable city does not (hypothesis, not stated by Carson). The same holds for film editing, which controls order and framing for a passive viewer (NAR-13).
- **The shipped evidence for ambient staging in Night City is thin.** CD PROJEKT RED's talks describe the art and the scene system, not how ambient scenes were judged. Everything about what players read from Night City's streets is [hypothesis] until a session says otherwise.

## 4. Principles

IDs: STG (staged evidence), ARV (arrival: early, late, wrong side, interfering), BYS (passers-by), AGT (legible agents), SND (sound and diegetic channels), PLC (place, scent, adjacency and objects), ENC (encounters as containers), XPL (explanation on request). Grades are P1 §4's. Scale codes follow dossier 8 (gl glance, en encounter, vg vignette, gg gig, ar arc, se season). Layer codes: 1 to 4 are the four layers; C player configuration; E evaluation; X expression; P8 realisation; P10 tooling. "Produce" says what a procedure can do; "Check" says what it can test. Requirement IDs (`S20-S2-nn`, `S20-P8-nn`, `S20-P10-nn`) are in §12; measure IDs (`M20-nn`) are in §7. No row asks the engine to learn anything about V: where a principle needs a number about people, it is a population value set in P9 (D-09).

## 5. Models the engine could run

Everything here is a proposal for the specification stage. It stays inside what the foundation, S0 and S2 already hold, and it names what each piece asks of the game port. Interfaces are TypeScript-style sketches with normative field names only where I say so.

### 5.1 How staging realises the foundation

| Foundation idea | In staging |
|---|---|
| **Open a locality only as far as something needs it** (D-07, relevance drives resolution) | A place is opened to a *rung* (§5.2). Nothing is placed unless V's frames, or the director's search for a window, need the detail |
| **Pins carry a reason: observed, committed, mirrored, authored** | A displayed trace pins the fields of its cause as `committed` (S2 §2.15). A prop V scanned is `observed`. The game's own placed people are `mirrored` |
| **Close folds a bundle to a summary and keeps its pins** | Dissolve (BYS-03) is Close: the cast and props go, the residue's pins and the event stay. Falling a rung is a Close |
| **Everything is relationships; an address is one path among many** | The vendor is reachable as the stall's occupant, as the Claws' collection list entry, and as V's memory of a Friday. The stage compiler walks the event's participants, not a scene list |
| **Explanation is tracing provenance** (foundation §7) | `story.why` walks `causes` and `served`; the draw trace is a second account (XPL-10) |
| **Perspectives: a belief is a pin in the believer** (D-10) | A diegetic "why" is a belief record rendered; AGT-11 acts on a belief; the settings voice is the only voice that speaks the record directly |

### 5.2 The staging ladder

Five rungs. The engine realises a scene at the highest rung V's distance, attention and the port's capabilities justify, and falls down the ladder as they fall.

| Rung | Name | What exists | What it needs from the port | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Performance** | Cast acting the phases, props, barks, scan entries, ambience hint | Clone casts, directed movement, look-at, barks, props, a phase-offset join (S20-P8-01 to -08) | A scene V is within about 30 m of, in view |
| 3 | **Routine** | Cast at their spots doing what the place is for; props; no performance | Spots or carrier gestures; clones | A place V is near but not watching, or a scene between phases |
| 2 | **Residue** | Props, marks, absences and the game's ordinary crowd; no engine cast | Props and marks (decals unknown); no clones | A place V may reach soon, or the aftermath of a performance |
| 1 | **Report** | Nothing placed. A news item, a rumour, a scanner entry on an existing object, a message, a shard | The channels of [player channels](../../../knowledge/player-channels.md); no placement | A place V is far from, or a phase V will not see |
| 0 | **Record** | The events and their causes in S0 | Nothing | Everything else; most events (D14) |

**Rules.**
1. **No rung is skipped on the way up.** A performance begins from the routine and residue already in place, so V sees continuity and never a pop-in (ARV-03, ARV-04).
2. **Truth is monotone.** An element at rung *k* must agree with every element at rung *k-1* and with the record. A lower rung may say less; it may not say something else. A report that contradicts the residue is a defect (S20-S2-09).
3. **Change rungs out of sight.** A rise or a fall happens outside V's view and beyond the perceptual range of DSP-04 and DSP-05 (S20-P8-02). Falling a rung inside V's view is allowed only as a dissolve (BYS-03).
4. **A seed declares its lowest tolerable rung.** A gig's encounter needs rung 4 when V arrives; a protection drive's shut stall needs only rung 2; a rumour needs rung 1. If a capability is missing, the compiler picks the highest rung it can and the fit report says so in plain words (L-28).
5. **Every fall to rung 1 or 0 leaves a delivery when a phase would otherwise be unseen,** so the closing and news rules of S2 §6.4 and §8.6 can run.

### 5.3 The stage specification

For S2 v0.13, a `stage` block on a fragment or beat of `glance`, `encounter` or `vignette` level, filled from an authored kit (S20-S2-01, -13).

```ts
interface StageSpec {
  kit: KitRef;                                   // the authored unit; the binder fits it to a place
  implies: EventPattern;                         // exactly one situation (L-32)
  residue: StageElement[];                       // true in every phase until its duration ends
  routine: Routine[];                            // what the place's people do on a cycle (workspot sets, markings)
  phases: Phase[];                               // the transient event, on the record's clock
  reads: Array<{ element: ElementKey; kind: SignKind; from: ApproachKey[] | 'all' }>;
  glance: ElementKey[]; stay: ElementKey[];      // BYS-01
  ifIgnored: Outcome; ifWatched: Outcome; ifJoined: Outcome;   // the same record outcome unless V's act is a fact
  onInterfere: Record<Interference, 'continue' | 'react' | 'fold' | 'release'>;
  dissolve: { route: 'walk-off' | 'settle'; maxDuration: Duration; leaves: ElementKey[] };
  lowestRung: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4;
  ambience?: 'hush' | 'busy' | 'tense';          // a hint; the port maps it or ignores it
}
interface StageElement {
  key: ElementKey;
  kind: 'prop' | 'mark' | 'person' | 'posture' | 'absence' | 'text' | 'sound' | 'light';
  cites: { id: Id; source: 'found' | 'surfaced' | 'constructed' };    // STG-01
  implies?: EventKindRef; order?: 'before' | 'after'; motif?: string; // STG-02, STG-04
  duration: Duration;                            // how long it stays true
  interaction: 'none' | 'scan' | 'read' | 'use'; // STG-05
  scan?: ContentRef;                             // about 20 words (SND-03)
  explainedBy?: ElementKey;                      // AGT-04
}
interface Phase { key: PhaseKey; at: EventTime | ClockOffset; premiseCue: ElementKey; tells: Tell[]; barks: Bark[] }
interface FocalCard { who: RoleKey; goal: string; obstacle?: string; alt?: string;
                      state: 'worried' | 'hurried' | 'wary' | 'angry' | 'tired' | 'hopeful' | 'afraid' | 'bored';
                      habit?: string; tells: Tell[] }                 // AGT-01, AGT-05, AGT-08
type Bark = { kind: 'colour' | 'claim' | 'exchange' | 'exposition'; text: ContentRef; cites?: Id; of?: RoleKey[] };  // AGT-09
type Interference = 'bump' | 'block' | 'talk' | 'scan' | 'steal' | 'threaten' | 'attack' | 'follow' | 'photograph';
```

The kit is authored once, the binder chooses the place and the variants, and the compiler checks `cites` against the record. Lint (S20-S2-03): at least two events implied in order, at least two sign kinds, a `premiseCue` per phase, a `reads` entry per key read, every `claim` bark citing a fact, `ifIgnored` and `ifJoined` reaching the same outcome, every `Interference` answered, and no element that promises a use it does not give.

### 5.5 Who can be staged: the game's people and the engine's

The NPC-direction design refuses to pick a protected person: quest-marked, companion, boss, vendor, police, child, one on autonomous AI, one in a scene or workspot ([NPC direction design](../../runtime/npc-direction-design.md) §3.1, §3.6). Vendors and police are exactly the people a street scene most wants.

- **The game's people are witnesses, not cast.** They keep their presets (BYS-04); the stage reads them through `scene.read` and never directs them.
- **The engine's cast are clones** of crowd records with the crowd flag off, spawned non-persistently, out of sight where possible, and counted against a cap.
- **A "vendor" the stage needs is a clone at an unused `vendor_stand` spot,** or the scene lives in the residue and the cast around the game's own vendor, whose behaviour stays the game's. I have not found a source that says whether a clone at a marked spot behaves like the game's vendor (§10, question 4).

### 5.6 The answer to "why"

**The answer object** (S20-S2-11):

```ts
interface WhyAnswer {
  register: 'speaker' | 'settings';
  speaker?: Id;                                  // for a diegetic answer
  because: Array<{ event: Id; text: PhraseRef }>;  // at most two (XPL-03)
  rather?: { foil: 'baseline' | 'expectation' | 'alternative'; text: PhraseRef };   // XPL-02
  more: boolean;                                 // a deeper link exists
  offStage?: AskRef;                             // the chain continues unseen (XPL-09)
  depth: 'world' | 'lab';                        // lab adds the draw trace and the constructed flag
}
```

**Selection** over the event chain reachable by `causes` from the event that produced the thing asked about, at most four links (S2 §2.16):

1. Keep the links whose exposure meets the register's rule (the settings voice: exposed; a speaker: whatever that speaker believes).
2. Choose the foil: the baseline V confirmed (the ledger), else the speaker's expectation, else the alternative the event's actor passed over (`served` names the goal the act served).
3. Score each cause: intentional (the event has an actor and a `served` frame), abnormal against the foil, necessary for the effect; ties go to the nearer cause. Trace through a proximal accident to a distal intention (XPL-04).
4. Keep the best two. Render each from the closed phrasing table. If a further link exists, set `more`; if it exists but is not exposed, set `offStage` (XPL-09).

**What is never shown:** a rate, band or heat number; a draw trace or runner-up (lab only); a model belief about V; an unexposed fact; a speaker's belief presented as the record; and any claim that a sampled detail "was chosen".

| V asks | Diegetic (a speaker's belief) | Settings voice |
|---|---|---|
| "Why is the stall shut?" (V saw it open on Thursday) | The neighbour: "The Claws came round Friday. I think he couldn't pay." (a belief; may be wrong) | "The Tyger Claws' collector came on Friday and was not paid. More happened that you have not seen." (with a handle to the neighbour) |
| "Why did he run?" (a passer-by veered as V watched) | The scanner clue: "Panic response. Subject recognised an approaching collector." (the scan entry's author is the game's investigation grammar, not a person) | "He recognised the collector who had come to the stall." |
| "Why didn't the Claws come on Thursday?" | The neighbour: "They never come Thursday." | "Nothing you have seen points to a Thursday visit." |
| "Why am I being shown this?" | Not diegetic | The existing pull entry (S2 §8.8), only if a hook's output was non-zero |

**V asks.** At the shut stall V talks to the neighbour: a diegetic answer, "The Claws came round Friday. I think he couldn't pay." It is the neighbour's belief, so it might be wrong (D-10), and it ends with an `ask` towards the collector's route. V scans the notice and the sign: two clues and a conclusion in the game's own grammar. V opens the pull entry: "The Tyger Claws' collector came on Friday and was not paid. More happened that you have not seen." It cites the exposed event only, and it does not say that a cause was ever constructed.

**What the walk-through exposes.** The vendor cannot be the game's vendor (a protected person), which changes who "the vendor" is. The scene needs three clones within 30 m (collector, vendor, neighbour), exactly the branch's cap, so no further witness can be cast. Decals, a scorch mark or a knocked sign are not on the bridge's list. A scanner entry on a spawned prop has never run. The negative-space rule means the binder might refuse this stall if a market is cluttered (STG-08).

## 8. Tensions

| Tension | The two sides | Resolution here |
|---|---|---|
| **True to the record against the shipped economy of smoke and mirrors** (dossiers 14, 6) | Orkin's squad dialogue worked because what to say was chosen after the action; dossier 14 says most events are never seen, so the record behind a street scene may be a coarse count. The engine's rule is that traces are true to the record (S2 L-49) | Colour is free; claims cite. Citing costs little because the compiler walks the event; fine detail is materialised on demand from the summary (S0 §9.3) and constructed only within `latitude` (XPL-11, AGT-09) |
| **Legibility against mystery** (dossier 13 on curiosity; dossier 7 on dread; SEL-10) | Signs should say what kind of thing happened; a mystery should keep its cause | Stage the *situation*, withhold the *cause*: the signs are consistent with every nature a `withheld` cause may take, and the settings voice returns the open question (XPL-13) |
| **Rich staging against what the bridge can do today** | A scene wants cast, props, marks, sound and scan entries; the branch gives three clones within 30 m, 24 props within 60 m, no marks and no hostile casts | The ladder (§5.2): a seed declares its lowest tolerable rung and the compiler picks what the port can do. Kits stay within the cap until the cap rises (§12.2) |
| **Authored kits against variety** (dossier 5) | Hand-built kits are the only thing the evidence supports (STG-07), and they are costly (F-06); a few kits repeated read as a template | Variety comes from binding, from variants and from the order of residue and routine; the pastiche index and the per-feature repeat counters of D13 watch it. A district's kit count is a P6 number |
| **V's freedom against the record's clock and a complete scene** (dossier 9) | V may walk through, shoot or leave; the world keeps its clock and the scene must be complete if ignored | The record absorbs V's act as an event (confirmed); `onInterfere` answers each behaviour; the cast are mortal and reactive (ARV-06) |
| **Explanation against the fiction** (D-09, the honest voice) | A "why" in the settings voice is out of the fiction; the fiction should not explain itself | Diegetic first (a neighbour, a scanner conclusion, a news line); the settings voice is a pull entry like the journal, never pushed; a player who never opens it loses nothing (XPL-06, XPL-08) |
| **The settings voice's honesty against constructed causes** (dossiers 10, 12) | The answer is true to the record, but the record's cause was filled in when V first looked | No change once displayed; the lab view flags it; the noticed page says in general that unseen details are filled in at first look. | **Consistent sign sets against repetition** (D8 STR-06, dossier 5) | A front's signs must be learnable; the same sign everywhere is a template | A small sign set per front, varied in place and kind (STG-03); repeats counted per feature |

## 9. Lateral links

- **Pull narrative is the class 0 trace.** Smith's pull against push is dossier 7's rule that class 0 costs nothing and may be missed, and dossier 13's redundancy rule for what is missed.
- **Invisible theatre has no reveal; the engine explains only on request.** The "reveal" is the pull entry, and it is V's to open.
- **Orkin's smoke and mirrors is generate-then-justify** (compromise C-01 in the foundation). The engine keeps the economy and adds a citation.
- **Heider's equifinality is already in the context chain.** The foundation's frames hold a goal, what blocks it and what serves it, so a focal card (§5.3) is a view of a frame, not new data.
- **Miller's foil is an exposed baseline;** the kernel's runner-up is a draw-level cousin that stays in the lab.
- **Lim and colleagues' five questions are the `ask` types:** what (the return view and recap), why (`story.why`), why-not (the bounded form), what-if (not offered), how-to (an offer).
- **Van Lent's replay log with a question menu** (through Sado and colleagues) **is the exposure ledger with ask handles.**
- **Radio drama's signposting is the first bark's premise;** Beck's "flatline" of constant sound is D13's habituation.
- **Kopytoff's singularisation is a prop promoted to a plant** (S2 §2.8).
- **Tomsiński's first-pass generator is the staged scene's compiler;** bespoke work is kept for peaks.
- **Carson's "where am I" in fifteen seconds is the glance layer;** his cause-and-effect clues are traces.
- **Kuleshov is the adjacency audit;** Zacks's boundaries are where a phase may change.
- **The braindance is the game's own "what happened here" device** (layers and clues over a recorded scene); a candidate carrier for the diegetic answer (§10, question 13).

## 10. Open questions

1. **Can an engine clone at a marked spot behave like the game's own vendor?** The game's vendors are protected from direction. A clone at a `vendor_stand` spot needs an S4 spot query and either `AIUseWorkspotCommand` with a node reference or our own carrier. Nothing in the pages I read says which works.
2. **What route places a decal, stain, scorch mark or damage, or plays a vanilla sound event at a place?** I found none in the knowledge pages. This goes to the research queue (§12.2, S20-P8-04, -12).
3. **Does a scanner description or clue work on a spawned prop or clone?** The player-channels page's session rows 1 to 6 ask it; V8 stays "want".
4. **Can a clone be made mortal, hostile or handed back to vanilla AI at run time without a bad state?** The branch makes casts friendly and immortal; the interference table (ARV-06) needs the reverse.
5. **Do the animacy and contingency cues carry on rendered crowd NPCs in a first-person camera, in motion?** The evidence is shapes on a screen. A panel on recorded footage can answer it cheaply (§11.2).
6. **How many clones can a street scene afford?** The branch caps three within 30 m. P7 measures what the frame budget allows.
7. **How many kits make a district feel varied, and what does a kit cost an author?** Dossier 5 and P6.
8. **Is a two-clause settings-voice answer with an off-stage line satisfying or irritating?** Needs a panel and then the game.
9. **Should one targeted lens run on gameplay encounter design?** No lens covered combat, stealth or traversal as a container. The game's own shape is measured (ENC-01, ENC-02), but the craft of arena shape, enemy roles and difficulty is not. I would run a short targeted lens before P8 specifies encounters; the map already allows for it.
10. **How should a constructed cause be disclosed?** My default is a general line on the noticed page plus a lab flag, with no per-answer flag. This is the coordinator's call, open to the maintainer's override.
11. **Photo mode.** V can pause the world and move the camera anywhere. Staged scenes must hold from any camera, with nothing backstage visible, and the same freedom gives the arrival matrix cameras that need no walking (§11.3). Does the bridge's photo-mode camera work over a staged scene, and does the world's clock stay put?
12. **At speed.** In a vehicle V sees a scene for seconds. The rung rule should favour residue at speed (D13's activity tiers); where the line sits is unmeasured.
13. **Can the game's own replay-and-investigate device carry a diegetic "why"?** A vanilla braindance is a scene played with visual, audio and thermal layers and clue nodes, and an installed mod casts scenes at run time ([quests and story](../../../knowledge/quests-and-story.md) §5). Offering a recorded event as a braindance would be the most native way to answer "what happened here", but it needs a scene resource written from the record, and whether clue nodes work on a mod scene is untested.

## 11. Testing agenda

Almost nothing here has been tested on games, so this is the evidence the design will produce. It goes in the order cheapest first.

### 11.1 Offline, on the reference model (do first)

- **Compile and cite.** From fixture records, compile a `StageSpec`, check every element's `cites`, and run the orphan audit (M20-02) and lint (§5.3).
- **Ladder properties.** Generate random records and rungs; assert that rung *k* agrees with rung *k-1* and with the record, and that no rung is skipped on a rise.
- **Selection fixtures for `story.why`.** Hand-written chains with expected `because`, `rather` and `more` (intentional over proximal, abnormal against a baseline, a trace through an accident to an intention), including a speaker whose belief is wrong.
- **Leak differential.** Paired records that differ in one unexposed fact must give identical settings-voice answers (M20-15).
- **Register and layer audit.** No answer mixes registers; no world answer carries a draw trace (M20-16).
- **Determinism.** The same record gives the same stage and the same answer on every run.
- **Audience scripts on the offline simulated port** (D-18): every closed-list behaviour gets a defined outcome (M20-11), and a reload restages at the current phase (ARV-07).

### 11.2 Panels on stills and recorded clips (after the first prototype)

- **Reconstruct from residue** with foils, across the arrival matrix, from stills and short clips (M20-01), with sign kinds removed one at a time (STG-03).
- **Trait or situation:** the same behaviour with and without the situation cue (AGT-04).
- **Animacy and latency:** a clone that veers on a cause at 0.3 s and at 2 s; with and without the speed change (AGT-02, AGT-03).
- **Believed against omniscient** behaviour (AGT-11).
- **Answers:** one-cause against two-cause, with and without a foil, why against why-not, pull against push (M20-12, M20-13).
- **Glance and stay dwell groups** on a recorded street (M20-08).

### 11.4 Longitudinal, with early testers

Ask a few testers to describe three places they remember from a week of play and what happened there, and compare with the stage log (M20-01 at a delay, M20-14).
