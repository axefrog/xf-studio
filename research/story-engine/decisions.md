Every settled decision, in one place, so that nothing depends on a conversation being remembered. A decision is changed only by a new decision that replaces it here. Each row gives where it came from and where it is worked out.

**Sources:**
- **maintainer:** the project maintainer's direction;
- **agreement:** settled with another coordinating chat and recorded on the coordination board;
- **coordinator:** this vertical's own call within its remit, open to the maintainer's override.

| D-25 | 25 Oct 2026 | **The protagonist is a parameter, not V.** A longer-term ambition for the whole project is a new protagonist who isn't V. | D-27 | 25 Oct 2026 | **Stories emerge from layered abstract plotlines, not from errands.** The engine is not primarily a generator of fetch quests and jobs. Its source material is a library of abstract plotlines and story beats that prescribe no specifics (the composite, WP16); every ingredient (who, where, what is at stake, why now) is cast from the live simulation. Two or more plotlines layered over the same people and places, plus growth from small to large, give multilayered, multitiered, chained storylines that emerge from what happens. Requests to the protagonist are one form among many, and errands a minority. Evaluation judges threads over time, not single hooks, and the director's ranking favours what matters to the people the protagonist knows over whether a hook has an ask. Quotes: "just want to clarify my hope that this doesn't become primarily a generator of fetch quests and the like"; "standard plotlines that don't prescribe specifics, etc. Layer a 2 or more of those together into a melting pot where the ingredients and seasoning are drawn straight from the live elements in the simulation, and you get lots of multilayered, multitiered, chained storylines and story beats emerging naturally." | maintainer | [plan §8.2](plan.md#82-work-packages) WP16, WP17 |
| D-28 | 25 Oct 2026 | **The composite is composable patterns, not templates.** D-27's library is a set of small story patterns and formulas (tensions between people, moves and turns such as complication, reversal, revelation, escalation and payoff, and the forms that close a thread) that combine with each other and choose their own shape from the situation. **One language spans the whole range,** from fully abstract patterns to highly specific stories that people write (an authored story is a template with most of its parts fixed, D-03). Every point on that range must adapt to the situation (fitting) and intersect and compose with the others, so even a specific authored story can share junctures with procedural ones. What is ruled out is a catalogue of fixed plots replayed with new people in the role slots. The test is the player's: a long run must not read as the same package of stories recast with new people. The evaluation measures repeated structure at thread level (beat functions with names and specifics masked), not only repeated wording. Quote: "Just don't lose the opportunity at your fingertips re: an underlying set of composable patterns/formulas for good stories. Better than simply "templates" because they adapt to context more readily and don't end up feeling like you're replaying the same package of 20 stories, each with different characters assigned to play the same roles." Refining it: "Templates are fine if they're abstract, but you want them to be adaptable and intersectable/composable so that they don't feel repetitive." And: "In hindsight I acknowledge that templates of a more naive form need to be allowed anyway, given the fact that a human-contributed story is probably often going to be highly specific, and what is a template if not that?" The seed language, which leans towards whole-plot templates today, is to be improved accordingly ([DS-70](design-seeds.md)). | maintainer | [plan §8.2](plan.md#82-work-packages) WP16 |
