# Jewellery construction set — design proposal

Research/design snapshot, 24 September 2026. This proposes a reviewable *authoring grammar*, not an implemented editor or a game-ready piercing package. Eye makeup remains the active authored feature. Distances below are illustrative model dimensions, **not** fitting advice for a real piercing or calibrated REDengine coordinates. No external photo, mesh, texture, product silhouette, or mod asset is included in XF Studio.

## What the pictured references teach

The [Association of Professional Piercers' illustrated initial-jewellery brochure](https://safepiercing.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/APP_Initial_Print.pdf) separates *gauge/thickness*, *wearable length* and *ring diameter* in its measurement diagram and pictures a nostril screw, labret stud, circular barbell and fixed-bead ring as distinct mechanical forms. Its [procedure-manual illustrations, p. 31](https://safepiercing.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/APP_Procedures_2013_A_Web.pdf) further distinguish nostril nails, septum retainers, eyelets and captive-bead ends. These are the right conceptual measurements and closures for our controls; a single generic “scale” slider would conflate them. The brochures credit photos to **Paul King**, **Neometal**, and **Industrial Strength Body Jewelry** where noted, and their measurement diagram/text to **Elayne Angel, *The Piercing Bible***. The initial-jewellery brochure states **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**. These illustrations are viewed as references only, not copied or adapted.

The linked image URLs are a *reference manifest*, not assets to ingest. Product pages may change; before a design is implemented, compare the cited front/side images and the brochure's gauge/diameter diagram again. The examples below use common structural primitives rather than tracing any one pictured product.

Nathan's [36 local earring photographs, individually indexed with hashes and dimensions](earring-reference-inventory.md) greatly broaden the design target beyond small studs and nose pieces. The visible forms include concentric/multi-rail and irregular hoops (E01, E06, E13, E31–E36), open sculpted wire spirals (E08, E16, E19–E21, E29), fishhook or post-supported bead spines (E02, E07, E11, E24, E30), large framed chandeliers with many suspended strands (E04, E12, E17, E23, E26), and interlocking circles at different apparent depths (E22, photographed on an ear). A fringe is not just one charm enlarged: the frame has a **distribution of attachment points**, and each branch has its own length, beads, and terminal drop. The worn E22 image is especially valuable for judging silhouette at face scale. E09, E13, E34 and E35 show enough front/back hardware to warn against modeling every earring as a visible front ornament glued to skin; uncertain closures remain marked as such in the index. E03's different star/moon charms argue for deliberate, editable asymmetry rather than mandatory mirroring. E22's differently oriented loops might simply have pivoted between photos; the evidence supports movable joints, not a claim of different left/right construction. Original creators and rights of these local photographs are unresolved; they are visual inspiration only, with no copied image or geometry.

## Game-side constraints already established

The [local PRC inventory](prc-inventory.md) shows how eagul's older framework fills fixed morph slots in a replacement vanilla piercing `.app` (128 slots per body variant, appearances by colour). Some installed item packs collide on the same morph-resource paths; profile selection does not prove which resource renders. This is useful evidence for rigged attachment and morph-following needs, **not** a catalogue to reuse or an export architecture to inherit. Its Nexus terms require author permission for copying/modifying assets. No PRC asset is used here.

The [character-customization resource-chain map](../character-customization/file-chain-map.md) connects CCXL options to `.app` definitions, mesh/morph components and materials, with illustrated `.inkcharcustomization`, `.app`, `.mesh` and `.mi` examples. A creator choice may activate one or several components, while an inventory item can use `.ent` and appearance resources. The [PRC survey](prc-inventory.md#preview-and-future-implementation-routes) identifies a possible CCXL `piercings`/`piercings_color` route and a proven *pattern* of EquipmentEx ear-slot use in Kwek's Small Fancy Hoop Earrings, but neither route yet proves our authored geometry, attachment, animation or save behavior. Keep the recipe independent of deployment route.

## Proposed composition model

One `JewelleryDesign` is a small directed assembly graph with stable IDs, authored parameters and a display name. It contains `placements` (one or more anatomical sites), `parts` (reusable generated primitives or future **owned/licensed** mesh imports), `joins` (rigid socket, hinged, chain, or pendant), `patterns` (distributed sockets and repeated ornament branches), and `materials` (metal, stone/enamel, finish). `variants` hold left/right, paired, colour and size choices without copying the whole design; a collection/preset references stable design IDs. Reserve the lowercase `xfs_` prefix for generated *game appearance names* at compilation, not for a user's editable display name. The repeated-fringe pattern is a first-class parametric assembly: storing forty near-identical hand-positioned charms would be hard to edit and would recreate the old combinatorial problem in mesh form.

```text
JewelleryDesign { id, revision, displayName,
  placements: [{ id, site, side, bindingProfile, localFrame, fitOverrides }],
  parts: [{ id, kind, parametersMm, materialSlot, sockets[] }],
  joins: [{ fromSocket, toSocket, mode, restLengthMm?, stiffness? }],
  patterns: [{ id, hostPart, socketDistribution, branchTemplate, count,
               lengthFalloff?, phase?, jitterSeed? }],
  variants: [{ id, overrides, symmetryMode }],
  previewPolicy: { chainMotion, showHiddenPost, collisionEnvelope, detailBudget }
}
```

The *site* names should be semantic (`nostril.left`, `nostril.right`, `septum`, `ear.left.lobe1`, `ear.left.helix`, etc.), not naked global XYZ. Each resolves to an oriented frame on the current character's deforming head/ear with a calibration revision and provenance. A placement can be `pierced`, `cuff/clamp`, or `surface adornment`; this prevents a decorative cuff from inventing a save-game piercing. Mirroring must swap anatomical sides and recompute handed orientation, not just negate world X; asymmetry may be intentional. A missing site/binding is an explicit unresolved state, never a silent default placement.

The initial part library needs `post/flat-back`, `butterfly back`, `earwire/fishhook`, `lever/latch`, `stud end`, `ring arc` (round or elliptical centreline, inner diameter, wire cross-section, angular gap/closure), `circular barbell`, `bead`, `bezel/prong setting`, `faceted/cabochon stone`, `cuff arc`, `link/chain`, `rigid bridge`, and `pendant/charm socket`. The local photos additionally warrant a **freeform swept-wire frame** with editable centreline, optional twist/hammered surface profile and sockets distributed by arc length; a **multi-rail hoop** with rail count, spacing and independent cross-section; a **strung bead/pin segment** with spacers; and a **fan/fringe emitter** that instantiates branches from a frame or hub. Generic silhouettes for charms can be simple sphere/disc/drop/star/geometric plate; authored shapes come later. The graph validates socket compatibility and cycles; a chain connecting two anatomical anchors is not the same as a pendant chain with one free end. A ring that visually intersects another ring (E22) should retain independent part IDs, depth planes and hinge/pivot choices; do not fuse it into a flat decal-like silhouette.

Use **millimetres internally** for diameter, wire thickness/gauge, wearable post length, stone size and connector length, with a user-facing gauge conversion where useful. The scene importer must measure its model-unit-to-mm conversion before claiming fit. Diameter and wire thickness are separate axes; for a large drop, overall height and width are independent too. Geometry can regenerate continuously within safe parameter bounds, but changing `ring` to `circularBarbell` or `post` to `cuff`, changing closure topology, stone cut, number of prongs, chain link family, rail count, branch count, or anchor count is a discrete graph edit. Frame-control points, rail spacing, strand-length gradient and branch distribution are continuous authoring controls within the chosen topology. Too-small diameter, excessive wire thickness and other invalid fits should produce a visible warning and preserve the last valid preview instead of silently clipping or distorting the part.

Each material is a *visual* specification with base colour, roughness, metallic response, optional anisotropy/texture, and stone optical intent. Preview may approximate polished steel/titanium, gold-coloured metal, matte black, enamel, pearl-like and coloured stone. Do not equate a browser PBR preset with a validated REDengine shader or with real-world material safety. Export must later map this abstract material to tested game templates, with warnings where gemstone refraction, physics or high-frequency sparkle cannot match.

## Six representative **earring** designs to review

All dimensions are **starter UI values for virtual characters**, to be calibrated against the actual head mesh and available rig. None reproduces a referenced commercial piece. The local photo IDs identify *structural lessons*, not templates to trace. Nose and septum fixtures remain important for attachment tests, but the earring collection shows why a construction set must handle expressive large-scale assemblies from the start.

| Design | Assembly and editable parameters | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sculpted multi-rail hoop** | Lobe post/latch → two or three offset rail sweeps sharing a closure, each with independent spacing, wire profile and metal finish; a single small pendant can optionally join one rail. Inspired by structural breadth in E01, E06, E31–E36. | Real front/back construction, topology-aware rail count, polished versus textured surfaces, and self-intersection under parameter changes. |
| **Interlocking oval cascade** | One small ear-mounted hoop → two or three larger open/interlocking loops, each with independent scale, plane angle and pivot; left/right variants may deliberately rotate the final loop differently. E22 is worn-scale evidence; E16/E18/E21 add scroll and nested-ring vocabulary. | Large head-context silhouette, independent rigid-body pivots, depth ordering and deliberate asymmetry. |
| **Graduated bead-spine drop** | Fishhook or stud attachment → a series of beads/spacers/links whose diameter, spacing and colour progress along the strand → terminal pear-shaped drop. E02, E07, E11, E24, E25, E28, E30 illustrate different density and closures. | Repeat with controlled gradient, material variation, transparent gem approximation and one-axis articulation. |
| **Open-frame chandelier** | Fishhook → editable pointed/oval/teardrop wire frame → optional suspended inner ornament → arc-length-distributed lower sockets, each carrying independently varied chain/bead/drop branches. E04, E12, E17 and E27 show distinct frame/fringe balance. | The core frame-plus-fringe graph, radial distribution and length falloff; performance/LOD when dozens of stones and strands move. |
| **Sculpted-wire pendant** | Fishhook → freeform 2D/3D wire centreline with twists/wraps and one or more sockets → a restrained gem/drop cluster. E08, E10, E19, E20 and E29 show why a circle-only primitive is insufficient. | Editable sweep paths, controlled filigree complexity, wire crossings and distinct wrapped-versus-smooth finishes. |
| **Asymmetric cluster set** | One ear: top hoop/ring with multiple short clustered briolettes or bead chains at graded lengths; other ear: one complementary short stud/drop, optionally a different charm family. E03 supports distinct paired motifs; E23/E26 show cluster density; E22 shows depth variation. | Pair editing without forced mirroring, independent branch motion, palette cohesion, hair/shoulder occlusion and distant-detail simplification. |

The **first ear-focused vertical slice** should include one simple post hoop and one fishhook bead-spine drop, viewed against Nathan's saved-V ears with hair on/off and neutral/morphed/idle poses. This tests two closures, front/back visibility, a pivoting strand, millimetre calibration and the large silhouette at realistic camera distances before attempting 30 moving branches. The original pinpoint nostril stud, plain nostril hoop and circular-barbell septum remain separate small **attachment fixtures** for nose-site calibration, not the dominant aesthetic specification. A stud-to-cuff chain or rigid bridge is a subsequent two-site test informed by the commercial examples above; it is not represented in Nathan's local earring folder.

## Authoring and validation boundaries

The future editor should let someone choose a site, add/swap parts, connect compatible sockets, edit physical dimensions numerically or with direct handles, select materials per part, duplicate/mirror a placement or deliberately break symmetry, save reusable components/presets, and compare neutral/animated/left/right views with hair and other head details toggled. For earrings specifically, expose **front/back/side views**, overall drop height/width, earwire/post/latch/back selection, sweep-path handles, rail count/spacing, branch count and distribution, strand-length falloff, bead-size progression, gem-facing direction and per-part pivot/rigidity. A tight ear crop is essential for construction, but face-scale preview must remain one click away: E22 shows how large interlocking loops read as a whole-head silhouette. Expose validation results at the design location: missing site, hole/skin intersection, ear or nose penetration, overlap with makeup/other piercings, occlusion by hair, and unverified material or motion mapping. A “show hidden post/back” inspection toggle helps explain why only the visible ornament appears in normal preview.

Jewellery should largely retain its own rigid shape while its attachment frame follows head/ear skin and facial morphs. For multi-point pieces, the *two* attachment frames must deform independently, then the chain/bridge recomputes between them. A rigid bridge can strain or intersect after morphing; either constrain it to suitable placements or report a fit conflict. Chain motion needs a deterministic paused pose for design and, later, optional physical/damped preview. A chandelier requires more than one physics transform: the main frame may remain rigid while each fringe strand and stone pivots separately. Separate *visual repetition* from *motion groups* so GPU instancing or simplified distant LOD need not change authored hierarchy. Do not bake one flattering camera angle into the geometry. Run sampled neutral, saved-V morph, creator idle and expression extremes; test front, profile, three-quarter, blink/jaw and close camera as relevant. For earrings include hair and glasses, collars/shoulders, and ear movement/physics when supported. Save warnings and source/candidate confidence separately from actual game-verified results.

Game compilation will have to decide whether a design appears as a CCXL creator choice, an inventory/EquipmentEx fashion item, or both. Since PRC's replacement bank collides by resource path, our authored design identities should not depend on PRC slot numbers. Keep the studio recipe/SQLite object independent of `.app`, `.ent`, `.morphtarget` and TweakDB packing; create deployment adapters only after an isolated, legal owned-geometry fixture works and the user reviews the interaction model. Compile only selected designs and deduplicate shared material/geometry recipes so arbitrary colours and components do not become another unmanageable matrix.

## Decisions for Nathan before jewellery authoring starts

1. **First scope:** Should the first released construction set prioritize expressive lobe earrings (including hoops, bead-spine drops and frame/fringe chandeliers) alongside basic nostril/septum pieces, or stay with simpler hoops/studs until the preview/physics foundation is firmer? The local collection makes the former the more compelling creative target, but it raises performance and motion work.
2. **Creator versus outfit:** Should facial piercings be permanent character-creator selections while removable earrings are inventory items, or should users be able to publish the same design through both routes where technically proven?
3. **Physical vocabulary:** Should freeform wire frames, multi-rail hoops and repeated fringe branches be first-class controls from the start, while distinctive custom charms remain an import/owned-model feature for later? This supports the local collection without copying a photographed design.
4. **Movement target:** Are static, believable chain drapes sufficient for first export, while browser animation/physics are a later quality tier? Their in-game physics needs its own evidence.
5. **Fit responsibility:** Should designs be calibrated per head/body preset automatically where possible, or should the studio expose local fit overrides for every saved-V face? The likely answer is both, but it affects recipe portability and user experience.
