# Separately versioned direct-light Glitter browser study

24 September 2026, isolated `codex/glitter-studio-integration` worktree. The main Studio now offers **Try direct-light glints** on a Glitter layer, alongside the existing raster candidate. Choosing it explicitly advances the portable document to `xfs/recipe-8` and stores `uv-cell-direct-1` with density, fine-facet share, strength, seed and independent facet colour. Layer colour remains the base pigment. Recipe-7 `irregular-planar-1` data, recipe-1–6 legacy appearances, old SQLite revisions and normal mask/PNG authoring remain supported. The preset compiler continues to reject every Glitter model. This is a **browser-only experimental material**, without REDengine equivalence or game export.

The direct model reuses the independently authored Experiment 007 UV-cell polygon shader as a foundation. Its own explicit profile uses a stable 1536-cell UV grid, one seeded jittered facet per occupied cell, 75% fine facets by default, smaller fine/coarse radius populations, and a 3×3 local lookup per fragment. Density changes occupancy without reshuffling surviving cell identities. The original 960-cell pilot profile remains selectable in the isolated study; recipe-7's raster field is not silently changed. A 0.4 clearcoat with .24 clearcoat roughness and .55 base roughness gives the pigment a continuous browser sheen below direct-light glints. The shader varies response with view/light orientation and fades discrete sampling at minification. These constants are visual hypotheses, not measured cosmetic material properties.

The worker continues to publish only the complete generated shape mask; this direct model needs no per-flake normal/surface/albedo maps. Material activation waits until that mask is complete. Switching finish, disabling or replacing layers disposes the shader owner and any prior raster maps. The preview-quality estimate counts one mask map for direct layers: 23 MiB at 1K or 91 MiB at 2K for the single enabled layer in the local study, versus 575 MiB for the 2K raster candidate. These are conservative generated-texture estimates and omit native assets, framebuffers and browser/driver overhead.

Visual inspection used the isolated `?verify=1` Chrome preview at 1K and 2K, both full-face and close-eye framings, with a #592640 base matching the purple reference's broad hue. The direct candidate produces many smaller, independent bright glints instead of the raster candidate's broader pale patches; at face scale the pigment sheen remains continuous between specks. In two same-camera close-eye 410×135 and 400×135 screen crops, a +30/255 luminance difference from each model's own zero-facet baseline gave:

| Model and preview tier | Left bright components; median area | Right bright components; median area |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Direct at 1K | 301; 3 px | 197; 3 px |
| Direct at 2K | 299; 3 px | 194; 3 px |
| Raster `350k/.00045` at 2K | 163; 13 px | 149; 10 px |

The close-eye direct result is almost tier-invariant because its facet field is evaluated at screen pixels rather than baked into the preview atlas. The screen comparison is a JPEG-screenshot contrast proxy, **not a flake count**; the two models have different base shading, but each is compared only to its own zero-facet state. Non-eye forehead/cheek controls had zero changed pixels at the same threshold. The earlier [visibility audit](../../experiments/007-irregular-glitter/visibility-audit.md) found 758 small highpass components (median 3 px) in a 400×135 crop of user reference 6 and 327 (median 5 px) in a 420×165 crop of reference 7. Photo highpass also includes texture, eyelashes and image processing, and is not numerically interchangeable with a rendered on/off difference. The direct candidate's small component size is closer to those references, but reference 6 still appears more densely and irregularly clustered across the full lid. The current UV-cell occupancy is nearly uniform, and the brightest glints concentrate near the lash line under this preview key light. Changing the key-light angle from 329° to 90° moved strong sparkle from the right lid toward the left while maintaining the pigment shape, confirming light dependence but exposing uneven response. Angular white streaks at close range also remain more graphic than the photographs.

On the local Chrome preview with the tab brought to the foreground, rolling 180-frame intervals at both 1K and 2K close-eye framings measured 16.7 ms median and about 16.8–16.9 ms p95; raster at 2K also measured 16.7 ms median and 16.9 ms p95. CPU `renderer.render` call duration was roughly 0.4–0.6 ms p95, which is **not a GPU-time measurement**. Background Chrome throttled the tab to about one frame per second, so the foreground condition is essential. These results support responsiveness on this one device and scene, not a cross-hardware performance guarantee.

Validation: `bun run check`, browser bundle build and all 211 authoring tests passed in the isolated worktree, including old recipe/flake hashes, worker cancellation, schema/SQLite/collection round-trips, direct mask-only bundles, material disposal and the game-export guard. No game launch, private reference image or derived game asset is part of this checkpoint. The locally linked GLB fixtures remain ignored. Further visual acceptance requires a reference-guided nonuniform/clustered distribution, better treatment of close-range angular streaks and light/view testing beyond one key-light pose; game material mapping remains a separate unresolved question.
