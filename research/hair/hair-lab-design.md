## Authored data and identity

The groom stores a root-population seed and budget, a surface binding, volume graph, field expressions, fibre properties and solver configuration. A root ID is derived from the population seed and its population index, independently of preview density. Each evaluated root also has its scalp triangle and barycentric attachment. Morphing moves attachments without changing identity. A changed topology invalidates the binding and requires explicit rebinding; it must never silently move roots to unrelated triangles.

New grooms use root population → two major sections → sixteen root-chart clumps per section → natural termination. Existing authored graphs remain unchanged. The logical population defaults to 60,000 fibres; the preview solves a deterministic nested subset of 256 samples. Empty preview clumps do not claim evaluated geometry: the verification V populates 26 of the 32 default clumps. These are prototype budgets, not counts measured from the reference character. Length defaults to 0.55 m intrinsic; the default's measured vertical extent is approximately 0.535 m. Visible extent depends on roots, waves and drape.

A volume carries an incoming membership and material coordinates, with length, wave and spread fields. Branches partition incoming identities with a deterministic selection map. Convergence accepts only disjoint identities, unions them and retains individual roots, consumed length and phase. Termination records ended identities; it never reallocates their budget. The validator rejects cycles, duplicate IDs, overlapping convergence, missing outlets, invalid units, negative/non-finite lengths and references to absent nodes. Branch/convergence conservation is tested in M1 even though interactive sectioning belongs to M3. A minimal convergence tie is brought forward for the structural regression test: a head-bound target sphere gathers its members, retains a sampled hold, and releases the remaining intrinsic length into a free tail. Braids and interactive tie editing remain later work.

## Fields and coordinates

Fields are expression data, evaluated only at requested sample points. Each declares a value unit (`m`, `rad`, `ratio` or a named physical compliance unit) and a chart. Sample coordinates include stable follicle ID, root UV, child cross-section coordinates, intrinsic distance from root and distance from volume entrance. Cross-section plus distance is a three-dimensional material chart, not an ordinary scalp bitmap.

Transport first maps a child's sample back through its identity mapping into the parent's chart; then it evaluates the parent expression and combines local layers in order. It must not substitute the child's raw UV for the parent's UV. Distance from the root and wave phase survive every branch and convergence. Transport and evaluation are pure, with no raster allocation.

Supported expression intent: constant, ramp, deterministic identity variation, smoothstep threshold, multiply, override, masked blend, soft minimum and soft maximum. Multiply requires a dimensionless multiplier; min/max/blend require matching units. Soft extrema use a smoothness in the same units as their operands. Masks are clamped to [0,1]. Zero-width thresholds and singular transforms are validation failures. Display/painting may materialise a field later, but images are not authoritative in M1.

## Scalp and contact

The scalp domain is the bald head mesh's scalp region, bound to head topology independently of the selected hairstyle. Follicle density is an authored scalp-space field with a soft hairline. At creation only, a usable resolved cap seeds this map by projection; record the cap resource and geometry revision. Without one, seed a conservative anatomical estimate, excluding face, ears and neck. Subsequent hairstyle selections never reseed or change an existing groom. Length is a root map too.

Vanilla cap study (game 2.31, native resolver, base archives only): female choices 01 (`hh_033_wa__player`), 24 (`hh_064_wa__bob_fringe`) and 28 (`hh_083_wa__ponytail_base_01`) each use a 746-vertex cap surface at the same bind-space extent. Their mask images were inspected directly. Choice 01 has fine feathered edge strokes and a directional split; 24 has dense bob coverage and a crown whorl; 28 has a drawn-back hairline and a patterned shaved nape. Geometry alone therefore cannot seed density faithfully: project the red mask channel through cap UV and exact nearest-surface barycentrics, then constrain it to head scalp topology. The private projections onto the verification head populated 679, 697 and 712 vertices respectively, with zero positive excluded-region vertices. These are resource observations, not game rendering proof.

The scalp field is bound to head topology; a UV-island edge is not an anatomical hairline. Restricting coverage to crown-connected UV islands produced a right-angle temple notch where vanilla coverage crosses a UV seam. The current map uses geometric forehead/ear/neck guards and the projected native hairline profile across that seam. Coverage is rasterised in the resolved cap's UV chart, including texel-centre alignment and border extrusion, and records region leakage. The existing gradient-recolour cap adapter supplies V's resolved hair colour. Density and edge softness control coverage; the material retains the game adapter's opacity. Tint-only and paths-only captures are required when judging hairline changes.

Replacement removes the selected hairstyle's cap. M1 therefore supplies a decal-style scalp tint modulated by the authored density map and V's colour profile through the existing cap-material path where usable. M2 adds dense inner cards. The tint must be ready before hair supersession.

Solve and generate in bind-pose head space, using the bind-pose head-space down vector for gravity; attach generated output rigidly to Head (equivalent to 100% Head skinning). Idle and poses move it without re-solving. Dynamic motion is reserved for M4.

Area-weighted deterministic sampling produces barycentric roots inside eligible triangles. The population surface binding fixes candidate ordering and topology so preview sample counts cannot change roots. Root emergence uses the local scalp tangent and part field; it is not simply a permanently outward skin normal. An inferred central part is the initial default, with user-made major sections added in M3.

Contact uses the actual head and ear surfaces in head coordinates, and a second distance grid from the actual neck/shoulder/upper-torso skin. Fitted capsules are only a fallback when that surface is unavailable; they are not used as collision geometry beside the body grid. A missing body gives head-only contact with an explicit limitation in the panel's status. Capsules must not be described as clothing collision. A signed-distance grid of the head and ears is built once per geometry revision in the worker and reused across groom edits. A triangle acceleration tree supports construction; query grid distances and gradients for contact. Contact checks cover particles and intervening segments; endpoint-only tests can let a long segment pass through the head. M1 records minimum clearance, maximum segment error and remaining penetration rather than declaring convergence from iteration count.

## Shape regression contract

Every shape-engine revision regenerates long centre-part waves, side part with swept bangs, shoulder bob, high ponytail and one-sided undercut through the same evaluator. These are groom data: density/length maps, shared growth-field parameters, tip curvature and convergence topology. No style name is consulted by the engine. A five-by-three front/side/back contact sheet is the visual gate; missing operators and fidelity regressions stay explicit. The tie sphere is a static hold, not a dynamic attachment or export claim. A bob uses a head-space tip plane: settle full lengths, trim at their downward crossing, and retain the measured intrinsic lengths. This generalises to a later cut surface rather than changing gravity by style.

The approved direction now prioritises an end-to-end slice: path quality is gated numerically, while silhouette, crown lift and clumping are judged on shaded cards beside the game-hair benchmarks. The former path-aesthetics gate no longer blocks card work. The prototype warm target is roughly 100 ms coarse and at most 1 s refined; the original 0.5 s target remains the longer-term goal. The five data-only regressions are loose waves, swept side part, a jaw-length bob matched to the selected benchmark, high ponytail and side shave. No style name is read by the evaluator.

Derived volumes retain member identities and cross-section coordinates in transported frames. Their covariance records width, thickness and cross-correlation; ring budgets follow the intrinsic span between a root, convergence hold and termination. A convergence has incoming clumps and an explicit outgoing tail volume. The unlit diagnostic uses section hues, distinct clump lightness and shrunken, tapered covariance cores to expose overlap and gaps. These cores are approximate spread displays, not containment bounds or authored surfaces. They remain future grab targets.

## Coarse sweep and drape

The immediate sweep starts 10–30 degrees above an authored scalp tangent growth field, then follows the head signed-distance surface before falling. A crown whorl combed away from a centre part steers front sections around the face toward the ears; shoulder contact separates front and rear lengths. Volume is supported layer thickness, not an outward launch. The first 1–3 cm retain emergence; long lengths use compliant bending and tangential contact sliding. Three diagnostic candidates compare tangent descent, surface-supported descent and surface-supported face framing before card rendering. It produces a limited preview while a worker refines the same identities.

Refinement follows the compliant-constraint update in [XPBD by Macklin, Müller and Chentanez](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf). Use fixed deterministic steps and constraint ordering, root attachments, length constraints, bending relative to rest curvature, head/ear/shoulder contact and limited bundle separation. Compliance is scaled by the squared time step and constraint multipliers are accumulated within the step. Pin weights and inverse mass must participate in constraint gradients. Record residuals after contact; do not silently lengthen strands to remove penetration.

The derived samples represent a volume at several root/cross-section positions. They are not one centreline with a radius. Current solve budget: 256 paths, at most 64 adaptive intervals, 12 steps and 8 constraint iterations. Solver limits are data, so smaller fixtures exercise identical rules. Contact-aware arclength sampling retains tight bends, roots and holds. Voxel-distance smoothing, continuous scalp support and a constrained guide filter remove millimetre reversals before sampling. Refined results fail automatically above 350 m⁻¹ curvature, 1.65 radians turning angle or 250 m⁻¹ adjacent curvature jump, excluding explicit holds. These are prototype diagnostic bounds, not measured biological limits. This is a static styling solve, without game Dyng motion.

Cutting happens after settling. The complete tip-curl deformation is limited against segment contact, then retained lengths are measured. A valid cut receives no extra gravity; later terminated continuations relax only failed constraints. Tiny length increases extend the last free span instead of appending a sub-millimetre segment that could fold on the next wave edit. Automatic topology changes during editing remain out of scope.

### Collision and continuation research

Nearest-triangle normal signs failed at acute neck boundaries, including a reproducible asset-free capped-dome fixture. Capping physical boundary loops alone did not fix that sign ambiguity. Oriented scanline crossings supply occupancy instead. Triangle-distance interpolation then exposed narrow ear traps, and separate capped head/body fields created an internal neck seam. The current candidate constructs the head/body union and derives fine head/ear/neck and coarser torso fields from that same solid, with overlapping query domains. Binary closing seals one-voxel grooves before a separable anisotropic Euclidean distance transform. Grid cells bound spatial accuracy; positive grid clearance is not exact triangle or in-game clearance.

The distance transform follows the mathematical lower-envelope construction in [Pedro F. Felzenszwalb and Daniel P. Huttenlocher, Distance Transforms of Sampled Functions](https://cs.brown.edu/people/pfelzens/papers/dt-final.pdf), Theory of Computing 8 (2012), DOI 10.4086/toc.2012.v008a019, Sections 2.1–2.2. Figure 1 and the adjacent construction were inspected. The implementation is independent; the authors' C++ download was not used. An exhaustive small-grid oracle checks anisotropic squared distances.
