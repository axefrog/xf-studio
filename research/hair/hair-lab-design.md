# Hair Lab prototype

Status: **M1 model approved by the coordinator with amendments; implementation in progress**. Hair Lab is an optional experiment, outside the first public release's requirements. The approved target is an interactive, loosely wavy, mid-back groom on the user's own V. The authored representation is a strand-budget volume graph. Curves are derived evaluation samples, never authored guides.

## Authored data and identity

The groom stores a root-population seed and budget, a surface binding, volume graph, field expressions, fibre properties and solver configuration. A root ID is derived from the population seed and its population index, independently of preview density. Each evaluated root also has its scalp triangle and barycentric attachment. Morphing moves attachments without changing identity. A changed topology invalidates the binding and requires explicit rebinding; it must never silently move roots to unrelated triangles.

The initial graph is root population â†’ one volume â†’ natural termination. Its logical population defaults to 60,000 fibres; M1 solves a deterministic nested subset, initially 256 samples. These numbers are prototype budgets, not counts measured from the reference character. Length defaults to 0.55 m of intrinsic fibre length; visible extent depends on roots, waves and drape. Tune silhouette against the supplied references before calling it mid-back.

A volume carries an incoming membership and material coordinates, with length, wave and spread fields. Branches partition incoming identities with a deterministic selection map. Convergence accepts only disjoint identities, unions them and retains individual roots, consumed length and phase. Termination records ended identities; it never reallocates their budget. The validator rejects cycles, duplicate IDs, overlapping convergence, missing outlets, invalid units, negative/non-finite lengths and references to absent nodes. Branch/convergence conservation is tested in M1 even though interactive sectioning belongs to M3. A minimal convergence tie is brought forward for the structural regression test: a head-bound target sphere gathers its members, retains a sampled hold, and releases the remaining intrinsic length into a free tail. Braids and interactive tie editing remain later work.

## Fields and coordinates

Fields are expression data, evaluated only at requested sample points. Each declares a value unit (`m`, `rad`, `ratio` or a named physical compliance unit) and a chart. Sample coordinates include stable follicle ID, root UV, child cross-section coordinates, intrinsic distance from root and distance from volume entrance. Cross-section plus distance is a three-dimensional material chart, not an ordinary scalp bitmap.

Transport first maps a child's sample back through its identity mapping into the parent's chart; then it evaluates the parent expression and combines local layers in order. It must not substitute the child's raw UV for the parent's UV. Distance from the root and wave phase survive every branch and convergence. Transport and evaluation are pure, with no raster allocation.

Supported expression intent: constant, ramp, deterministic identity variation, smoothstep threshold, multiply, override, masked blend, soft minimum and soft maximum. Multiply requires a dimensionless multiplier; min/max/blend require matching units. Soft extrema use a smoothness in the same units as their operands. Masks are clamped to [0,1]. Zero-width thresholds and singular transforms are validation failures. Display/painting may materialise a field later, but images are not authoritative in M1.

## Scalp and contact

The scalp domain is the bald head mesh's scalp region, bound to head topology independently of the selected hairstyle. Follicle density is an authored scalp-space field with a soft hairline. At creation only, a usable resolved cap seeds this map by projection; record the cap resource and geometry revision. Without one, seed a conservative anatomical estimate, excluding face, ears and neck. Subsequent hairstyle selections never reseed or change an existing groom. Length is a root map too.

Vanilla cap study (game 2.31, native resolver, base archives only): female choices 01 (`hh_033_wa__player`), 24 (`hh_064_wa__bob_fringe`) and 28 (`hh_083_wa__ponytail_base_01`) each use a 746-vertex cap surface at the same bind-space extent. Their mask images were inspected directly. Choice 01 has fine feathered edge strokes and a directional split; 24 has dense bob coverage and a crown whorl; 28 has a drawn-back hairline and a patterned shaved nape. Geometry alone therefore cannot seed density faithfully: project the red mask channel through cap UV and exact nearest-surface barycentrics, then constrain it to head scalp topology. The private projections onto the verification head populated 679, 697 and 712 vertices respectively, with zero positive excluded-region vertices. These are resource observations, not game rendering proof.

The head's index-connected UV islands provide a topology-based scalp boundary independent of hairstyle. Crown-reaching islands exclude the large face/ear island and the neck islands; geometric forehead/ear/neck guards supplement this boundary. Coverage generation constrains density before rasterising it on the head UV chart, and records region leakage. The preview uses the existing gradient-recolour cap adapter with this generated mask and the shown V's resolved gradient. Tint stays subtle; dense inner cards supply the later coverage. Tint-only and strands-only captures are required when judging hairline changes.

Replacement removes the selected hairstyle's cap. M1 therefore supplies a decal-style scalp tint modulated by the authored density map and V's colour profile through the existing cap-material path where usable. M2 adds dense inner cards. The tint must be ready before hair supersession.

Solve and generate in bind-pose head space, using the bind-pose head-space down vector for gravity; attach generated output rigidly to Head (equivalent to 100% Head skinning). Idle and poses move it without re-solving. Dynamic motion is reserved for M4.

Area-weighted deterministic sampling produces barycentric roots inside eligible triangles. The population surface binding fixes candidate ordering and topology so preview sample counts cannot change roots. Root emergence uses the local scalp tangent and part field; it is not simply a permanently outward skin normal. An inferred central part is the initial default, with user-made major sections added in M3.

Contact uses the actual head and ear surfaces in head coordinates, and a second distance grid from the actual neck/shoulder/upper-torso skin. Fitted capsules are only a fallback when that surface is unavailable; they are not used as collision geometry beside the body grid. A missing body gives head-only contact with an explicit limitation in the panel's status. Capsules must not be described as clothing collision. A signed-distance grid of the head and ears is built once per geometry revision in the worker and reused across groom edits. A triangle acceleration tree supports construction; query grid distances and gradients for contact. Contact checks cover particles and intervening segments; endpoint-only tests can let a long segment pass through the head. M1 records minimum clearance, maximum segment error and remaining penetration rather than declaring convergence from iteration count.

## Shape regression contract

Every shape-engine revision regenerates long centre-part waves, side part with swept bangs, shoulder bob, high ponytail and one-sided undercut through the same evaluator. These are groom data: density/length maps, shared growth-field parameters, tip curvature and convergence topology. No style name is consulted by the engine. A five-by-three front/side/back contact sheet is the visual gate; missing operators and fidelity regressions stay explicit. The tie sphere is a static hold, not a dynamic attachment or export claim. A bob uses a head-space tip plane: settle full lengths, trim at their downward crossing, and retain the measured intrinsic lengths. This generalises to a later cut surface rather than changing gravity by style.

Before cards, the volume display gate requires no collider penetration (per-collider measurements and four views), the default reference silhouette, at least four recognisable styles including a crude tied ponytail, continuous small-step map edits, roughly 100 ms coarse and at most 1 s warm refinement, and an authored section-to-clump hierarchy. Derived clump envelopes use member spread for elliptical cross-sections, flat section colours and visible budget/topology. They are display and future grab targets, never authored surfaces. Cards will be derived from these volumes. The 1 s refinement allowance is the coordinator's prototype relaxation of the original 0.5 s target.

## Coarse sweep and drape

The immediate sweep starts 10–30 degrees above an authored scalp tangent growth field, then follows the head signed-distance surface before falling. A crown whorl combed away from a centre part steers front sections around the face toward the ears; shoulder contact separates front and rear lengths. Volume is supported layer thickness, not an outward launch. The first 1–3 cm retain emergence; long lengths use compliant bending and tangential contact sliding. Three diagnostic candidates compare tangent descent, surface-supported descent and surface-supported face framing before card rendering. It produces a limited preview while a worker refines the same identities.

Refinement follows the compliant-constraint update in [XPBD by Macklin, MÃ¼ller and Chentanez](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf). Use fixed deterministic steps and constraint ordering, root attachments, length constraints, bending relative to rest curvature, head/ear/shoulder contact and limited bundle separation. Compliance is scaled by the squared time step and constraint multipliers are accumulated within the step. Pin weights and inverse mass must participate in constraint gradients. Record residuals after contact; do not silently lengthen strands to remove penetration.

The derived samples represent a volume at several root/cross-section positions. They are not one centreline with a radius. Initial solve budget: 256 paths Ã— 24 intervals, 12 fixed steps Ã— 8 constraint iterations. Solver limits are data, so a smaller verification fixture exercises identical rules. This is a static styling solve; it does not implement the game's Dyng motion. Rest-shape edits persist; future Clip adds a hold, while temporary pushes remain session-only. Automatic fine splits retain deterministic lineage and stay fixed throughout a gesture; M1 has no automatic topology changes during editing.

## Verification and budgets

All budgets are proposals until measured. End-to-end timing starts at action dispatch and ends at the first frame showing the matching result, with geometry preparation, queue time, generation, upload and shader readiness reported separately. A fast solver alone is not a fast interaction.

| Boundary | Initial budget |
|---|---|
| Flag off | Zero Hair Lab imports, workers, geometry extraction or extra asset requests; no module/menu/panel registrations |
| Action feedback | Selection/value/progress updated in the same cycle |
| Coarse first frame | At most 50 ms for already prepared surfaces; coarse generation at most 8 ms on page thread |
| Refined first frame | Target at most 500 ms warm; report misses with the largest measured stage |
| Page responsiveness | No Hair Lab page task over 50 ms; worker jobs abort/yield at bounded iteration batches |
| Working memory | At most 128 MiB incremental CPU buffers and 16 MiB diagnostic GPU buffers initially; full suites under a 6 GB guard |

Model-derived scenarios, to be written before product implementation after review:

1. Start with default preferences and visit every shell menu: no lab imports/requests or contributions. Enable the flag, activate, edit, deactivate, reload; normal hair and original character/save/draft bytes are preserved throughout.
2. Verification override on/off never changes ordinary preferences. Unknown flags and malformed overrides cannot activate modules. Toggle off during dynamic import, geometry preparation, solve and GPU attachment; no stale activation is possible.
3. One seeded population evaluated at two preview budgets has nested identical IDs and attachment coordinates. Branch then disjoint convergence preserves identity and intrinsic distances; duplicate/overlapping membership is refused; termination accounts for every ended fibre.
4. A non-identity child chart yields the same inherited values as direct parent evaluation. Check composition order, unit mismatch, mask bounds, soft boundaries, phase continuity and invalid transforms.
5. Same inputs produce identical coarse/solved arrays. Root error stays within 1 micrometre; final segment length error target is under 2%; contact clearance target is at least 0.5 mm with penetration reported. Check long segments crossing a head, contact plus attachment, degenerate geometry and extreme allowed values.
6. Change V or groom while a result is outstanding. Only the matching revision can attach; old work is aborted, buffers are released, and restored normal hair is never hidden by a stale result. Repeat with two lab scenes to exclude implicit singleton state.
7. Worker crash, missing geometry and invalid scalp produce a plain message plus Try again; normal hair remains. A second editing action pre-empts the first solve. Undo/Redo restores document entries and reproducible output without rewriting historical entries.
8. Capture every added UI state in light/dark at narrow/wide widths and the groom from four views in an isolated workspace. Compare supplied photos read-only; do not put those photos or paths in public evidence.

### Cap rendering checks

The generated cap retains the resolved vanilla topology and UVs. Projecting its vertices alone caused triangle interiors to intersect the finer head mesh: 1,846 of 12,450 barycentric samples were inside, with 0.86 mm maximum depth. Adaptive fitting now checks interiors with 0.40 mm target clearance and keeps duplicate UV-seam vertices together. A high-pass mask autocorrelation diagnostic flags repeated holes independently of geometric clearance.

The cap uses V's resolved cap gradient through a caller-owned copy of template shading. Geometry-specific eight-influence shader extensions must not be copied onto generated four-influence geometry; the new material-copy adapter preserves template shading while removing that foreign skin layout. An extra 0.9 opacity multiplier caused a 32–41/255 luminance mismatch. Preserving the adapter's own opacity reduced the front/side/back crown-patch RGB differences to at most 5.81/255, within the declared 10/255 tolerance, against a base-game 01 cap rendered on the same V. These patches exclude the part and hairline.
