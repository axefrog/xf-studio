# Saved V hair colour and material resolution

24 September 2026. Read-only inspection of Nathan's selected MO2 profile `2025 (again)`, the captured `AutoSave-12` hair choice, installed ArchiveXL resources, and the local 2.31 game archives. Extracted resources and converted PNGs were kept in a temporary directory; this note records paths, hashes and conclusions, not third-party payloads. It extends the [saved-V hair preview](saved-v-hair-preview.md), whose visible geometry currently has approximate colour and shading.

## Result and confidence

The save's `hairs` choice is the MELUMINARY `lm097_hair.app` resource hash **14407260537193084196** with definition **`38_ash_brown`**. These are contributions from **two** enabled mods: MELUMINARY Long Length Pak Vol 3 supplies the hairstyle and its two mesh parts; [island_dancer's Hair Profiles CCXL](https://www.nexusmods.com/cyberpunk2077/mods/19115) supplies the `38_ash_brown` creator definition, `ash_brown.hp`, its cap gradient and a material/appearance patch. The MELUMINARY `.app` and meshes contain an example `01_blonde_platinum` appearance, not a baked `38_ash_brown` material. ArchiveXL is expected to compose the saved colour dynamically from these inputs. This is a **source-supported expectation**, not a measured in-game material binding or a rendering match.

The current browser preview uses both real mesh parts and the source `hair_lm60_a` strand alpha, but colours all cards and cap with an approximate dark-brown material. It omits the `ash_brown.hp` strand-ID/root-tip gradients, `hair_lm60_id`, `hair_lm60_grad`, `hair_lm60_f` flow, separate cap gradient and cap mask shader, and REDengine's hair scattering/anisotropic response. No standalone normal texture is referenced by the two inspected MELUMINARY material instances; describing the preview as simply "missing the normal map" would misdiagnose the resource chain. Physics bones remain at rest in the browser.

### Actual profile and texture bindings

The installed `ash_brown.hp` is a `CHairProfile` with five `gradientEntriesID` stops and five `gradientEntriesRootToTip` stops, `sampleCount=127`. Its root-tip stops are `(0.1463, RGB 16/10/8)`, `(0.4108, 186/165/142)`, `(0.5684, 143/123/110)`, `(0.8308, 65/47/41)`, `(1.0000, 193/168/149)`; these are raw stored values, **not** promised on-screen colours without the shader. `gradientEntriesID` contains stops near `0.116`, `0.552`, `0.996`, and two at `1.0`. The matching cap-gradient image converts to **32×4 RGBA**. It is a separate colour input for the scalp/cap and cannot be replaced faithfully by colouring the cap triangles uniformly.

### Flow-map and specular follow-up, 24 September

The still-missing `hair_lm60_f.xbm` was independently extracted from MELUMINARY Long Vol 3's installed archive by depot hash `7349194629445346418`, and its extracted bytes matched the recorded SHA-256 `cadf11d8a2d6647563efef7eda4ae1d56a812b4e3ecc9fd3db904c5807cdd1ed`. WolvenKit 8.17.4 converted it to a 512×512 RGBA PNG (SHA-256 `c9974c20fb0474a4fede28a8394a9e7eecc39aa53936d14b5ba55efec75e6968`). The map is effectively constant: **262,142 of 262,144 pixels** are `(163,255,105,255)` and the other two are `(163,254,106,255)`. This confirms the source `Flow` input exists, while showing that it contains almost no spatial variation to recover in this style. These measurements refer to the installed source/conversion, not a proven effective game winner. The converted source stayed in ignored local space.

The [material-property guide](https://github.com/CDPR-Modding-Documentation/Cyberpunk-Modding-Docs/blob/be2f44eed8419342ec13f72ed9cab008e9f7b289/for-mod-creators-theory/materials/configuring-materials/hair-and-skin-material-properties.md) identifies `Flow` as anisotropic rotation and `FlowStrength` as its influence; the MELUMINARY material instance's low `RoughnessScale`/`RoughnessBias` indicates a shinier response than the browser's current `roughness=0.65`. Neither source specifies how to translate the REDengine flow channels, roughness terms, scattering and lights into Three.js. A temporary local `MeshPhysicalMaterial` trial loaded the verified Flow PNG through Three's anisotropy map and lowered roughness. It compiled without browser shader errors, but the first front-view frame developed broad pale/silver streaks over the brown cards. Raising roughness and reducing environment reflection restored the brown silhouette, then looked almost the same as the existing version-2 preview at a matched camera and lighting. The source map's near-constant pixels explain why a texture-driven directional effect is slight here. The temporary visibility hook and material/schema changes were removed; there is **no accepted preview change** from this trial.

The next useful fidelity step is a matched-lighting game frame of this exact `lm097_hair`/`38_ash_brown` choice, including a slight camera yaw, before choosing a browser BRDF mapping. The current front view already shows dark roots and lighter AshBrown strands, but a browser-only adjustment cannot establish which highlights or greys are true to game rendering. The two inspected material instances still reference no separate hair normal map, and the browser still does not model hair scattering or physics. This follow-up learns from MELUMINARY's private installed material/texture and the credited community guide; it copies no mod asset into tracked output. ### Cap UV tile correction, 24 September

The installed MELUMINARY cap GLB's 798 UV0 vertices occupy U **2.015625–2.982422** and V **0.093750–0.997259**. The source Wizzy cap mask is a single 1024² tile; every pixel of its rightmost column is black, while its centre column spans 0–255 and averages 214.3. Three.js defaults to clamp addressing, so the preview previously sampled the mask's black right edge for the whole cap. This caused the cap's alpha test to remove it entirely, even though its separate source mask and AshBrown cap gradient were present. The strand meshes' UV0 coordinates are within 0–1, so their addressing was already appropriate.

The browser now repeats **only the cap mask's U address**. Its opacity and cap-gradient lookup both use that same mask, with V unchanged. In an isolated `?verify=1` session at port 4453 using the exact saved hash/definition and ignored local assets, a temporary cap-only diagnostic showed a bald head with clamping and the fitted brown scalp cap after U repeat. The diagnostic was removed before commit; re-enabling all cards preserved the saved-V hair toggle. [The measured checkpoint](../../projects/xf-studio/authoring/evidence/hair-cap-wrap-2026-09-24.json) records the private source digests and test limits. This fixes a browser texture-addressing error, **not** the remaining pale/flat card lighting, REDengine alpha or strand shading, hair physics, or exact game appearance. The guide/resource chain above established the cap mask's role; the UV and pixel measurements here come from the privately extracted installed assets.

The three saved strand meshes stay within U=0.011–0.965 and V=0.007–0.976, so their ID, root-tip and opacity textures do not need the cap's repeat address. The converted 2048² `Strand_Alpha` image is greyscale RGB with nearly opaque PNG alpha; the browser correctly uses its green channel. About 70.9% of source texels fall below 31/255, illustrating how much card space is cut away by the current alpha test. Three.js currently treats those cards as opaque cutouts with multisample alpha-to-coverage and depth writes, so there is no per-card transparent sorting. This is a deliberate preview approximation, **not** proof that REDengine's `AlphaCutoff=0` has equivalent coverage, mip or ordering behavior. The cards still look pale and layered in the isolated full-hair view after the cap fix; changing their threshold, gradient blend, roughness or sorting without a matched game frame would conflate separate causes.
