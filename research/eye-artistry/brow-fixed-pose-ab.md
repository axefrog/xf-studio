## Browser observation and screen-space measure

The 24 September isolated local browser run used a 481×720 pixel grid for *both* panes. The comparison page first renders each brow alone against a transparent background at the identical pose/camera, then measures alpha before rendering the normal shaded head. The screen-space silhouette count (`alpha >25/255` and `>127/255`) is separate from the brow-only mean display RGB inside the latter mask. This is a raster measure, not a geometric area or a measured game silhouette. A unit test proves the count ignores RGB changes. At the fixed view:

| Right-pane candidate | `alpha >25` pixels, left → right | `alpha >127` pixels, left → right | Brow-only mean RGB, left → right | Visual observation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Current skin + current brow | 7,948 → 7,948 | 3,488 → 3,488 | 58.2 → 58.2 | Identical fixed baseline. |
| Base D05 skin + current brow | 7,948 → 7,948 | 3,488 → 3,488 | 58.2 → 58.2 | Base source skin adds pink eye-area colouring; brow pixels themselves do not change. |
| Arkhe skin candidate + current brow | 7,948 → 7,948 | 3,488 → 3,488 | 58.2 → 58.2 | Dark eye-area colouring and different skin shading change the perceived brow edge without changing brow opacity. |
| Current skin + direct packed-normal probe | 7,948 → 7,948 | 3,488 → 3,488 | 58.2 → 86.0 | Brow becomes silvery/washed out in the standard Three material. |

The alpha bounds were `x=78..337, y=369..422` for both sides. I visually inspected neutral, base D05, Arkhe and packed-normal panes in the browser. The source-set change explains some *perceived* contour difference at this one view; it does **not** prove the reported game/studio thickness discrepancy is entirely shading. The current GLB, alpha path, pose and camera yielded no screen-space silhouette difference across the variants. Directly assigning the decoded normal to a Three `normalMap` at the exploratory `(0.8, −0.8)` scale makes the brow conspicuously too light. The game's `NormalAlpha≈0.8` is **not** a Three normal scale; the post-G-buffer decal's normal/skin mixing, tangent frame and lighting are absent here. Reject this naive normal assignment as a production fix. The source alpha and gradient stay in the current production material.
