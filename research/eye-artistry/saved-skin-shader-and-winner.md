## Compiled `skin` variant: what the channels do

The installed `shader_final.cache` SHA-256 is `339145371a3b5aaa08eb4ef82d558f445b632e28603ee0f3b4860270dfc3ccfa`. The audited `skin` `renderstage_gbuffer_regular`, MeshSkinned technique pairs vertex GUID `7494393843130164436` (DXBC SHA-256 `789b74c45773620d4597f792cc3e2db5af614b12e09be15cb89527e3bd8f459b`) with pixel GUID `12806642364631437234` (DXBC SHA-256 `8040f820f64055156d8223e65d1463e785ca62007db8eaae92356cadcfa36053`). The template's three installed serialized copies are identical, and its `MaterialUsedParameter` list maps `Normal`→register 4, `DetailNormal`→5, `Roughness`→8, roughness-bias min/max→9/10, `MicroDetail`→11, UV scales→12/13, microdetail influence→14, tint mask→15 and `SkinProfile`→24. Windows SDK 10.0.22621 x64 `dxc -dumpbin` exposed the DXIL operations below. These mappings are for **this compiled variant**, not a declaration that it was selected at runtime for Nathan's particular frame.

- **UV path:** the vertex shader reads input `TEXCOORD0.xy` and writes it to output `TEXCOORD2.yz` (`%20/%21` → output row 3, channels 1/2). The pixel shader reads that pair as `%28/%29` and uses it directly for the main Normal, DetailNormal, Roughness, tint mask and Albedo samples. This is UV0 in this variant. `MicroDetail` instead uses scaled/fractional UV0 and derivative-guided samples, with two frequencies from registers 12/13. A single shared, unmodified UV for every term would be wrong.
- **Packed normal:** register 4 samples only **R and G** for the main tangent normal (`%43` → `%44/%45`). Each is mapped from [0,1] to [−1,1], and Z is reconstructed as `sqrt(max(0, 1−x²−y²))` (`%46`–`%53`). The optional wrinkle stretch/squash maps (registers 20/21) and `DetailNormal` (5) repeat RG/Z reconstruction and blend via their influence terms before tangent-basis conversion. The decoded selected Arkhe normal candidate has blue fixed at zero, consistent with this packed path. Feeding its raw PNG as a conventional RGB normal would produce the wrong vector; a blanket Y-flip is not established by these operations.
- **Roughness:** register 8 samples R/G/B at UV0 (`%182` → `%183/%184/%185`). **R** is the base roughness term. Bias min/max registers 9/10 generate a spatially varying factor, and **B** gates how much that factor modifies R (`%398`–`%408`); the result is saturated (`%567`), may receive a later factor (`%1087`), and is written to G-buffer target 2 **G** (`%1095`). In simplified algebra for the first blend, `roughness = saturate(R × (1 + B × (bias − 1)))`, where `bias` is the computed min/max blend, **not** a constant. **G** is written separately to target 2 **R** and participates in another branch (`%860`–`%867`); it is not the base roughness channel in this variant. The inherited decoded roughness XBM's G is identically zero, so handing it directly to Three's green-channel `roughnessMap` would collapse this term rather than approximate the compiled path. B also weights microdetail normal and colour adjustments, so simply copying R to G captures only the base term.

The 2.31 shader cache is a compiled source of arithmetic, not proof of which variant the live head used, the rest of deferred lighting, skin translucency, dynamic wetness or post-processing. The selected `.app` may also be affected by cooked appearance caches or ArchiveXL at runtime. No claim of REDengine parity follows from matching the one G-buffer path.
