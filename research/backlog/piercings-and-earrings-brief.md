# Piercings and earrings: design brief

**Status: research and discussion brief, 30 September 2026. Nothing is built.** Piercings and earrings are later feature 1 in the [queue](README.md#later-features--discuss-with-the-maintainer-before-building-each). Each later feature is discussed with the maintainer before any editor, renderer or exporter work starts; the existing piercing preview and the staged probe mod are not permission to build a piercing editor. This brief pulls together the earlier studies, which stay the detailed references: the [CCXL piercing feasibility study](../jewellery/ccxl-piercing-feasibility.md) (26 September, with its decisions), the [jewellery construction-set proposal](../jewellery/construction-set-design.md) (24 September), the [earring reference inventory](../jewellery/earring-reference-inventory.md), [experiment 024](../../experiments/024-ccxl-piercings/README.md) (the staged in-game probe) and the knowledge pages [piercings and jewellery](../../knowledge/jewellery-resources.md) and [dangle physics](../../knowledge/hair-physics.md). It adds what was learned on 30 September (component naming and headgear, LODs, the site atlas, the community rigging workflow, materials and gems, dangles on creator pieces) and turns the lot into a build plan.

**The ask, as the product frames it.** Piercings are contributed as extra **creator (CCXL) choices** that sit beside the vanilla piercing options and never replace a preset. Behind them is a **design kit**: users design their own piercings and earrings in the Studio (shapes, metals and finishes, gems, placement on the ear and face, dangles), and Build produces an XF-branded mod.

Evidence grades follow the [knowledge rules](../../knowledge/README.md#rules-for-knowledge-pages): **[resource]** extracted game or mod resources, **[source]** framework or tool source, **[exe]** the disassembled 2.31 executable, **[wiki]** Modding Docs text or images, **[offline]** our own measurements, **[runtime]** the running game, **[hypothesis]** not yet established.

## What the research established

| Finding | Grade |
|---|---|
| **The creator's route works for us already.** A Studio-built CCXL option (XF Eye Artistry: its own row, Off plus one definition per look, `face` and `character_customization` groups, a morph-skinned component carrying head skin bytes) works in the creator, gameplay and photo mode, and survives saves. A piercing row is the same kind of option with different geometry. | [runtime] |
| **A vanilla piercing style is a chunk-mask selection from four shared "bank" meshes** (11, 13, 2 and 3 pieces), all in one colour. Each bank is skinned to the face joints under its pieces and carries only the morph regions they sit on (ear pieces: `ear`). | [resource] |
| **Vanilla pieces are small and have one level of detail.** Every bank has `lodLevelInfo` `[0]` and every chunk `lodMask` 1; pieces are 12 to 786 vertices; components cast shadows (`Always`) and hide beyond 50 m (`autoHideDistance`). No LOD work is needed to match vanilla. | [resource] (30 September) |
| **The vanilla pieces make a site atlas.** The 29 pieces sit at about 20 distinct sites: both lobes, a graded run of six helix studs up V's left ear, upper-ear pieces on V's right, both brows, the nose bridge, the right upper cheek, three lip sites, the septum and the left nostril. Three sites are left/right pairs whose centres mirror within about 1 mm (lobe studs, lip corners, bridge), which suggests the head is near-symmetric there. Sizes: lobe hoop about 12 mm, septum ring 14.5 mm wide, helix studs 1.7–3.5 mm, the large ear piece 56 mm tall. | [offline] from the bank GLBs |
| **ArchiveXL adds options additively when every name, label and index is new**; a colliding definition name or switcher label replaces the existing entry. Free row indices after vanilla Piercings: female 306–309; male 538–539 (and 510–519 just before it, 541–549 after Teeth). | [source] [resource] |
| **Headgear hides head items by component prefix.** ArchiveXL's bundled `hide_Head` tag hides components whose prefix is `i1_` (vanilla piercings are `i1_000_…`), as it hides `hx_` makeup. The prefix is the text up to the first `_` within six characters, so the probe's `xfs_probe_hoop_silver` components have the prefix `xfs_`, which nothing hides. Production pieces should be named `i1_xfs_…`, as XF makeup became `hx_xfs_…`. How vanilla helmets hide vanilla piercings (no vanilla item uses `hide_Head`) is still unread. | [source] ArchiveXL `Garment/Prefix.cpp`, `VisualTags.xl`; [resource] |
| **Rigid anchor-copied pieces are a sound fit.** The probe copies one head vertex's skin bytes and morph rows onto every piece vertex, so the piece moves exactly as that vertex. The community's PRC workflow does the same by hand: transfer weights from the head or a vanilla piece, then flatten them to one weight "since piercings are solid objects", and position the piece by hand for each shape key of its own region only. | [offline] experiment 024; [wiki] PRC piercing guide |
| **A piece morph with no moving target is dangerous.** The PRC guide warns that a morph target whose shape keys are all left at the basis "will flatline your game" (crash). A generator must refuse a piece whose morph carries no non-zero row. | [wiki] |
| **Each head shape carries its own joint binds.** The head morph target's 21 shapes (all five regions of one shape share them) move the binds of 244 of 254 joints, including the six jaw-ear joints, by up to about 13 mm in the inverse binds' translation; `Head` does not move. A piece that copies the head's per-target rig and its anchor's skin behaves as its anchor; the vanilla banks carry no per-target rig at all. What the engine does with these binds is unread. | [resource] ([facial animation](../../knowledge/facial-animation.md)) |
| **Vanilla finishes are multilayered and tied to vanilla UVs.** All 16 finishes (`silver`, `gold`, `black`, `cooper`, `red`, `pink`, `pearl`, `blue`, `mixed`, `mixed2`, `neon_teal`, `pink_metalic`, `rainbow`, `rose_gold`, `steel`, `wood`) are `multilayered.mt` with one shared `.mlmask` per bank. Plain metals come from the base layer; patterned finishes (wood, rainbow, mixed) show the mask's vanilla UV islands on new geometry, as the community guide's in-game screenshot of a wood septum shows. | [resource]; [wiki] image `custom_weird_mat.png` |
| **Multilayered colours are names, not numbers.** A layer's colour is a CName looked up in its template's colour table, so an arbitrary user colour needs another template or `metal_base` (whose `BaseColorScale` is a free colour). `metal_base` also has emission (`EmissiveEV`), which multilayered lacks; no character instance uses it. | [source] ([materials §4.6–4.7](../../knowledge/materials-and-shaders.md)) |
| **Gems have no ideal game template.** The G-buffer has one surface per pixel and no anisotropy except for hair. `glass_onesided` is a forward transparent pass with refraction, but it reflects only the sun and the probes: no spot or point lights, so the creator's lights and photo-mode lights would put no highlight on a glass gem. An opaque faceted surface on a Standard template catches every light, facet by facet. | [source] ([metal and glass](../materials/shader-metal-glass.md)) |
| **Dangles are data the Studio can already run.** Physics jewellery uses a dangle component (a small rig of `dyng_*` chains hanging from `Head` and a graph with one particle simulation). CCXL hairstyles carry such components inside their creator `.app` definitions; worn physics earrings (Kwek's hoops, Claire's) carry them too. Vanilla creator piercings carry none. The Studio's resolver, spec reader and solver ([dangle physics §4](../../knowledge/hair-physics.md#4-what-the-studio-does)) run any such set from its own data. | [resource] [exe] |
| **No installed mod adds piercings through the creator.** Of 31 jewellery and piercing mods on the reference installation, none declares `customizations`: they are PRC packs (which replace vanilla style 12, or 14 for male V) or worn items. An additive XF row is new ground. | [resource] |

## How vanilla piercings work, end to end

The detailed chain is on the [knowledge page](../../knowledge/jewellery-resources.md#1-the-chain-at-a-glance); in short:

1. **Creator rows.** A `piercings` switcher (Off + 14 styles female, Off + 16 male, labels `01`…) turns on one appearance option `piercings_NN`, whose 16 definitions fill a shared colour row linked by `piercings color` [resource].
2. **Definitions.** Each definition in `i0_000__earring_NN.app` lists up to three `i1_000_…__morphs_earring_0N` components (morph-skinned), each with a `chunkMask` (which pieces) and one `meshAppearance` (the colour) [resource].
3. **Geometry and rig.** The four banks, skinned to the face joints near their pieces (the ear bank to `Head`, the jaw-ear and jaw rows; the face bank to 73 face joints), each carrying its region's `(target, region)` morph pairs; within one piece the morph deltas vary per vertex, as if transferred from the skin under each vertex [resource].
4. **Materials.** Per-bank, per-colour `.mi` on `multilayered.mt` with one `.mlmask` and one `.mlsetup` per colour; the silver setup's base is `silver_brushed_01_300.mltemplate`, so the brushed look comes from the layer's normal and microblend, not from an anisotropic BRDF [resource] [source].
5. **Consumers.** The options are in `face` (gameplay and photo mode) and `character_customization` (the creator puppet) [resource].

How mods add piercings: PRC replaces the vanilla `earring_14.app` with a 128-slot bank that packs fill by overwriting placeholder morph targets, often with "linked" meshes bound to the full head skeleton and all 105 targets; other mods are worn items ([feasibility §3–4](../jewellery/ccxl-piercing-feasibility.md#3-could-prcs-technique-be-driven-by-creator-choices)). Both show what the engine accepts: full-head-skinned piece meshes with the head's targets, and dangle components on worn items. Neither is a route we copy.

## Authoring routes

| Route | What it is | Fit with our pipeline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Parametric kit** | Pieces generated in the Studio from a small grammar (post, ball, disc, faceted stone, captive/seam ring, circular barbell, clicker, straight and curved bar, later chains, wire sweeps and fringes), in millimetres, then fitted by the attachment solver | Experiment 024 already writes such pieces: procedural geometry, head skin bytes and morph rows copied from an anchor vertex, the head's vertex layout and quantisation, packed through WolvenKit and verified by the product verifier's replica of ArchiveXL's merge [offline]. Build still converts JSON to CR2W with WolvenKit (the native writer is future work) | **Recommended core.** Everything is owned and generated per user, sizes are exact, and preview and Build share one solver |
| **(b) Imported meshes (glTF)** | A user's own model, fitted to a site; rigid pieces take the anchor's skin and deltas like (a); a marked pivot splits a dangling part later | The Studio already reads GLB (`glb.ts`); the same writer serves. Needs checks the kit gets by construction: scale and units, vertex budget (vanilla pieces are under 800 vertices), UVs for materials, closed and non-degenerate geometry | **Later (P7).** Useful for custom charms; provenance is the user's own, and imported meshes stay in the local library, never in shared collections |
| **(c) Vanilla pieces, restyled** | Two forms. *By reference:* our component points at a vanilla bank morph target with our own `chunkMask` and `meshAppearance`, so any vanilla piece can join an XF look, and two components on one bank give mixed metals. *As a copy:* Build copies a vanilla piece's geometry from the user's own installed game (as the eye plate is cut from the head) into our mesh, so it can take our own finishes | By reference: no geometry at all, nothing shipped, nothing replaced [source: reading a vanilla path is additive]. As a copy: our writer plus a bank reader the native reader already has | **By reference: cheap, include as a part source.** Placement is fixed to vanilla sites and colours to the 16 vanilla finishes. Copies wait for demand. Third-party mod meshes are never copied or referenced (permissions, per-mod coupling) |

**Skin and morph rules.** Pieces follow the [expanded-plate rule](../../AGENTS.md#expanded-eye-plate) in spirit: skin bytes are copied from the installed head byte for byte, never re-quantised from normalised weights, and the head's per-target rig is kept. The morph rows are the anchor's own rows, so the piece's slider response equals the anchor's bit for bit [offline]. Build refuses a head route changed by an `.xl` patch, as the plate does. Whether a piece needs only its site's regions (the vanilla habit and the community's) or all of them is probe check 5.

**Clearance.** Pierced pieces are meant to pass through skin; what must not happen is a stud face sinking, a ring cutting the lobe beyond its hole, or a piece floating off. The solver can test that offline: distance from each piece's visible surface to the deformed head at the neutral head, every shape of its region (21 per region), and the facial solver's expression extremes, with a plain warning at the design location. Earrings add hair, collar and shoulder checks. This reuses the plate's surface tooling (`head-surface`, `plate-reach`) rather than new machinery.

## Placement in the 3D view

- **Sites first, free placement second.** A site catalogue (data, per body gender) names each site (`ear.left.lobe`, `ear.left.helix.1…6`, `ear.left.tragus`, `nostril.right`, `septum`, `brow.left`, `bridge`, `labret`, `lip.corner.left`, `cheek.left`, …) and resolves it to an anchor vertex and an oriented frame on the installed head. It is seeded from the vanilla atlas above and extended with hand-tagged ear sites; a site shown only on one side in vanilla is mirrored to the other.
- **Snapping.** Dragging a piece slides it over the head surface (the preview's head, deformed by the V's own sliders), snapping to site anchors within a few millimetres; the frame follows the surface normal, with a twist handle about it and a depth control (how far a stud stands proud, how deep a ring sits). Fine placement is numeric in millimetres.
- **Symmetry.** Mirror is a link, not a copy: the mirrored piece reflects X (V's left is −X), then re-projects to the surface and re-solves its own anchor, because the two sides are near but not exactly symmetric. Breaking the link keeps both pieces. Pairs with different motifs (the reference inventory's E03) are ordinary unlinked pieces.
- **Views.** A tight ear crop with front, back and side presets, one click from the whole-head view, with hair and other details toggled. A "show hidden post" inspection toggle explains what sits inside the skin.
- **Components.** Per the component-first rule, the feature specifies and the UI track builds: a surface-attached placement handle (drag, snap, twist, depth), a millimetre field with a gauge readout, a site picker (an ear and face map with the catalogue's sites), a part palette, and per-part material swatches (the existing swatch card). The existing tree view and sliders cover the look's part list and parameters.

## Dangles and physics

The first scope excludes dangles (26 September decision). When they come, the route is data the Studio already understands:

- **D0 Static drape.** A dangling part posed once at rest and exported rigid. Cheapest, no physics; fine for short drops.
- **D1 Dangle component in the look's `.app`**, the CCXL-hair pattern: an `entAnimatedComponent` (`i1_xfs_…_dangle`) with a small `.rig` (V's `Root` and `Head`, plus one `dyng_*` chain per dangling part) and a Dyng `.animgraph` with our own values, controlled by `root`. The piece mesh carries the anchor's morph rows on every vertex; its top part is skinned to `Head` (or its anchor's joints) and its dangling part to the chain. The Studio previews it with the existing solver from the generated spec, so preview and export share one data set.
- **Open points** [hypothesis]: whether a creator `face` option's animated component simulates in the creator, gameplay and photo mode (CCXL hair suggests yes); how a mesh skinned to `root` finds chain joints (worn earrings, check H5); and whether the chain's pivot follows the ear sliders. Joints don't move with morphs, so under a strong ear shape the pivot may sit up to about 6.5 mm from the lobe (the probe hoop's anchor moves that far under `h115`) unless the per-target rig also moves the chain's bind (worth one test). Mitigations: a loop or ring at the top that hides a small offset, or conservative swing cones.
- **Performance:** a Dyng frame for a 40-particle hair set costs 10–15 µs in the preview; earrings are a few particles [offline].

## Export

- **Rows.** Own XF rows per area, after the vanilla Piercings row (decided 26 September). Each row is a `gameuiAppearanceInfo` option (`xfs_piercings_<area>`, own `uiSlot`, `useThumbnails = 0`, `editTags` `NewGame`/`HairDresser`/`Ripperdoc`, `randomizeCategory` `FaceModification`), in `face` and `character_customization`, with Off first and one definition per look. Indices are picked from the user's merged creator catalogue and reported; female has four free slots after Piercings, male two (538–539), so a male third or fourth row sits just before Piercings (518–519) or after Teeth.
- **Names.** Definitions and mesh appearances `xfs_…` (the naming contract; the probe's `silver`/`gold` mesh appearances were throwaway); components `i1_xfs_…` so `hide_Head` applies; labels are unique text keys, never numbers (a numeric label would overwrite a vanilla style); resources under the collection's key and a `piercings` folder, as eye makeup's are.
- **Banks, like vanilla.** Build packs a row's pieces into bank meshes of up to 64 chunks (the chunk mask is 64 bits): each look selects its pieces with `chunkMask`, and each distinct material combination is a mesh appearance. One morph target per bank then carries the head's per-target rig once. The probe's per-piece morph targets are about 6.5 MB each (1.3 MB with only the `ear` targets), mostly that rig; banks keep a large collection small.
- **Identity and removal.** Look IDs never renumber; a look removed from a later collection leaves a tombstone definition that draws nothing, if probe check 11 shows it is needed.
- **One mod or many.** A piercings `FeatureExporter` and `FeatureVerifier` on the platform's export contract ([feature-module platform](../authoring/feature-module-platform.md#step-8-status)): merged into one "XF Looks" mod by default with eye makeup, split into "XF Piercings" on request. The verifier resolves the built package through the character resolver and the ArchiveXL merge replica, as experiment 024's does. The [Studio-to-mod pipeline](../authoring/studio-to-mod-pipeline.md) is updated in the checkpoint that builds it.

## Runtime questions for one batched session

The probe's [twelve checks](../../experiments/024-ccxl-piercings/README.md#test-card-the-12-checks) come first. Added by this brief (need the P0 rebuild unless marked):

1. **Headgear by prefix.** With the `i1_xfs_` build: a `hide_Head` mod item, then a vanilla helmet and a face mask, each with vanilla style 06 worn too. Do XF pieces hide exactly when vanilla ones do?
2. **Custom-colour metal and emission.** The M3 stud and its emissive twin in the creator, gameplay at night and photo mode.
3. **Gems.** The opaque faceted gem against the glass gem under creator lights and a photo-mode light moved around the face: which sparkles, which reads dull?
4. **Physics earrings (no build).** H5 of the [dangle page](../../knowledge/hair-physics.md#in-game-test-asks): an installed physics earring worn, head turned and walking. Does it swing although its mesh binds to the body skeleton?
5. **A creator dangle** (needs P6's first build; not this session): does a dangle component in a creator `face` option swing in the creator, gameplay and photo mode, and does the drop stay attached under the strongest ear shapes?

## Questions for the maintainer

Each has a proposed default; the Studio proceeds with it unless told otherwise.

1. **Go-ahead and order.** Run the probe session (P0, rebuilt with the names and three extra pieces) before any authoring code, and start the models (P1) alongside? *Default: yes.*
2. **Which area rows.** *Default: four rows, "XF Ears", "XF Nose", "XF Lips" and "XF Brows & Face" (bridge, cheeks, brows), each only present when the collection has a look for it.*
3. **Both ears in one look.** A look in "XF Ears" covers both ears, as vanilla styles do, rather than separate left and right rows. *Default: both ears in one look.*
4. **First-scope parts.** Studs (ball, disc, faceted stone), seam and captive-bead hoops, circular barbells, septum clickers, straight and curved bars. Anything to add or drop? *Default: as listed.*
5. **Finishes.** The vanilla metals by name, plus any-colour metals and enamel through `metal_base`; patterned finishes later. *Default: yes.*
6. **Gems.** Opaque faceted stones first; glass and a real gem shader later. *Default: yes.*
7. **Glowing pieces.** Emissive (LED, neon) jewellery through `metal_base` emission: in the first release if P0 shows it works, or later? *Default: in the first release if it works, labelled as a finish like any other.*
8. **Vanilla pieces as parts.** Let a look include any vanilla piece (fixed position, 16 vanilla finishes, mixed metals allowed), by reference and at no size cost? *Default: yes, as one category of the part palette.*
9. **Male V.** Female and male in the first release, now that the masculine head draws in the preview. *Default: both.*
10. **Imported meshes.** glTF import of one's own models: first release or later? *Default: later (P7), after the kit.*
11. **Dangles.** Static first stays decided; when they come, physics through the CCXL-hair pattern rather than baked drapes only. *Default: yes.*
12. **Names.** Mod names are decided (split "XF Piercings", merged "XF Looks"); creator row labels as in question 2. *Default: as proposed.*
