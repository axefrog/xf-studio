# XF Studio changelog

What changed in each XF Studio desktop release, newest first, written for the people using it. Each version has exactly two parts:

- **New and improved:** features and changes you can see, in plain language.
- **Fixes and under the hood:** bug fixes, performance, reliability and maintenance.

When a change lands, add a line to **Unreleased**. When a version is tagged, rename **Unreleased** to that version (for example `## 0.1.0-alpha.2`) and start a new, empty **Unreleased** above it. The release workflow copies the tagged version's section into the GitHub release and refuses to create the release if that section is missing or either part is empty. Say what was checked and how; never describe something as tested in the game until it has been.

## Unreleased

### New and improved

- **Your V's own piercings, and the creator's graphic eye designs, in the 3D preview.** The head now shows the piercings and earrings your V wears, read from the same game and mod setup your Build uses, in their real metals and plastics: silver and gold look brushed, black plastic keeps its narrow shine. Piercing mods that work by replacing a creator style, such as a modular jewellery framework, now show exactly what that style shows in game, all parts changing colour together, with no separate "PRC" entry any more. In the **Character** panel, **Try a piercing style** lists every style and colour the creator offers on your setup; picking one shows it on your V in the preview only, and **Your V's own** goes back. The creator's graphic eye designs (hearts, rings, skulls and the rest) are drawn now instead of the default eye. Checked in a browser test workspace with the default V, two saves and a tried style, under both lighting choices; not yet compared with the game.
- **A look made with a newer XF Studio no longer locks your whole workspace.** If a collection has a look from a newer version of XF Studio, this version keeps that look exactly as it is and says so in **Layers**, with a button to get the latest version. Your other looks stay editable and save as usual. That one look can't be changed, saved to the library or put in a mod here until you update; Export collection keeps it as it is. Checked by automated tests that write such looks back and compare them byte for byte, and in a browser test workspace.
- **Your makeup's shine in the preview now follows your V's skin.** The 3D preview now lights the makeup you draw the way the game lights your exported XF Eye Artistry look: all your layers together, as one surface, with the same light as your V's skin. Before, each layer was lit on its own, as ordinary plastic. Metallic now shows a bright, pale reflection, and Glossy and Satin shine about as much as the game makes them shine. Where layers overlap or fade out, you see one shine in between rather than two, and Metallic makeup only loses the skin's soft glow where there is enough of it, as in the game. Glitter and the earlier preview models still look as before. Checked by tests that compare the result with the game's maths on a real graphics card, and in a browser test workspace with the finish test boards under both lighting choices, a moving light and both themes; not yet compared with the game.
- **Soft and see-through makeup looks as dark in the preview as it will in game.** The 3D preview now mixes the makeup you draw with the skin, and with your other layers, the way the game mixes your exported XF Eye Artistry look. Soft edges, faded ends and layers below full opacity used to look lighter than the game will show them, and now look a little darker and richer; fully opaque makeup and bare skin look as before. Glitter still mixes as before, because the game can't draw it yet. Checked by a test that measures the colours on a real graphics card (a black layer at 25, 50 and 75 % opacity leaves 56, 25 and 6 % of the skin's brightness, as the game's own maths predicts) and in a browser test workspace under both lighting choices and in both themes; not yet compared with the game.
- **Your V's makeup looks the same under both lighting choices.** Under **Studio** lighting the 3D preview now mixes your V's own eye makeup, lipstick, blush and freckles, the brows, and the wet layer over the eyes the way the game mixes them, as **Character creator** lighting already did. Blush, lipstick and the darker liners used to look too strong under Studio lighting and now look softer. The eye corners are a little less dark, and the edges of fine hair and lashes are a little lighter. Skin and everything else solid looks exactly as before. Checked in a browser test workspace with the default V and two saves, under both lighting choices and in both themes, and by a test that measures the colours on a real graphics card; not yet compared with the game.
- **Guided tours and a Help panel.** A short *Getting started* tour walks through layers, drawing on the UV map, the head view, colour and finish, presets and the library, and making your mod. It lights up each part in turn, dimming the rest in the dark theme and lightening it in the light theme. It can also do a step for you, such as adding a layer, using the same actions as the rest of the app, so Undo works as usual. The tour is offered once after the welcome screen, and **Not now** is remembered. A *What's new* tour covers the History panel, your V's own brows, lashes and hair, and character-creator lighting. Press **F1**, or use the new **?** button in the header, to open **Help**: search the tours, answers to common questions and every keyboard and mouse shortcut, and open the knowledge pages or report a problem. The tours work from the keyboard too: Esc stops, and the arrow keys and Enter move between steps. Checked in a browser test workspace in both themes and in a narrow window; not yet tried in the desktop app.

- **Finds Cyberpunk 2077 whichever PC store you use.** XF Studio looks for the game in every Steam library, GOG, the Epic Games Store and Mod Organizer 2, and skips an Epic copy that hasn't finished installing. If it finds only an Xbox app copy, it says plainly that XF Eye Artistry needs the PC edition sold on Steam, GOG or Epic Games, and how to get going. Checked on one PC with the GOG version; Steam, Epic and Xbox app setups are checked by automated tests only.
- **Character creator lighting in the 3D preview.** Camera & light has a new **Lighting** choice: **Character creator** shows your V under the game's own character-creator and mirror lights, on black, through the colour grade your game uses, including one from an installed LUT mod. **Creator face** and **Creator hair** frame the head as those creator pages do. Switch back to **Studio** at any time; nothing else changes. It exists so the preview can be compared with the game's own screen. Light strengths and overall brightness still need matching against a screenshot, and shadows aren't simulated yet. Checked in the app on one PC; not yet compared with the game.
- **Your V's own makeup and face details in the 3D preview.** The head now shows the game's own eye makeup, lipstick, blush, freckles, blemishes, scars, face tattoos, face cyberware and the personal-link port your V wears, read from the same game and mod setup your Build uses, next to the XF makeup you are drawing. Makeup from installed CCXL mods shows the same way. Colours blend the way the game blends them, and lipstick or blush that doesn't change the shine keeps the skin's own. Your own XF makeup stays drawn on top. The **Character** panel lists what is shown, and says so if part of a face detail uses a material the preview can't draw yet. Checked in a browser test workspace with the default V, two saves and a test V with eye makeup, a scar, a tattoo and face cyberware, under both lighting choices and in both themes; not yet compared with the game.
- **Your V's own eyes in the 3D preview.** The head now shows your V's eye colour, read from the same game and mod setup your Build uses. That includes every one of the creator's gradient and texture eyes and eye colours from installed CCXL eye mods, even ones packed in older archives. Gradient irises take their colour from the game's own colour ramps. Eyes are no longer drawn upside down compared with the game. A thin wet layer darkens the corners of the eye and adds the shine along the lower lid. The creator's graphic (layered) eye designs aren't drawn yet: the default eye shows in their place, and the app says so. The eye's depth under the cornea and the game's eye lighting come later. Checked in a browser test workspace with the default V and two saves, under both lighting choices and in both themes; not yet compared with the game.
- **Your V's own skin in the 3D preview.** The head now shows your V's skin type and skin tone, read from the same game and mod setup your Build uses, including installed complexion and skin-texture mods. Without a save it shows the character creator's default V; loading another save changes the skin with the rest of the character. Tone, skin detail and shine follow the game's own skin material; lighting under the skin is approximated, and it hasn't been compared with the game yet.
- **Exported makeup keeps much finer detail.** Build now spends the whole texture on the eye-makeup area instead of the whole head, so edges, fine lines and Shimmer facets come out about four times sharper in your XF Eye Artistry mod, with no extra memory. Colour-shifting keeps its previous detail for now. Checked by Build's own tests and on three test collections; not yet seen in game.

### Fixes and under the hood

- **Restore the creator lighting calibration in one click.** Under **Lighting → Advanced: creator lighting calibration**, **Restore defaults** now puts the intensity reading, the cone angles and the creator exposure all back to their defaults. The old **Default exposure** button reset only the exposure, so after changing the other two there was no way back. The button is greyed out, with the reason, when everything is already at its default. Checked by automated tests.
- Your V's details are prepared once more after this update (the preview's record now also carries piercings and layered materials); the private piercing files earlier development builds read are no longer used.
- **Undo keeps up in big collections, and keeps more when storage runs short.** Each preset's Undo history now stores only what each step changed, so switching presets, jumping through History and autosave stay quick in large collections (switching between presets with long histories is about three times faster). When browser storage runs short, Undo history is first stored in a more compact form that keeps every step (a preset with 32 detailed layers now keeps all 80 steps where it used to keep 4); only if that still doesn't fit does the current preset fall back to at least its last 10 steps, other presets' steps and recovery copies being given up first, and the app says that older steps were not kept. Undo, Redo and History work exactly as before. Checked by automated tests against the previous version (same steps, same stored drafts) and in a browser test workspace.
- **Your V's details download and open faster.** The preview now fetches only the parts of each face detail, eye and brow mesh it actually shows, and reads a mesh shared by two details once. For a V with a scar, a tattoo and face cyberware that is 73 MB instead of 151 MB, which also leaves room for more details. An eye missing an extra texture the preview doesn't use yet is shown instead of left out, and older mods packed without file names are read faster and without pointless retries. Checked by automated tests and in a browser test workspace with the default V and two saves.
- **Recipes and collections open in older versions more often.** Export recipe, saved collections and exported collection files now use the oldest recipe format that holds your look. Removing the last game-matched Glossy, Shimmer or Colour-shift layer no longer keeps a look on the newest format, so XF Studio 0.1.0-alpha.1 can open it again. Nothing about how a look appears or builds changes. Checked by automated tests against the previous version, a Build of a four-look collection whose files came out identical, and a browser test workspace.
- **Your draft is stored in a new format, ready for more features than eye makeup.** Each preset now keeps its eye makeup as one part of a look, so later features (lips, brows, hair) can join the same preset. Your draft, Undo history and remembered Glitter and Colour-shift settings carry over unchanged the first time this version opens them. Saved library versions and exported collection files of eye makeup keep the format 0.1.0-alpha.1 reads, so you can still open them there. The draft itself can't go back: 0.1.0-alpha.1 won't open a draft saved by this version, and leaves it untouched instead of replacing it. The desktop app keeps your previous draft beside it as `workspace.v1.bak`; rename it to `workspace.json` to use it in 0.1.0-alpha.1. Checked with automated tests on copies of older drafts and libraries, a Build whose mod files came out byte-identical to before, and by running 0.1.0-alpha.1's own readers on the new files. Menus and buttons that check a preset also answer much faster in large collections.
- **Export collection and Export compiler plan work for every collection.** A collection with parts XF Studio 0.1.0-alpha.1 can't read isn't saved to your library, because that version opens the same library, and exporting one used to fail with a message telling you to export it. It is now exported straight from your draft, and the message says plainly that it wasn't saved to the library. Collections the library takes are still saved first. Checked by automated tests with a real library.
- **Undo names the right change, and Esc never removes an older step.** After a drag that ended where it started, the next change is undone under its own name instead of the drag's, and pressing Esc during a drag that began there no longer takes that earlier step away. Checked by automated tests.
- **A developer tool to calibrate creator lighting against a screenshot.** It measures the same patches in a creator screenshot and a matching preview render, fits the one brightness setting, and reports the pass marks. It only reads the images.
- **Build checks where every texture lands on the eye-makeup area** before it accepts a mod, by following the game's own texture mapping from the eye plate's shape to the finished texture.
- **Your V's details and the creator colour grade no longer trip over each other.** Preparing both at once could lose files or wrongly remember a resource as unreadable until the mod changed; they now take turns, and a resource is marked unreadable only when WolvenKit really couldn't read it. Old marks left by this problem are cleared automatically. The colour grade now gives up on a stuck WolvenKit after a time limit, says plainly when .NET is missing, tries again after a failure, and follows a changed mod setup or a newly ready WolvenKit without restarting the app. Checked by automated tests and a run on one PC's mod setup.
- **Check says when it can't yet tell whether a look reaches the eye area.** Before your first Build, or after you change launch route, MO2 profile, installed mods, the head choice or the game version, Check now says "Whether each look reaches the eye area is checked when you Build." instead of quietly counting every look as ready. If the eye plate XF Studio saved turns out to be out of date during a Build, it prepares a fresh one and carries on instead of asking you to build again. Checked by automated tests and real Builds of two test collections.
- **The colour grade and your V's details cope better with WolvenKit trouble.** The colour grade no longer retries a failing WolvenKit every 40 seconds forever, pausing the app for about two seconds each time: it retries less and less often, then waits until your game, mods or WolvenKit change. Updating WolvenKit now retries resources an older version couldn't read, and an interrupted first scan of a mod archive can no longer hide that mod's files until the archive changes. Checked by automated tests.
- **Build's texture-placement check is stricter for very faint makeup**, and no longer estimates a shift from too little content to be meaningful. Checked by automated tests and the same two real Builds.
- **Game detection only looks at drives that are there.** It checks fixed and removable drives that are connected now and never touches network drives, so a disconnected mapped drive can't slow it down. When no game folder is found, the setup card now explains an unfinished Epic Games install, or a launcher record XF Studio couldn't read, instead of saying nothing.

## 0.1.0-alpha.1

### New and improved

- **The first test version of XF Studio for Windows.** XF Studio is a desktop app for customising Cyberpunk 2077, starting with your own V. Eye makeup is the first supported feature. It installs for your Windows user, with its own window, icon and About page.
- **Design eye makeup in layers on the flat UV map.** Build complete looks from stacked layers (up to 32), each with its own colour, opacity, strength and finish. Draw each layer as a smooth or cornered curve, soften its edge (differently at different points if you like), bend it with warp controls, and move, rotate or scale the whole shape. Every change can be undone.
- **The UV map uses the whole panel.** The map fills its panel at any size, docked or floating. Fit shape fills the panel with a small margin, a single eye fills it on its own, the wheel zooms around the pointer, and right-drag or Ctrl-drag pans. Your zoom and pan are kept when you reopen the app.
- **A 3D head preview built from your own game.** Once XF Studio knows your Cyberpunk 2077 folder and has WolvenKit, it prepares the 3D preview from your own game files the first time you open it: the head with its facial shapes, the eye-makeup area and the eyes, with the game's default skin and eye textures. On the test PC this took 11 to 16 seconds. It shows its progress, can be cancelled, changes nothing in your game, and the head appears without restarting. The eyes use the game's plain eye texture rather than your character's eye colour. Checked on one PC with game version 2.31.
- **Your V's brows, lashes and hair in the 3D preview.** Load a save and the preview shows that V's own eyebrows, eyelashes and hair, including styles and colours from installed character-creator mods and mods that recolour hair. XF Studio reads them from the same game and mod setup your Build uses, the way the game picks them, and changes nothing. Without a save it shows the character creator's default V. Loading another save replaces the whole character (face, eyes, brows, lashes, hair and piercings); your looks and library are never touched. The first time takes a couple of minutes while WolvenKit reads the character-creator files, then seconds; the head is usable meanwhile, with a small note while they prepare. If something can't be shown, one line says so. Colours and lighting are close to the game but not yet checked against it, and hair doesn't move.
- **Eyes follow the eye shape.** Changing the eye shape moves the eyeballs with the eyelids, as the game does. Eye shapes are numbered as in the character creator.
- **Keep your looks in a library.** Presets can be added, copied, renamed, reordered and removed. Your collection is saved in a local library with version history, your current draft comes back when you reopen the app, and looks can be exported and imported as files to back up or share.
- **A History panel lists your recent changes.** Click any step to go back to it, or a step you undid to go forward again. Steps you undid stay listed, dimmed, until you make a new change. Each preset keeps its own history, and the panel says when older steps were not kept. The Undo and Redo buttons (next to Save) say which change they would undo or redo, such as “Redo: Move point”, and show their shortcuts: Ctrl+Z, and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y.
- **Check which looks can become mod files.** Check lists every preset and layer that can be built into **XF Eye Artistry**, the eye-makeup mod XF Studio makes for you from your own looks, and names anything that would be left out, and why. It needs no game files.
- **Build your own XF Eye Artistry.** Build turns your looks into mod files with one selector, labelled **XF**, that switches between your looks in the character creator. XF Eye Artistry has been seen in game: its looks show in the character creator, in gameplay and in photo mode. Nothing is installed into your game or mod manager for you.
- **The eye-makeup area follows your own head.** The eye-makeup area is cut from the head your game actually loads, including a head changed by an installed mod. If a head mod changes the head in a way XF Eye Artistry doesn't support yet, Build stops and names the mod; to build anyway, set **Head used for the eye plate** to **The unmodified game head** under Game & tools. If XF Studio can't read one of your mod folders or archives that could hide a head mod, Build stops and says what to fix instead of guessing.
- **Seven finishes to choose from.** Matte, Satin and Metallic can be built into your mod. Glossy, Shimmer and Colour-shifting can be built as experiments, and each preview follows how the game can actually draw it: Glossy is one sharp reflection (the game has no separate clear coat), Shimmer uses fine reflective facets that sparkle close up and soften into a sheen further away, and Colour-shifting turns toward a shift colour you choose, with its own strength, as the lid curves away from view. A Colour-shifting preset can only contain that one colour-shift pigment for now; a Glitter layer beside it is left out on its own and the colour shift is still built. Glitter is preview only: Check and Build leave those layers out and tell you which. The finish picker groups finishes by what they can do today (Exports, Experimental, Preview only); other names such as foil, pearl, wet look and duochrome are in the tooltips and descriptions.
- **No WolvenKit setup needed.** If you don't have WolvenKit, XF Studio offers to download it for you the first time it needs it. Before anything is downloaded it tells you what WolvenKit is, why it's needed, how big it is (45 MB), where it comes from (WolvenKit's official release on GitHub) and its licence (GPL-3.0), and you choose. The download shows its progress, can be cancelled, is retried if the connection drops, and is checked against the official release before it's used. It goes into XF Studio's own folder; nothing is installed in Windows or your game. Once it's there, the 3D preview and **Build** set themselves up. Checked end to end on one PC with game version 2.31: from a fresh start to the 3D head in about a minute, and a Build with the downloaded WolvenKit passed its checks.
- **Help getting Microsoft .NET.** WolvenKit needs Microsoft's free .NET 10 Runtime. If your PC doesn't have it, XF Studio says so plainly and offers one button that gets Microsoft's own installer, then picks it up when you come back.
- **Use your own WolvenKit if you prefer.** If you already use WolvenKit CLI 8.17.4 or 9.0.1, choose it under **About → Build setup**; it always takes priority. The field is optional: leave it empty and XF Studio can download WolvenKit for you.
- **The 3D preview setup is always one click away.** If you choose **Not now**, the head panel keeps saying what the preview needs and offers **Set up 3D preview** to bring the setup back. While the preview is being prepared or WolvenKit is downloading, the head panel shows the progress in neutral colours; red is kept for real problems.
- **Licences in About.** About → Licences shows XF Studio's MIT licence and the notices for the software it includes.

### Fixes and under the hood

- **Known limitation: the 3D preview shows the head, the eye-makeup area, the eyes, and your V's brows, lashes and hair.** The skin always uses the game's default complexion and tone, the eyes the game's plain eye texture, and piercings and the character-creator idle aren't in this version.
- **Known limitation: the in-game look is still being tuned.** XF Eye Artistry shows in the character creator, in gameplay and in photo mode, but how it looks close up, and how each finish looks in game, are still being tuned. Glossy, Shimmer and Colour-shifting in particular may not look the same in game as in the preview.
- **Known limitation: the installer is not code-signed,** so Windows SmartScreen may warn before it runs. Checksums and a build-provenance attestation are published with each release so you can check the file came from this project's automated build.
- **Known limitation: automatic updates are off.** Download new versions from the releases page. Installing one version over another hasn't been tested yet, so export your looks as a backup first. Uninstalling with the default **App** option keeps your library and settings.
- **Right-drag always pans the 3D head view.** Holding Ctrl or Shift while right-dragging turned the head instead of panning it, although the hints said right-drag pans. The head view now does exactly what its hints say for every mouse button, modifier key and touch gesture. Checked with automated tests that drive the real camera controls, and in the app.
- Nothing from the game or other mods is included in the download. Every build is checked automatically so that only the app's own files, its licence and the third-party notices are packaged, and that none of them contains a personal folder path or an email address.
- **Autosave you can rely on.** Your draft is saved only after you change something, keeps full Undo for the preset you are working on and the last few steps for the others, and a damaged backup entry doesn't stop the rest of your draft from coming back. If storage runs short, the status bar says so. Closing the window waits for your latest draft to be saved, and shows a message instead of closing if saving fails.
- XF Studio needs the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime, which most Windows PCs already have. If it's missing, XF Studio offers to install it with one click using Microsoft's own installer, and then opens; if its copy of that installer is missing, it points you to Microsoft's page. Any other startup failure shows **Try again** and **Copy diagnostics** instead of an empty window.
- If the 3D head can't be shown, XF Studio says why in plain words and offers the next step: update your graphics driver (or open XF Studio on the computer itself rather than over Remote Desktop) when the window can't draw 3D, **Prepare again** when the prepared preview files are damaged, and **Try again** otherwise. Trying again doesn't need a restart.
- If the 3D preview setup or the WolvenKit download briefly loses contact with XF Studio's background service, it keeps trying, says so, and carries on when the service answers again.
- If WolvenKit only partly exports the game files, the incomplete result isn't kept, so trying again really retries. The preview is prepared again when WolvenKit changes, and XF Studio says it can't find the head only when the head really is missing from your game files. Problems saving the preview, such as a full disk, are explained in plain words.
- Whether the 3D preview may start preparing by itself is remembered between launches. The setup card takes the keyboard focus only when you ask to see it.
- The WolvenKit download dialog starts on its heading, so an accidental Enter downloads nothing; Esc is the same as **Not now**.
- **Build setup** (in About) and **Game & tools** (in the Mod package panel) share one copy of your settings, so a game folder or WolvenKit path saved in either, or with **Use this folder** on the 3D preview card, shows up in both straight away.
- Build reuses the eye plate it prepared earlier when your game and mods haven't changed, so a repeat Build starts in about 2 seconds on the test PC.
- Build's independent check unpacks and converts the finished mod files itself, reads the ArchiveXL file line by line, and works out each look's finish method from its layers by itself; the package record lists the method for each look.
- A failed Check or Build shows when it failed, not when you looked at it.
- Switching a layer away from Colour-shifting and back brings its shift colour and strength back, and leaving Glitter keeps the last Glitter model's settings for when you choose it again. The Colour & finish panel says **Left out of this preset** for a Colour-shifting layer that sits beside other finishes, matching what Check reports.
- Cancelling a slider or drag with Esc keeps the changes you could still redo.
- The mouse and keyboard hints float over the head and UV map and stay at most two lines high, so changing hints never resize the view.
- Right-click menus show only what you can do with the thing under the pointer. Right-clicking any layer's makeup, on the head or the UV map, offers to select that layer; right-clicking bare skin or the background shows just the view options.
